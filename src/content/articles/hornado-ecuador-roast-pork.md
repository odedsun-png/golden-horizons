## Quick Taste

Hornado is a whole pig roasted in an oven until the meat is tender and the skin is crackling. At a market stall, a cook carves a plate of pork and adds a starch such as llapingachos (potato patties) or mote (hominy-like corn), plus a salad and a sauce. Published prices run from about $2 at a Cuenca market in May 2024 to $4 to $5 at a Quito market in 2023. Prices may have changed, so ask before you order.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Hornado comes from the Andean highlands and is eaten across much of Ecuador. The name comes from the Spanish word for oven, horno. Cooks roast the pig in an oven, often wood-fired, and many baste or inject it with chicha de jora, beer or fruit juice before roasting. Vendors keep their marinades secret, so the flavor differs from stall to stall. We found conflicting accounts of how the dish reached Ecuador, so we do not give a date.

## How Locals Eat It

Locals order a plate at a market stall, often at breakfast or lunch. Crispy skin (cuero, cuerito or cascaritas) is prized, and some stalls sell it separately. Sides vary by town. Potato patties or mote are common, and so are toasted corn, avocado and ripe plantain. Many places serve agrio, a sauce of onion, tomato, lemon, chicha de jora and panela that tastes sweet more than sour. Some stalls do not serve it, and some add a hot ají sauce.

## Where to Find It

In Quito, a 2023 Primicias article describes Hornados Doña Lolita at Mercado La Carolina, open 7:00 to 16:00, with plates at $4 to $5. It also lists Hornado Pastuso El Pupo, which serves a Carchi-style plate with pork broth for $5.90 or $6.90. In Cuenca, a May 2024 El Mercurio report names the 10 de Agosto, 12 de Abril and 27 de Febrero markets, with plates "from two dollars", served with mote, llapingachos, salad and ají. We could not confirm current hours or prices. Call or ask a host before you go.

## Regional Variations

Tulcán serves hornado with broth and roast potatoes. Sangolquí adds avocado. Riobamba cooks the pork over coals in a wooden box lined with eucalyptus leaves, according to a travel blog. The seasoning changes by region, but salt is always used.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

Use the published ranges as a guide only: from $2 in Cuenca (May 2024), $4 to $5 at a Quito market (2023), and $5.90 to $6.90 at a Quito sit-down restaurant (2023). Ask the size of the plate, how many potato patties come with it, and whether cuero (crispy skin) is extra. If you eat less meat, ask for a small portion.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Vendors cook daily and popular stalls sell out, so go early. The dish is heavy on pork and starch. If you have a heart or blood-pressure condition, ask your doctor about fatty pork and salty sides before you make it a habit.

## Before You Plan a Stay

The U.S. State Department rates Ecuador Level 2: Exercise Increased Caution, in an advisory dated August 17, 2026, citing crime, terrorism, unrest and kidnapping. The areas it lists as off-limits to U.S. government employees include parts of Guayaquil, Huaquillas, Arenillas, Quevedo, Duran and Esmeraldas. We did not find Quito or Cuenca on the list we read, but read the full advisory on travel.state.gov before you plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is hornado made of?

Hornado is a whole pig roasted in an oven, served with sides such as potato patties, mote, salad and sauce.

### Is hornado spicy?

Not by itself. Agrio is sweet, and ají is a separate hot sauce that some stalls serve on the side.

### Where does hornado come from?

It comes from the Andean highlands and is eaten across much of Ecuador, and the name comes from the Spanish word for oven.

### How is hornado different from other roast pork dishes?

Cooks often baste it with chicha de jora, beer or fruit juice, and serve it with Andean sides such as mote and potato patties.
