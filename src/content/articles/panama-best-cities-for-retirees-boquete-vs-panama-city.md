# Panama - Best Cities for Retirees: Boquete vs Panama City — A Local's Perspective

Panama is home to over 25,000 American retirees, and it’s not hard to see why. With its favorable tax laws, use of the U.S. dollar, and a range of climates and landscapes, Panama has something to offer for those looking to retire abroad. But the decision between the busy life of Panama City and the serene atmosphere of Boquete often poses a challenge. This article will delve into what life is really like in these two popular destinations, helping you make an informed choice.

## Costs and Lifestyle in Boquete

In Boquete, a monthly budget of $2,000 can cover essentials comfortably. You can rent a two-bedroom apartment in a neighborhood like Jaramillo for around $800 to $1,000 per month. Eating out is affordable, with a meal at a local restaurant costing about $5 to $10. The Tuesday Market at BCP (Boquete Community Players) is a center where you can find fresh produce and local crafts, supporting a lifestyle that’s both economical and community-focused.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/18183895/pexels-photo-18183895.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Boquete offers a slower pace of life that many retirees find appealing. The town is located in the highlands, with temperatures averaging between 60°F to 80°F year-round. This makes it a perfect escape from the scorching heat many retirees are trying to leave behind. The sense of community here is strong, with events like the annual Boquete Jazz & Blues Festival and regular meet-ups at local spots like The Garden Restaurant & Cafe.

However, Boquete is not without its limitations. While the cost of living is lower compared to Panama City, the trade-off comes in the form of limited healthcare facilities. The nearest full-service hospital is in David, about a 30-minute drive away, which can be a significant factor for those needing regular medical care. Additionally, while English is widely spoken due to the large expat community, learning basic Spanish will go a long way in day-to-day interactions.

## The Urban Experience in Panama City

Living in Panama City requires a bigger budget. A comfortable lifestyle here can cost between $2,500 to $3,500 per month. Rent for a two-bedroom apartment in areas like El Cangrejo or San Francisco typically ranges from $1,200 to $2,000. Eating out is a bit pricier than in Boquete, with meals at mid-range restaurants costing between $15 to $30.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/36811337/pexels-photo-36811337.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Panama City offers a completely different experience with its urban amenities. The Multiplaza Pacific Mall provides high-end shopping, while the Mercado de Mariscos offers a taste of local seafood. The city's skyline boasts modern skyscrapers, and areas such as Casco Viejo offer a glimpse into Panama’s colonial past with cobblestone streets and historical landmarks.

Healthcare in Panama City is one of its strongest advantages. Facilities like Hospital Punta Pacifica, affiliated with Johns Hopkins Medicine, provide excellent healthcare services. This makes the city a practical choice for retirees who prioritize easy access to medical care.

However, the pace of city life may not be for everyone. Traffic congestion is a common issue, particularly during rush hours. Although public transport options include the Metro and buses, many find getting around the city easier with a car. While Spanish is the official language, English is widely spoken, especially in business and tourist areas, easing the transition for those who aren’t fluent in Spanish.

## Climate and Outdoor Activities

Boquete’s climate is one of its main attractions, often referred to as "eternal spring." The cooler temperatures and lush landscapes provide a perfect setting for outdoor activities. Hiking enthusiasts will enjoy trails like the Quetzal Trail, which offers impressive views and a chance to spot the elusive Resplendent Quetzal bird. The Caldera River provides opportunities for more leisurely activities like bird watching and fishing.

Panama City, by contrast, experiences a tropical climate with higher humidity levels. Temperatures here range from 75°F to 90°F, which can be challenging for those unaccustomed to the heat. However, the city’s location on the Pacific Ocean allows easy access to beaches and water activities. The nearby Amador Causeway offers a scenic route for biking or walking, with views of the Panama Canal and the entrance to the Pacific Ocean.

One challenge in Panama City is dealing with the rainy season, which lasts from May to November. Heavy rains can lead to flooding in some areas, affecting daily life. In contrast, Boquete’s rainfall is more predictable and often occurs in the afternoon, leaving the mornings clear for outdoor pursuits.
