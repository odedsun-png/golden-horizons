A one-bedroom runs about $770 outside the center or $899 in the center, and a casual lunch is under $8. The catch: Medicare generally does not cover care abroad, and the long-stay residency bar is about $1,226 a month in pension income. Healthcare and paperwork, not rent, decide whether this works.

A cappuccino for about $3.60 and a private one-bedroom for less than $900 a month: those numbers make San Salvador worth a closer look. The real question is whether a $2,000 income supports an ordinary life, with a kitchen, a ride to the clinic, and a cushion for what rental listings leave out.

For a careful renter, the answer can be yes. But the apartment, your routines, and your healthcare plan decide whether the cushion feels reassuring or thin.

## What $2,000 Has to Cover in San Salvador

Housing sets the mood for everything else. Numbeo-based figures list a city-center one-bedroom at $898.99 and an outside-center one-bedroom at $770, with the center range running roughly $704-$1,140. Treat both as small crowd-sourced samples, not a listing guarantee.

Use the higher figure for Escalón, Zona Rosa, and Centro Histórico, and the lower one farther out, including Santa Elena. Ask what comes with the rent: cooling, water, garbage, internet equipment, parking, and building fees.

Food is the next lever. Expatistan's San Salvador page (June 2026) lists whole milk near $1.86 per liter and 12 large eggs near $2.45; Numbeo lists a loaf of bread near $2.59 and an inexpensive restaurant meal near $7.00. Those are the numbers behind a week of breakfasts and lunches.

Utilities deserve the same attention as the rent. Published estimates vary widely, from about $66 for one person to about $130 for a larger apartment, plus roughly $43 for internet. Before choosing a building, ask to see three recent bills and confirm how cooling is handled.

Transportation fares were not confirmed today, so this guide does not quote a bus price. Ask at a transit office for current fares, and price fuel and parking before you consider a car.

**How your money compares to the US:** A one-bedroom in San Salvador sits well below a typical U.S. metro range of $1,500-$2,400. A casual lunch at this price is below the roughly $10-$20 often spent on a simple U.S. lunch. Housing is the clearest place the monthly difference shows up.

## Healthcare, Long Stays, and the Everyday Questions That Matter

San Salvador's daily prices can make the move feel possible. Healthcare, residency, and safety are where you slow down.

For healthcare, call or visit a private clinic in central San Salvador and ask for self-pay prices for a general appointment, lab work, imaging, prescriptions, and urgent care. Medicare generally does not cover care outside the United States, so your plans for care abroad, and for any return to the U.S., belong in the budget from the start.

For residency, a 2026 report says the pensionado route requires a monthly, permanent pension of at least three commerce-and-services minimum wages, about $1,226. The rentista route (other passive income) requires four, about $1,635. Neither permits paid work. Confirm the amounts with El Salvador's immigration authority (DGME) or a consulate before placing a deposit, because the thresholds move with the minimum wage.

The State Department's Level 1 advisory is reassuring, but it does not replace neighborhood judgment. Walk the blocks around a prospective apartment at different times of day, map the route to groceries and the clinic you would use, and enroll in STEP before a longer visit.

The rent may fit. The bigger question is whether your ordinary errands feel easy enough to make the city feel like home.
