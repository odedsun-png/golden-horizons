# What $2,000 a Month Gets You in San Salvador, El Salvador
**Golden Horizons Status:** 🟡 WORTH TESTING — VERIFY COSTS

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** A single retiree can build a San Salvador budget around $1,472-$1,899 a month, leaving a possible $101-$528 cushion from $2,000. A one-bedroom runs about $770 outside the center or $899 in the center. Groceries can fit around $350-$500, while utilities and internet land near $172-$210. A bus ride costs $0.35, and a casual lunch is about $6.75.

A morning coffee for $3.55, a bus ride for $0.35, and a private one-bedroom for less than $900 a month: those are the numbers that make San Salvador worth a closer look. The question is not whether you can find a bargain. It is whether a $2,000 monthly income can support the kind of ordinary life you want—comfortable enough to cook, get around, meet a friend for lunch, and keep a cushion for the things that do not show up on a rental listing.

For a careful renter, the answer can be yes. But the apartment you choose, the routines you build, and the healthcare arrangements you make will decide whether that monthly cushion feels reassuring or thin.

## What $2,000 Has to Cover in San Salvador

The housing choice sets the mood for everything else. Numbeo’s October 2026 reference for a one-bedroom is $898.99 in the city center and $770 outside it. That difference can become groceries, a phone plan, transit, or simply a little breathing room.

Use the $898.99 city-center benchmark when comparing one-bedroom listings in Escalón, Zona Rosa, and Centro Histórico. Use the $770 outside-center benchmark when you look farther out, including Santa Elena. Ask what comes with the rent: cooling, water, garbage, internet equipment, parking, and building fees can all change what looks like a good monthly deal.

Then comes the quiet work of daily life. Milk is about $1.84 per liter, bread $2.59 a loaf, eggs $3.02 a dozen, tomatoes $1.56 per pound, and bananas $0.63 per pound. Those are not glamorous numbers, but they are the ones that shape a week of breakfasts, lunches, and the comfort of having a kitchen you actually use.

Utilities deserve the same attention as the rent. Basic utilities for a larger apartment average $129.92, internet averages $42.50, and a mobile plan averages $21.25. Before choosing a building, ask to see recent utility bills and confirm how cooling is handled. The apartment that feels like a deal in the afternoon can look different after a month of bills.

Getting around can be inexpensive if the routes work for your real life. A one-way local fare averages $0.35, and a monthly pass averages $37.50. Gasoline is about $1.26 per liter, so the cost of keeping a car can change the picture once fuel, parking, and maintenance enter the conversation.

**How your money compares to the US:** A one-bedroom in San Salvador is well below a typical U.S. metro range of $1,500-$2,400. A casual lunch at $6.75 is also below the roughly $10-$20 often spent on a simple U.S. lunch. Grocery spending can look closer to a modest U.S. food budget of $300-$500, which makes housing the clearest place where the monthly difference shows up.

## Healthcare, Long Stays, and the Everyday Questions That Matter

San Salvador’s daily prices can make the move feel possible. Healthcare, residency, and safety are where you slow down and make sure the plan works for *you*.

For healthcare, use a scouting stay to make the questions concrete. Call or visit a private clinic in central San Salvador and ask for self-pay prices for a general appointment, laboratory work, imaging, prescriptions, and urgent care. Medicare generally does not cover care outside the United States, so your plans for care abroad—and for any return to the U.S.—belong in the budget conversation from the start.

For residency, begin with the Salvadoran consulate serving your area. Ask which route fits a retiree or long-stay visitor, then confirm the current documents, fees, insurance expectations, and renewal timing before placing a deposit. Bring income records, passport information, and housing plans to any consultation.

The State Department’s Level 1 advisory is reassuring, but it does not replace neighborhood judgment. Walk the blocks around a prospective apartment at different times of day. Map the route to groceries, transit, and the clinic you would actually use. Enroll in STEP before a longer visit so travel updates come directly to you.

The rent may fit. The bigger question is whether your ordinary errands feel easy enough to make the city feel like home.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is $2,000 a month realistic for one retiree in San Salvador?

Yes. With rent around $770-$899, groceries around $350-$500, and limited dining out, the sample core budget runs from $1,472-$1,899 before separately priced healthcare needs. The lower end leaves more flexibility for flights, medical care, and apartment setup. ([numbeo.com](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/San-Salvador))

### What does a one-bedroom apartment cost in San Salvador?

The city-center reference is $898.99 per month, while the outside-center reference is $770. Use the higher city-center figure when you compare Escalón, Zona Rosa, and Centro Histórico, then compare it with outside-center listings near Santa Elena. Ask each landlord which utilities, parking, and building fees are included before you decide. ([numbeo.com](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/San-Salvador))

### How much should I expect to spend on food?

A home-focused grocery plan can run about $350-$500 per month for one person. Milk is about $1.84 per liter, bread is $2.59 a loaf, and eggs are $3.02 a dozen. Your total depends on how often you cook and how often you make restaurants part of the week. ([expatistan.com](https://www.expatistan.com/cost-of-living/san-salvador))

### Is San Salvador safe enough to scout for retirement?

The U.S. Department of State lists El Salvador at Level 1, “Exercise normal precautions,” as of June 25, 2026. During a scouting stay, walk the routes between housing, groceries, and transit at the times you would use them. Register with STEP for travel updates before an extended visit. ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/el-salvador.html))

### What does transportation cost in San Salvador?

A one-way local transport fare is about $0.35, and a monthly pass is about $37.50. Gasoline averages roughly $1.26 per liter, so a car can add more to your monthly plan than the purchase price alone. Try transit first on your likely grocery, medical, and social routes. ([numbeo.com](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/San-Salvador))
