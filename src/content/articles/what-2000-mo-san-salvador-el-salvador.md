# What $2,000 a Month Gets You in San Salvador, El Salvador
**Golden Horizons Status:** 🟡 WORTH TESTING — VERIFY COSTS

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** A single retiree can build a San Salvador budget around $1,472-$1,899 a month, leaving a possible $101-$528 cushion from $2,000. A one-bedroom runs about $770 outside the center or $899 in the center. Groceries can fit around $350-$500, while utilities and internet land near $172-$210. A bus ride costs about $0.30, and a casual lunch is about $6.75. A $2,000 monthly pension also clears El Salvador's retiree residency income requirement of roughly $1,226.

A morning coffee for $3.55, a bus ride for 30 cents, and a private one-bedroom for less than $900 a month: those are the numbers that make San Salvador worth a closer look. The question is not whether you can find a bargain. It is whether a $2,000 monthly income can support the kind of ordinary life you want—comfortable enough to cook, get around, meet a friend for lunch, and keep a cushion for the things that do not show up on a rental listing.

For a careful renter, the answer can be yes. And unlike many destinations, the income test for legal residency sits well below that $2,000. The apartment you choose, the routines you build, and the healthcare arrangements you make will decide whether the monthly cushion feels reassuring or thin.

## What $2,000 Has to Cover in San Salvador

The housing choice sets the mood for everything else. Numbeo’s October 2026 reference for a one-bedroom is $898.99 in the city center and $770 outside it. That difference can become groceries, a phone plan, transit, or simply a little breathing room.

Those averages hide a split market. In the newer towers of Escalón and San Benito, home of the Zona Rosa, one-bedroom listings in October 2026 cluster around $1,075-$1,200 a month for 50-60 square meters. Santa Elena, in neighboring Antiguo Cuscatlán, is also an upscale area. Those neighborhoods are worth visiting, but on a $2,000 income they belong on the "maybe later" list.

The $770-$899 range is realistic in established, non-luxury buildings around the city. Below that, furnished one-bedrooms near $650 and small mini-apartments around $450—sometimes with water, electricity, and internet included—do appear, but they take patience to find. Ask what comes with the rent: cooling, water, garbage, internet equipment, parking, and maintenance fees can all change what looks like a good monthly deal.

Then comes the quiet work of daily life. Milk is about $1.84 per liter, bread $2.59 a loaf, eggs $3.02 a dozen, tomatoes $1.56 per pound, and bananas $0.63 per pound. Those are not glamorous numbers, but they are the ones that shape a week of breakfasts, lunches, and the comfort of having a kitchen you actually use.

Utilities deserve the same attention as the rent. Basic utilities for a mid-size apartment of about 915 square feet average $129.92, internet averages $42.50, and a mobile plan averages $21.25. Before choosing a building, ask to see recent utility bills and confirm how cooling is handled. The apartment that feels like a deal in the afternoon can look different after a month of bills.

Getting around can be inexpensive if the routes work for your real life. A one-way local fare averages about $0.30, and a monthly pass averages about $15. Taxis are a different story, starting around $6 before distance is added, so a few rides a month can cost more than the bus pass. Gasoline is about $1.26 per liter, so the cost of keeping a car can change the picture once fuel, parking, and maintenance enter the conversation.

**How your money compares to the US:** A one-bedroom in San Salvador is well below a typical U.S. metro range of $1,500-$2,400. A casual lunch at $6.75 is also below the roughly $10-$20 often spent on a simple U.S. lunch. Grocery spending can look closer to a modest U.S. food budget of $300-$500, which makes housing the clearest place where the monthly difference shows up. El Salvador also uses the U.S. dollar, so your Social Security check does not lose value to exchange rates.

## Healthcare, Long Stays, and the Everyday Questions That Matter

San Salvador’s daily prices can make the move feel possible. Healthcare, residency, and safety are where you slow down and make sure the plan works for *you*.

For healthcare, use a scouting stay to make the questions concrete. Call or visit a private clinic in central San Salvador and ask for self-pay prices for a general appointment, laboratory work, imaging, prescriptions, and urgent care. Medicare generally does not cover care outside the United States, so your plans for care abroad—and for any return to the U.S.—belong in the budget conversation from the start.

For residency, the numbers work in your favor. El Salvador ties its retiree income requirement to the local minimum wage for commerce and services, which has been $408.80 a month since June 1, 2025. The pensionado route requires three times that amount—about $1,226.40 a month—in permanent, stable pension income from abroad. The rentista route, for other passive income, requires four times the wage, about $1,635.20. Older figures of $1,095 or $1,200 still circulate online; they are based on the previous wage and are out of date.

Two recent rules also matter. Since March 31, 2026, temporary residents need to spend only 90 days a year inside El Salvador, which makes it easier to keep ties at home. And before you apply, remember that visitors share one 90-day clock across El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua—so a scouting trip that includes neighboring countries draws from the same allowance. Bring your Social Security award letter, passport, and background check to any consultation, and confirm current documents and fees with DGME before placing a deposit.

The State Department’s Level 1 advisory reflects a sharp drop in gang activity since 2022, but it does not replace neighborhood judgment. The same advisory still limits U.S. government staff from most nighttime travel between cities, apart from the routes between San Salvador and the airport and the coastal highway to La Libertad. Follow the same habit: do your intercity driving in daylight. El Salvador also enforces zero tolerance on drinking and driving; any detectable alcohol can lead to detention.

Walk the blocks around a prospective apartment at different times of day. Map the route to groceries, transit, and the clinic you would actually use. Enroll in STEP before a longer visit so travel updates come directly to you.

The rent may fit. The bigger question is whether your ordinary errands feel easy enough to make the city feel like home.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is $2,000 a month realistic for one retiree in San Salvador?

Yes. With rent around $770-$899, groceries around $350-$500, and limited dining out, the sample core budget runs from $1,472-$1,899 before separately priced healthcare needs. ### How much income do I need to retire in El Salvador?

The pensionado residency route requires stable foreign pension income of at least three times the commerce-and-services minimum wage, about $1,226.40 a month in 2026. The rentista route for other passive income requires about $1,635.20. Since March 31, 2026, temporary residents need to spend only 90 days a year in the country. Confirm current figures with DGME before applying. ([hansgoldstein.com](https://hansgoldstein.com/moving-abroad/el-salvador-retirement-residency-income/))
