## The Story Behind the Dish

Ceviche, a dish with deep roots in Latin American coastal communities, has become a staple in Panama City. The Mercado de Mariscos, located near the city's historic Casco Viejo, is a hub for fresh seafood, drawing locals and tourists alike. The market itself is a testament to Panama's maritime heritage, with its fishermen bringing in the day's catch each morning. Ceviche, traditionally made with corvina or other white fish, reflects the region's reliance on the sea and its bounty. The acidity of the lime juice not only flavors the fish but also acts as a natural preservative, a technique that has been used for generations.

## Regional Variations

While ceviche is a staple across Latin America, Panama's version is notable for its simplicity. The focus is on the freshness of the fish, often corvina, which is marinated in lime juice with minimal additional ingredients. In contrast, Peruvian ceviche might include sweet potatoes or corn, while Mexican versions often feature shrimp and avocado. In Panama, the emphasis is on letting the natural flavors of the fish shine through. This straightforward approach reflects the local palate and the availability of fresh seafood. For those exploring the region, trying ceviche in different countries offers a fascinating insight into how a single dish can be adapted to suit local tastes.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

At the Mercado de Mariscos, a cup of ceviche typically costs $3, making it an affordable treat. Portions are generous enough for a light meal or a substantial snack. When ordering, it's helpful to know a bit of Spanish, as most vendors speak limited English. Simply ask for "un ceviche, por favor," and be prepared to choose between different types of fish or seafood, such as corvina, shrimp, or mixed seafood. For those on a budget, enjoying ceviche at the market is a great way to experience local flavors without breaking the bank. It's an opportunity to eat like a local and enjoy the simple pleasures of fresh, flavorful food.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before diving into your cup of ceviche, it's important to understand the role of acidity in the dish. The lime juice not only flavors the fish but also "cooks" it, changing its texture and making it safe to eat. If you're sensitive to citrus, be mindful of the tartness, which is a defining characteristic of ceviche. The market can be busy, especially on weekends, so plan your visit accordingly. For those considering retirement or extended stays in Panama, this market is a microcosm of local life, offering insights into daily habits, social interactions, and the importance of seafood in the Panamanian diet.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is assuming all ceviche is the same. In Panama, the focus is on simplicity and freshness, so don't expect the same ingredients or preparation methods you might find elsewhere. Another error is not exploring beyond the main stalls at the market. While the front-facing vendors are popular, the deeper sections of the market offer a more traditional experience and often better prices. Lastly, some tourists might overlook the importance of timing; visiting the market earlier in the day ensures the freshest selections and a more relaxed atmosphere.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is ceviche safe to eat in Panama City?

Yes, ceviche is safe to eat in Panama City, especially at reputable places like Mercado de Mariscos. The lime juice used in the dish acts as a natural preservative and "cooks" the fish, making it safe for consumption.

### What type of fish is used in Panamanian ceviche?

In Panama, corvina is the most commonly used fish for ceviche, prized for its firm texture and mild flavor. However, other types of white fish or seafood like shrimp may also be used.

### Can I find ceviche in restaurants outside the market?

Yes, ceviche is a popular dish in many restaurants throughout Panama City. However, the market offers a more traditional and budget-friendly experience, with fresh seafood and a lively atmosphere.

### What should I drink with ceviche?

A cold beer or a refreshing soda pairs well with ceviche. If you're looking for a local option, try a Panama Lager, which complements the zesty flavors of the dish well.
