# The Vietnam Beachfront Condo Question: Could a $400/mo Retirement Budget Work in 2026, and What Would You Actually Pay for Rent, Groceries, Utilities, Transport, and Dining?

## Headline Framing and Cost Reality Check
Imagine waking up to the sound of waves in a beachfront condo in Vietnam. While the dream is enticing, the reality is that a true beachfront condo for $400/month is rare. In popular expat areas like Da Nang, rents for a one-bedroom often range from $500 to $700. While this might stretch your budget, the overall cost of living remains affordable. Groceries can cost between $175 and $250 per month, and utilities, including internet, typically range from $50 to $80. Dining out is a treat, with a casual lunch costing $3 to $6.50, and a modest dinner for two priced at $11 to $26.

**Estimated core monthly spend:** $1,800-$2,000  
**Remaining buffer vs the $2,000 budget:** +$200

**How your money compares to the US:** That $3 lunch? Your usual Panera combo back home is $12. A $40/month private plan covers what a $450 US premium buys.

**Income requirement:** Varies by visa type — confirm with the consulate

**A Typical Day in Vietnam**  
Start your day with a coffee at a beachfront café for about $1.70. Enjoy a casual lunch for $3, and take a short ride-hail trip for $0.40. Pick up fresh vegetables at the market for $0.85, and end the day with a dinner for two at $11.

## Residency Pathways and Healthcare Access
Vietnam doesn't offer a formal retirement visa, but there are other pathways for long-term stays. Options include temporary residence cards, often tied to investment or employment. These require specific documents, so it's essential to verify the requirements for your chosen route. Healthcare in Vietnam is accessible through private facilities, with insurance options available. Local insurance premiums vary, and it's wise to compare them with international plans to ensure comprehensive coverage.
