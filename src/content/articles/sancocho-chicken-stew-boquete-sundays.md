## The Story Behind the Dish

Sancocho is more than just a meal; it is a cultural emblem of Panama, with roots tracing back to the indigenous and Spanish influences that have shaped the region's culinary history. Traditionally, sancocho was a dish prepared by rural families using whatever ingredients were available, often cooked over an open fire. The stew's name comes from the Spanish word "sancochar," which means to parboil, reflecting the cooking method where ingredients are simmered slowly to extract deep flavors. Over time, sancocho has become a symbol of Panamanian hospitality, often served during family gatherings and celebrations. Its evolution from a humble rural dish to a national favorite underscores its importance in Panamanian culture.

## How Locals Eat It

In Boquete, sancocho is not just a meal; it's a Sunday ritual. Locals often gather with family and friends, sharing stories and laughter over steaming bowls of this nourishing stew. While some prefer to enjoy it at home, others head to local eateries where the stew is prepared in large pots, ensuring enough for everyone. It's common to see sancocho served with a side of rice, which diners mix into the broth to soak up every bit of flavor. A squeeze of lime and a sprinkle of fresh cilantro are often added just before eating, enhancing the stew's aromatic profile. For many, sancocho is a reminder of home and community, a dish that brings people together.

## Regional Variations

While sancocho is a staple across Panama, each region adds its own twist to the dish. In Boquete, the cooler climate influences the stew's preparation, with heartier ingredients like yucca and plantains playing a central role. Coastal areas might incorporate seafood into their sancocho, offering a lighter version that reflects the ocean's bounty. In the Azuero Peninsula, a region known for its cattle ranches, beef is often used instead of chicken, resulting in a more robust stew. Despite these variations, the essence of sancocho remains the same: a comforting, communal dish that adapts to the resources and tastes of each region.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What ingredients are typically used in sancocho?

Sancocho typically includes chicken, yucca, plantains, corn, and a variety of root vegetables such as carrots and potatoes. The broth is seasoned with herbs like culantro, garlic, and onions. Some variations might include beef or seafood, depending on the region.

### Is sancocho spicy?

Sancocho is generally not spicy, but some cooks may add a touch of pepper to enhance its flavor. If you're sensitive to heat, you can request a milder version or add spice to your liking with hot sauce on the side.

### Can I find sancocho outside of Panama?

Yes, sancocho is popular in several Latin American countries, each with its own variation. However, the Panamanian version is distinct in its use of ingredients and preparation methods, making it a unique dining experience.

### What's the best time to eat sancocho in Boquete?

Sundays are the most popular day for eating sancocho in Boquete, as it's a traditional meal for family gatherings. That said, many restaurants serve it throughout the week, especially during lunch hours.
