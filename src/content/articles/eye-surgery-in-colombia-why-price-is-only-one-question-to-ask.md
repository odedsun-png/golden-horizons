# Eye Surgery in Colombia: What price can and cannot tell you about total cost and care

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Medellín can make a surgery stay less costly around the edges: a one-bedroom outside the center runs about $485-$1,420, groceries can land near $150-$250 monthly, utilities run about $70-$180, and an urban transit pass is about $20-$35. But Colombia does not have one reliable national cataract-surgery price, and a quote must spell out the lens, facility, anesthesia, medicines, and follow-up. For an older American, the real question is whether two eye-care quotes, a workable recovery stay, and a clear aftercare plan add up better than treatment at home.

A lower surgery quote can catch your eye. The more consequential question comes afterward: where will you stay when you need a follow-up visit, who answers if your vision changes after hours, and how long can you comfortably remain before flying home?

That is where Medellín enters the conversation. A one-bedroom near the city center is estimated at about $885 a month, a figure that may put clinics, pharmacies, and the Metro within easier reach during recovery. It does not make surgery “cheap.” It may, however, make the days around surgery more manageable—if the clinic, surgeon, lens choice, and aftercare plan hold up to close questioning.

Medellín has established eye-care providers, including Clínica Clofán and Clínica de Oftalmología Sandiego. Foreign-patient support varies by clinic.

Centro and La Candelaria offer central access, while housing outside the center spans a much wider range by building and neighborhood.

## What a Recovery Stay in Medellín Actually Costs

The surgery quote is only one line in the notebook. A realistic plan also has room for 14 to 30 nights of lodging, rides to follow-up appointments, eye drops, meals, and perhaps an extra quiet week before you fly home.

Medellín’s ordinary expenses can make that recovery window less financially daunting. A café coffee commonly runs $2-$4, a casual lunch can be $5-$12, and a local transport ride is about $0.70-$1.00. After a procedure, those are not tourist details. They are the small decisions that shape whether daily life feels simple or exhausting.

At the market, a half-loaf of bread may cost $0.60-$0.91, a liter of milk about $0.91-$1.21, and a dozen eggs about $1.82-$2.42. A modest dinner can be $12-$25, while dinner for 2 at a mid-range restaurant often reaches $15-$35. Internet is commonly $20-$40 monthly, which can make a furnished recovery apartment more practical than a hotel room if your stay runs several weeks.

**Estimated core monthly spend:** $600-$900 in a lower-cost scenario, not counting healthcare or international travel; roughly $1,350-$1,600 in a higher-cost central-district scenario.

That range reflects a very different lifestyle choice. Outside the center, one-bedroom rents are reported around $485-$1,420. In the city center, a one-bedroom is estimated at roughly $885 a month. Housing can fit the plan—but the clinic’s follow-up schedule should determine where you stay, not the other way around.

### A Typical Day in Medellín

Picture a Tuesday like this: a $2-$4 coffee near your lodging, a $0.70-$1.00 ride to an appointment, then a $5-$12 casual lunch before you head back to rest. Pick up bread or milk for $0.60-$2.00, and a modest dinner may run $12-$25.

That kind of day can feel pleasantly ordinary rather than like a hospital bill. Still, airfare, surgery, prescription drops, and lodging are separate decisions—not one bargain bundled under the phrase “medical tourism.”

### Lower Living Costs Are Not a Surgical Price List

Numbeo and Expatistan offer useful signals about Medellín’s rents, meals, utilities, and transport. Both are crowd-sourced, however. They can help you plan for a $3-$8 casual meal or a $15-$35 dinner for 2; they cannot tell you what a particular surgeon will charge for your cataract procedure.

There is no verified single national cataract-surgery price for Colombia in 2026. A quote can differ depending on whether treatment is for 1 eye or 2, the intraocular lens selected, anesthesia arrangements, medicines, and the number of postoperative visits included. The lower local cost of living is real context. It is not an answer by itself.

### The Residency Question Can Wait—Until Your Stay Gets Longer

For a short recovery stay, timing and follow-up may matter more than residency. If you are considering a longer period, Colombia’s retirement and other visa pathways exist, but their income or savings requirements are not fixed in the evidence reviewed here.

Before building a 2-month or 3-month plan around a promising rental, contact the Colombian Ministry of Foreign Affairs or accredited immigration counsel for the current 2026 rules. Bring your passport details, proof of income or savings requested for your visa category, and your planned medical timeline. A visa pathway is useful only if it fits the medical timetable you and your clinic have agreed upon.

## The Clinic Conversation Matters More Than an Online Deal

Clínica Clofán and Clínica de Oftalmología Sandiego give you two Medellín starting points for direct questions. Fundación Oftalmológica Nacional and Fundación Barraquer are established Colombian eye-care names in Bogotá, useful reference points if you are comparing provider models across more than one city.

The important comparison is not simply clinic versus clinic. It is your diagnosis, your surgeon, your lens options, and the practical plan after the procedure. A careful cataract quote should separate the surgeon’s fee, facility fee, lens choice, anesthesia, medicines, and postoperative visits. If one estimate covers 1 eye and another covers 2, they are not comparable—even if one number initially looks far more appealing.

Then ask about the days that follow. Who sees you on day 1? During week 1? What happens if you notice a concern after day 7? Confirm whether an English-speaking coordinator is available, whether instructions are provided in writing, and how the clinic handles an urgent change in vision outside normal hours.

SURA and Sanitas are recognized Colombian insurers, but this research does not establish that either will cover an American visitor’s particular procedure or follow-up needs. Ask each clinic directly whether it accepts foreign self-pay patients, what it requires before surgery, and whether private insurance is relevant to your specific visa or stay.

### Public Care, Private Care, and Medicare

The available research does not provide a public-system cataract-surgery price or a public enrollment path for a visiting American in Medellín. It also does not provide a standard private cataract-surgery price. That is why the useful comparison starts with 2 itemized private-clinic quotes and the known costs of a 14- to 30-day recovery stay, such as $485-$1,420 for an outside-center one-bedroom and $20-$35 for monthly transit.

Do not count on Medicare to pay for planned eye surgery in Colombia. Medicare coverage outside the United States is limited, so call Medicare and your supplemental insurer before you travel. Ask the clinic whether it expects self-payment before surgery and whether SURA or Sanitas plays any role in your particular care plan.

For a short surgical stay, start with the clinic rather than trying to enroll in a public program. Send your passport information, diagnosis, eye records, medication list, and any test results the clinic requests. For a longer stay, the Colombian Ministry of Foreign Affairs is the official starting point for visa documents and residence rules.

### A Calm Way to Get Real Answers

1. Contact Clínica Clofán and Clínica de Oftalmología Sandiego for 2 written cataract-surgery estimates based on your diagnosis and lens preferences.

2. Ask whether each quote covers 1 eye or 2 eyes, anesthesia, postoperative medicines, and every scheduled review during the first 30 days.

3. Request the surgeon’s credentials, the emergency contact method, and written instructions for a complication after the first 24 hours.

4. Compare lodging in Centro or La Candelaria with a second neighborhood outside the city center. Then map the trip to your chosen clinic at 2 different times of day.

5. If recovery could extend beyond a short visit, contact the Colombian Ministry of Foreign Affairs or accredited immigration counsel. Ask for the active 2026 income rule, document list, and expected processing timeline for the visa category that fits your stay.

6. Keep your U.S. ophthalmologist involved before departure and arrange a return-home follow-up plan for the first 30 days after surgery.

### Safety Is About the Route You Will Actually Use

No citywide safety ranking was substantiated in the research reviewed here, so it would be careless to call Medellín safe or unsafe for your particular stay. What you can test is much more personal: make 2 daytime trips between your lodging and clinic, locate 1 nearby pharmacy, and confirm how you would reach urgent care after hours.

When vision is limited, a recovery apartment that saves 20 minutes each way may be worth more than a lower nightly rate farther from the clinic.

## Practical Comparison

### Total cost of care

**What the evidence says:**  
Medellín’s rent, food, and transport can be lower than many U.S. city costs, but there is no verified single price for cataract surgery across Colombia. A 2-week stay and a 2-eye procedure require separate calculations for lodging, flights, medicines, and follow-up.

**What to check next:**  
Build 2 complete scenarios using written clinic quotes, airfare, and accommodation near your preferred provider.

### Surgeon and lens choice

**What the evidence says:**  
Colombia has established ophthalmology providers, but the available research does not support an apples-to-apples national comparison of surgeons, outcomes, or premium-lens charges. A 2026 quote needs to identify the lens and all included services.

**What to check next:**  
Ask each clinic for surgeon credentials, lens options, expected recovery milestones, and the number of included postoperative appointments.

### Follow-up care in Medellín

**What the evidence says:**  
Postoperative support is central to cataract care, especially across the first 1 to 30 days. Clinics may differ in language support, appointment availability, and urgent-care arrangements.

**What to check next:**  
Confirm who answers after-hours questions, how quickly an urgent visit can be arranged, and whether instructions are available in English.

### Medicare, insurance, and self-pay planning

**What the evidence says:**  
Medicare should not be treated as payment for planned surgery in Colombia. The research also does not establish coverage for an American visitor through SURA or Sanitas, so a clinic’s self-pay terms may matter more than an insurer name.

**What to check next:**  
Call Medicare, your supplemental insurer, and your preferred clinic before booking. Ask for the payment schedule, cancellation terms, and the cost of an unscheduled postoperative visit.

### Longer stays and visa feasibility

**What the evidence says:**  
Retirement and longer-stay visa routes exist, but 2025-2026 eligibility rules can change and are not a substitute for a clinic recovery schedule. A visa route is not proof that a particular care plan is medically sensible.

**What to check next:**  
Request current requirements from the Colombian Ministry of Foreign Affairs before booking a stay longer than 30 days.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I retire in Colombia and arrange eye surgery there?

Colombia has retirement and longer-stay visa options, though the income threshold and document list depend on the visa category. For a recovery stay of 30 days or less, the clinic schedule and your travel timing may matter more than residency. If you are considering 2 months or longer, contact the Colombian Ministry of Foreign Affairs before signing a lease and ask for the active document list and processing timeline. ([Colombian Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co))

### What does cataract surgery cost in Colombia?

There is no verified single national cataract-surgery price that applies across Colombia in 2026. The figure changes with 1-eye versus 2-eye treatment, the intraocular lens selected, anesthesia, medicines, and included reviews. Request itemized estimates from at least 2 clinics rather than treating an online listing as a final quote. ([MediList](https://www.medilist.com))

### What does daily life in Medellín cost during recovery?

A city-center 1-bedroom is estimated at about $885 monthly, while housing outside the center is reported around $485-$1,420. Groceries may run $150-$250 monthly, utilities $70-$180, transport $20-$35, casual dining $3-$12, and a modest dinner $12-$35. These are crowd-sourced planning signals, but they give you a useful starting point for a 14- to 30-day recovery budget. ([Numbeo](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Medellin))

### How can I judge the quality of eye care in Medellín?

Start with 2 direct clinic conversations, such as with Clínica Clofán and Clínica de Oftalmología Sandiego, and ask about the individual surgeon—not only the clinic name. Request credentials, lens options, postoperative scheduling, and the urgent-care process during the first 7 days. The available evidence does not establish a single nationwide quality ranking, so the written aftercare plan matters as much as the initial quote. ([Clínica Clofán](https://clofan.com))

### What should I plan for after surgery before returning to the United States?

Plan for a written follow-up schedule, medication instructions, and a direct contact method if symptoms change after hours. A 30-day plan is more useful than a flight date alone because postoperative care may involve more than 1 visit. Before you leave, ask your U.S. eye doctor what records they need from the Colombian clinic and ask Medicare how its limited overseas coverage applies to your situation. ([Expatistan](https://www.expatistan.com/cost-of-living/medellin))
