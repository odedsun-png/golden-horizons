# North Macedonia - Residency Options for American Retirees

As you approach retirement, you might be considering whether your Social Security, pension, or savings could offer you more options abroad than in the United States. North Macedonia presents an intriguing possibility for American retirees, with its scenic landscapes and lower cost of living. However, the biggest practical trade-off involves navigating the residency permit process and ensuring financial sufficiency.

## What a ~$2,500 Retirement Budget Could Feel Like

In North Macedonia, living on a budget of around $2,500 per month could allow for a modest lifestyle, especially if you choose to live outside the capital, Skopje. Housing and daily expenses are generally lower than in many U.S. cities, but costs can vary significantly depending on your location and lifestyle choices. It's crucial to verify current housing and healthcare costs, as these will take a significant portion of your budget. Always check today's prices before making a decision.

## Where Your Housing Dollars May Go

Housing in North Macedonia can be affordable, with options ranging from city apartments to rural homes. The cost will vary based on location, size, and amenities. Skopje, being the capital, might have higher rental prices compared to smaller towns. It's advisable to explore current listings on platforms like Expat.com to get a sense of the market and find a place that fits your budget and lifestyle.

## Healthcare Reality

Healthcare in North Macedonia requires having valid health insurance, which is a prerequisite for obtaining a temporary residence permit. The quality of healthcare services can vary, with better facilities typically found in urban areas. Retirees should investigate the availability and coverage of health insurance plans that meet their needs. For the latest information on healthcare options and costs, refer to Expat.com.

## Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

To stay longer than 90 days, U.S. retirees must obtain a Type D visa and apply for a temporary residence permit under the category of "other legitimate reasons," which includes retirement. This permit is valid for one year and is renewable. After three years of continuous residence, you may apply for permanent residence. Tax residency is defined by spending 183 days in the country within a 12-month period. Always check the U.S. Department of State's travel advisory for safety updates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I live in North Macedonia on $2,500 a month?**  
It's possible with trade-offs, but verify current housing and healthcare costs.

**What is required for a temporary residence permit?**  
Proof of sufficient income, health insurance, housing, and a clean criminal record.

**How long can I stay in North Macedonia without a visa?**  
U.S. citizens can stay visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period.

**What are the tax residency rules?**  
You become a tax resident if you stay for 183 days or more within a 12-month period.

**Is North Macedonia safe for retirees?**  
Generally safe, but check the U.S. Department of State's travel advisory for updates.
