# France - Hidden Villages in Provence for Retirement — A Local's Perspective

In Provence, the cost of living can be surprisingly affordable compared to busy French cities like Paris. For instance, renting a two-bedroom house in the village of Roussillon can cost around €800 per month. Surrounded by ochre cliffs and lavender fields, these villages offer a picturesque setting at a fraction of the price you'd expect in more famous French locations. This article will help you understand what to expect when considering retirement in the hidden villages of Provence.

## What €2,000/Month Actually Buys in Roussillon

With a budget of €2,000 per month, life in Roussillon can be both comfortable and fulfilling. Rent for a two-bedroom home is about €800, leaving you with €1,200 for other expenses. Grocery shopping at the local market on Place de la Poste is a delightful experience. Fresh produce, cheese, and meats are not only abundant but also reasonably priced. For around €300 a month, you can enjoy high-quality local ingredients. Dining out is a treat as well; a three-course meal at "Le Piquebaure," a local eatery, will set you back about €30 per person.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/17794159/pexels-photo-17794159.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Transportation is another area where costs are manageable. Owning a car is advisable for exploring the wider region, and monthly expenses, including insurance and fuel, typically amount to about €250. However, if you prefer public transport, the bus service connecting the village to nearby towns like Apt is reliable and affordable, costing just a few euros per trip.

One limitation to be aware of is the seasonal nature of the village. While the summer months are busy with visitors and activities, winter can be quieter, with some businesses closing for the season. However, for many retirees, this slower pace during the cooler months adds to the village's tranquility.

## Healthcare Access in Lourmarin

Lourmarin, another treasure in Provence, offers excellent healthcare options. The village is home to a well-equipped medical center, Centre Médical du Luberon, which provides a range of services including general practice, physiotherapy, and specialized care. For more extensive medical needs, the Polyclinique du Luberon in nearby Cavaillon is just a 30-minute drive away and is known for its high-quality care and modern facilities.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/37384330/pexels-photo-37384330.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Pharmacies in Lourmarin are plentiful, and the pharmacists are well-trained and helpful, often serving as the first point of contact for minor health concerns. The French healthcare system is renowned for its efficiency and comprehensive coverage, and as a retiree, you can access it through the national health insurance scheme once you become a resident. Monthly contributions are typically around 8% of your pension income, which provides access to a wide range of medical services.

Language can be a barrier in healthcare settings, as many medical professionals may not be fluent in English. However, this is gradually changing, and hiring a translator or taking French classes can ease communication hurdles. Lourmarin itself is an active community with a mix of locals and expatriates, and joining local clubs or language classes can enhance your integration and ease any initial language challenges.

## Cultural and Social Life in Bonnieux

Bonnieux offers a variety of cultural and social activities, ideal for retirees looking to immerse themselves in local life. The village hosts a variety of events, from the weekly Tuesday market at Place Carnot, where you can mingle with locals and find artisanal products, to the annual Lavender Festival, which celebrates the region's agricultural heritage.

Art enthusiasts will find Bonnieux particularly appealing. The village is dotted with galleries showcasing local artists, and the nearby "Galerie Ortus" often hosts exhibitions and workshops. If you have a passion for painting or photography, this creative environment can be incredibly inspiring.

Social life in Bonnieux is centered around its cafés and restaurants. "Le Fournil," located in an ancient bakery, is a popular spot among both locals and expats, offering a warm atmosphere and traditional Provençal dishes. Joining local clubs, such as the pétanque league or the local history society, can provide opportunities to connect with the community and make new friends.

One aspect that may surprise you is the level of international influence in this seemingly remote village. Many expats call Bonnieux home, contributing to a diverse and welcoming community. This combination of cultures can be both comforting and enriching, offering a sense of familiarity while also encouraging you to embrace new experiences.
