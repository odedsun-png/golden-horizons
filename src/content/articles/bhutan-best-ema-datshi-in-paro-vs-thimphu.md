# Bhutan - Best Ema Datshi in Paro vs Thimphu

## Quick Taste

Ema Datshi, a quintessential Bhutanese dish, is a fiery stew made from chilies and cheese, and it holds a special place in the hearts of the Bhutanese people. This dish is a staple across the country, but the experience of eating Ema Datshi can vary significantly between Paro and Thimphu. Paro is known for its adherence to traditional methods, offering a rustic taste that reflects the region's agricultural roots. In contrast, Thimphu, the bustling capital, provides a more modern take on the dish, with variations that cater to a diverse palate and dietary preferences.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Ema Datshi is more than just a dish; it is a symbol of Bhutanese culture and identity. The name "Ema Datshi" translates to "chili" (ema) and "cheese" (datshi), which are the two primary ingredients that define its distinct flavor. Historically, this dish was created by Bhutanese farmers who relied on local produce to create hearty meals that could sustain them through the harsh Himalayan winters. The chilies used in Ema Datshi are typically green or red, and they are known for their intense heat, which is balanced by the creamy texture of the cheese.

Over the years, Ema Datshi has evolved from a simple farmer's meal to a national dish that is celebrated across Bhutan. It represents the Bhutanese love for spicy and comforting foods and is often served at important cultural events and family gatherings. The dish's popularity has also spread beyond Bhutan's borders, with many international visitors eager to try this well-known stew. Despite its simplicity, Ema Datshi's long history and cultural significance make it a common choice for anyone visiting Bhutan.

## How Locals Eat It

In Bhutan, Ema Datshi is a versatile dish that is enjoyed at any time of the day. Locals often eat it for breakfast, lunch, or dinner, and it is commonly served with red rice, a Bhutanese staple that complements the dish's spicy and creamy flavors. The combination of Ema Datshi and red rice provides a satisfying and nourishing meal that is both filling and flavorful.

In addition to red rice, Ema Datshi is sometimes accompanied by other Bhutanese dishes such as kewa datshi (potatoes with cheese) or shamu datshi (mushrooms with cheese). These side dishes add variety to the meal and allow diners to experience different textures and flavors. To enhance the richness of the meal, locals often pair Ema Datshi with suja, a traditional butter tea that is both salty and creamy. This combination of flavors creates a harmonious balance that is deeply satisfying and comforting.
