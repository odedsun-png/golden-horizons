# Bali Healthcare for Foreign Retirees: The Hospital Question to Ask First

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Bali can make ordinary expenses feel refreshingly light: private GP visits typically run $20-$50, specialist appointments $50-$150, and a one-bedroom can range from $500-$800 in Sanur or Ubud. But before you choose a beachside lease or a favorite café, ask one harder question: if your care becomes serious, where would you go next—and who would get you there? A solo retiree may spend about $1,300-$2,300 a month before private health insurance. The daily savings are real. Your hospital and evacuation plan need to be just as real.

## Retirement Snapshot

**Primary decision:**  
Choose a Bali neighborhood only after you know which private hospital you would use, how you would reach it, and whether your insurance includes medical evacuation.

**Best fit:**  
Americans age 55 and older who are comfortable using private care, can maintain international insurance with evacuation coverage, and prefer established expat areas such as Sanur, Ubud, Canggu, or Seminyak.

**Biggest warning:**  
BIMC Hospital, Siloam Hospital Bali, and Sanur International Hospital are key private-care options for expats, but complex cases may require evacuation to Singapore or Bangkok.

**Residency:**

**Healthcare:**

**Housing / daily life:**

## The Hospital Question That Matters More Than the View

The question is straightforward: **“If I need care beyond a routine consultation, where would you transfer me—and who arranges it?”**

Ask it before you fall in love with a rental lane, a favorite restaurant, or the idea of waking up near the water. Ask it at BIMC Hospital, Siloam Hospital Bali, and Sanur International Hospital. Then ask the same question of the insurer you are considering.

A routine appointment can be reassuringly affordable. Private GP visits typically run $20-$50, and specialist appointments commonly cost $50-$150. Those are manageable figures for an ordinary concern. They do not, by themselves, answer what happens when treatment becomes more complicated.

BIMC Hospital has locations in Kuta, Nusa Dua, and Ubud, while Siloam Hospital Bali and Sanur International Hospital serve other parts of the island’s expat geography. Where you live changes the experience. A Sanur appointment may feel quite different from getting to care from Canggu, Pererenan, or Umalas on a busy day.

For serious procedures, some patients need evacuation to Singapore or Bangkok. That makes international insurance with medical evacuation coverage a central part of a retirement plan—not an optional add-on. The lower cost of a local visit is appealing. The question is whether your policy, your preferred hospital, and your own health needs fit together when the situation is no longer routine.

Medicare usually does not pay for routine care outside the United States. Keep your Medicare choices separate from your Bali plan, and make sure your international policy covers treatment in Bali and emergency evacuation.

## What Everyday Life Costs Once You Add It Up

Bali’s cost advantage is easiest to see in the small, repeatable parts of a week. Coffee at a local café can run $1-$4. A casual lunch may be $4-$10, while a modest dinner can be $8-$20 at a mid-range expat venue or closer to $3-$8 at a local spot.

Housing determines how much breathing room those pleasant daily prices actually create. In quieter Sanur or Ubud, one-bedroom rentals commonly run about $500-$800 a month. In Canggu and Seminyak, where the expat scene is denser and rents are under more pressure, comparable housing often runs $750-$1,250.

Groceries and dining for one person generally fall around $350-$600 monthly. Bottled water runs about $0.50-$1, rice about $0.50-$1 per kilogram, and local vegetables about $1-$3 per kilogram. Internet and mobile service typically cost $30-$60 monthly; utilities can add $40-$100 or more, depending in part on air-conditioning use.

Transport is another quiet line in the budget. A scooter rental can run about $39-$55 monthly, while a mix of scooter use and ride-hailing typically lands around $60-$150 a month.

**Estimated core monthly spend:** $1,480-$2,720

That estimate includes rent, groceries and dining, utilities, transport, and modest extras. It does not include the personal cost of international health insurance or major medical treatment.

**How your money compares to the US:** U.S. one-bedroom rent often falls around $1,000-$2,000, and a casual lunch commonly costs $10-$20. U.S. monthly groceries for one person often run $300-$500, while private health insurance for Americans age 60 to 64 can cost $400-$800 or more each month. In Bali, rent and lunch can be lower—but the insurance decision carries more weight because serious care may involve leaving the island.

## A Typical Day in Bali

Picture a Tuesday in Sanur. You start with a $1-$4 coffee, pick up vegetables at about $1-$3 per kilogram, and have a $4-$10 lunch. A modest dinner might run $8-$20, while your transport remains part of a $60-$150 monthly habit.

In Canggu, the food prices may still feel manageable, but the housing math changes. A one-bedroom around $750-$1,250 can take a larger share of your monthly budget than a $500-$800 rental in Sanur or Ubud. And if you need a $20-$50 GP appointment, the distance between your home and preferred hospital matters as much as the consultation fee.

That is Bali’s real balancing act: modest daily costs, neighborhood-by-neighborhood housing differences, and a healthcare plan that works beyond an ordinary Tuesday.

## The Visa and Insurance Conversation to Have Early

Indonesia offers the KITAS Retirement E33F pathway for people age 55 and older. It requires verified pension or passive income and private health insurance, then renews in 1-year periods for up to 5 years, with a possible later path to KITAP.

Golden Visa options and the Second Home Visa also exist, though they are structured around long-term investment or substantial deposits. Documentation, bank requirements, and processing times can differ by route. Before committing to a 12-month rental, contact Indonesian immigration or a qualified agent for the current E33F rules.

Bring your proof of pension or passive income and proof of private health insurance when you discuss the E33F route. Ask for the current document list, filing process, and expected processing time before you pay a long-term lease deposit. The available retirement-visa guidance confirms the age 55 threshold and 1-year renewal terms, but it does not give one fixed income figure for every applicant.

For healthcare, start with the private hospitals near your chosen neighborhood rather than assuming a public enrollment path will meet your needs. Bring your insurance card and policy details to BIMC Hospital, Siloam Hospital Bali, or Sanur International Hospital. Ask the admissions desk which services are billed directly, what you pay upfront, and how emergency transfers are approved.

The sensible order is simple. First, confirm that your age, income evidence, and insurance meet the current E33F requirements. Next, obtain insurance quotes that specifically include emergency treatment in Bali and evacuation to Singapore or Bangkok. Then visit or contact the private hospitals nearest the neighborhoods you are considering.

The paperwork may not be the romantic part of a Bali retirement plan. It is, however, what allows the easier parts of life—the morning coffee, the walk, the dinner out—to stay easy.

## The Golden Horizons 30-Day Test

1. Spend 7 days in Sanur and 7 days in Canggu. Time the trip from each rental area to Siloam Hospital Bali or BIMC Hospital during both morning and evening traffic.

2. Visit BIMC Hospital in Kuta, Nusa Dua, or Ubud and ask for current pricing for a $20-$50 GP appointment and a $50-$150 specialist appointment.

3. Tour Sanur International Hospital and ask how a patient would be transferred to Singapore or Bangkok if a procedure is unavailable locally.

4. Request at least 2 international insurance quotes with medical evacuation coverage. Ask how each insurer handles hospital admission and transfer approval in Bali.

5. Collect at least 3 one-bedroom rental quotes: one in Sanur or Ubud near $500-$800, and one in Canggu or Seminyak near $750-$1,250.

6. Track ordinary costs for 30 days: $1-$4 coffees, $4-$10 lunches, $1-$3-per-kilogram vegetables, $60-$150 in monthly transport, and $70-$120 for utilities plus internet.

### What is the first hospital question a foreign retiree should ask in Bali?

Ask where you would be transferred if you needed care beyond a routine consultation or specialist appointment. BIMC Hospital, Siloam Hospital Bali, and Sanur International Hospital are key private-care options, with GP visits typically costing $20-$50 and specialists $50-$150. Ask each hospital who coordinates a transfer to Singapore or Bangkok and what your insurer must approve. (Expat.com Bali guide, May 2026.)

### What monthly budget should a single retiree expect in Bali?
