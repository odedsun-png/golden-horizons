# Morocco - Oceanfront Living on Morocco’s Atlantic Coast — A Local's Perspective

Did you know that living in Morocco can cost as much as 50% less than living in the United States? With the Atlantic Ocean stretching along Morocco's western edge, cities like Essaouira and Agadir offer not just affordability but also a chance to indulge in a coastal lifestyle. While the idea of oceanfront living might sound like a dream, this article will help you weigh the realities of making such a move.

## What $1,500/Month Actually Buys in Essaouira

In Essaouira, a budget of $1,500 a month can offer you a surprisingly comfortable lifestyle. For instance, you can rent a furnished two-bedroom apartment within walking distance of the beach for around $500 to $700 per month. Look at neighborhoods like the Kasbah or the Medina, where you can find charming homes with traditional Moroccan design elements. Utilities, including electricity, water, and gas, often come to about $50 to $100 monthly, depending on your usage.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/34855148/pexels-photo-34855148.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Dining out is another area where your dollar stretches further. A meal at a mid-range restaurant will cost about $10 to $15 per person, while local eateries offer delicious tagines and couscous for as little as $3 to $5. For fresh produce, the Souk El Had market is the place to be. Here, you can buy a week's worth of fruits and vegetables for under $20.

However, one limitation to keep in mind is the inconsistency in internet service. While it's improving, speeds can be slower than what you're used to in the U.S. This could be a significant consideration if you plan to work online or stream a lot of content. Overall, Essaouira offers a balanced lifestyle with just enough modern conveniences to keep you comfortable.

## Healthcare Options: What to Expect in Agadir

Healthcare is a crucial consideration for retirees, and Agadir has several options worth noting. The Hassan II Hospital is one of the primary public healthcare facilities in the area. While public hospitals are generally affordable, they can be crowded and may lack some of the amenities you're used to. For more personalized care, Clinique de l'Atlantique offers private healthcare services with English-speaking staff. A consultation at a private clinic typically costs around $30 to $50, which is significantly lower than in the U.S.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/30419070/pexels-photo-30419070.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

For prescription medications, pharmacies are readily available, and many medicines are available over-the-counter. In fact, some medications that require a prescription in the United States can be bought directly from the pharmacist here, often at a fraction of the cost. However, it’s essential to bring a list of your current medications, including their generic names, to facilitate easy communication.

One unexpected aspect of healthcare in Morocco is the prevalence of traditional remedies and herbal treatments. While these are widely accepted and used by locals, it's advisable to consult a healthcare professional before trying them. Agadir’s healthcare system may not be perfect, but the combination of private and public options provides a reasonable level of care for retirees.

## Transportation and Getting Around in Rabat

Rabat, the capital city, offers a variety of transportation options that make getting around relatively straightforward. The city’s tram system is efficient and costs about $0.70 per ride. It connects key areas, including the Medina and the more modern districts like Agdal. For those who prefer driving, car rentals are available, with prices starting at around $300 per month. However, keep in mind that traffic can be tricky, especially during peak hours.

Taxis are another convenient option. There are two types: the petit taxi, which operates within city limits, and the grand taxi, which can take you to nearby towns or even across the country. A typical ride within the city will cost about $2 to $5, depending on the distance.

One thing that might surprise you is the prevalence of bicycles. Rabat has been investing in bike lanes and cycling infrastructure, making it easier for residents to choose this eco-friendly mode of transport. However, be cautious as road conditions can vary, and not all drivers are used to sharing the road with cyclists.

While Rabat provides a range of transportation choices, it's important to remember that public transport can be crowded, particularly during rush hour. Overall, with a bit of planning, getting around Rabat can be both affordable and efficient.
