# Greece - Healthcare Quality for Expats in Greece — A Local's Perspective

Greece's healthcare system is a combination of public and private services, with public healthcare funded by the state and managed by the National Organization for Healthcare Provision (EOPYY). As of 2023, Greece spends about 8% of its GDP on healthcare, which is slightly below the European Union average. This article will help you evaluate whether Greece's healthcare quality meets your needs as an expat retiree considering a move.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want affordable healthcare with access to both public and private options.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to spend around $200–$400 on private health insurance if opting out of the public system.
- **Biggest advantage:** Access to high-quality, affordable private healthcare.
- **Biggest warning:** Public hospitals can be overcrowded, especially in urban areas.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Public vs. Private Healthcare Options

In Greece, public healthcare is available to all residents, including expats with a valid residence permit. The public system, however, can be strained, particularly in larger cities like Athens and Thessaloniki. Public hospitals such as the Evangelismos Hospital in Athens provide essential services but often face issues like long wait times and limited resources.

Conversely, private healthcare offers a more expedited and comfortable experience, though at a higher cost. Private hospitals like Hygeia Hospital in Athens or the Metropolitan Hospital in Piraeus are well-equipped and provide a range of services. While private healthcare is generally perceived as higher quality, it requires either out-of-pocket payments or private insurance, which can range from $200 to $400 monthly depending on coverage and age.

One limitation is that not all medical professionals in Greece are fluent in English, which can pose a communication barrier. Seek out English-speaking practitioners, particularly in private facilities, to ensure clarity in medical consultations and treatments.

## Access to Medical Facilities

In urban areas, access to medical facilities is generally good, with numerous hospitals and clinics available. For example, Athens has several public hospitals like the Athens General Hospital G. Gennimatas and private ones like the Athens Medical Center. However, in rural areas or on smaller islands, access can be limited, and facilities may not offer the same level of care found in cities.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1604145195376-e2c8195adf29?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8R3JlZWNlfGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODExNjcyNzJ8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

For routine check-ups and minor ailments, local clinics and pharmacies are widespread and can offer immediate assistance. Pharmacies in Greece are well-stocked, and pharmacists can often provide advice and over-the-counter solutions for common health issues.

A practical warning for expats is the potential for strikes within the public healthcare sector, which can disrupt services. It's wise to have a backup plan, such as knowing the nearest private facility or having a telemedicine option available.

## Insurance and Cost Considerations

Expats in Greece must decide whether to rely on public healthcare or opt for private insurance. The public system is funded through social security contributions, which expats employed in Greece will pay. However, retirees not contributing to the Greek social security system may find private insurance more suitable.

Private insurance costs vary, with basic plans starting around $200 per month. Comprehensive coverage, including dental and vision, can run upwards of $400 monthly. Assess your healthcare needs and budget accordingly.

A common assumption is that private insurance will cover all services without additional costs, but some procedures or specialist visits may still require out-of-pocket expenses. Reviewing policy details and exclusions is essential to avoid unexpected costs.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Before moving to Greece, verify the healthcare access you will have based on your residency status. If you plan to use private healthcare, ensure your insurance covers necessary treatments and includes emergency services.

Visa requirements may vary based on your nationality and intended length of stay. A residence permit is typically required for stays longer than 90 days. Consult official sources or an immigration lawyer for the most current information.

Tax obligations can be complex, as Greece has agreements with the U.S. to prevent double taxation. Understand your tax responsibilities in both countries. Professional advice can help clarify these obligations.

While Greece is generally safe, be aware of pickpocketing in tourist areas and strikes that may affect public services, including healthcare.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Assuming all doctors speak English; verify language skills before appointments.
- Overlooking the need for private insurance if relying on public healthcare isn't feasible.
- Not factoring in travel costs for medical appointments if living in rural or island areas.
- Ignoring the potential for public healthcare strikes and not having a backup plan.
- Failing to review private insurance policies for exclusions and coverage limits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is public healthcare free for expats in Greece?

Public healthcare is available to expats with a valid residence permit, but it may not be free. Costs can be subsidized, but expect some out-of-pocket expenses. Verify your eligibility and coverage details.

### Can I use my U.S. health insurance in Greece?

Most U.S. health insurance plans do not cover international healthcare costs. Consider purchasing international health insurance tailored for expats living in Greece.

### What are the main hospitals in Athens?

Athens hosts several major hospitals, including Evangelismos Hospital and Hygeia Hospital. For private care, Athens Medical Center and Metropolitan Hospital are popular choices.
