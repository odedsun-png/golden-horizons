## Quick Taste

Picture yourself in a lively San José soda, a small local eatery, where the midday sun filters through the windows, casting a warm glow on the tables. In front of you is a casado, Costa Rica's quintessential lunch plate. The aroma of grilled meats mingles with the earthy scent of black beans and rice. The plate is a popular mosaic of flavors and textures: tender chicken or beef, a mound of white rice, a ladle of savory black beans, a fresh salad, and a slice of fried plantain. For about 3,500 Costa Rican colones (roughly $6), this hearty meal offers a glimpse into everyday life in Costa Rica, where simplicity and satisfaction go hand in hand.

## The Story Behind the Dish

The casado, which means "married" in Spanish, is said to have originated from the idea of marrying various components on a single plate, much like a harmonious marriage. This dish has been a staple in Costa Rican households for generations, reflecting the country's agricultural roots. Traditionally, it was a meal prepared for laborers who needed a nutritious and filling lunch to sustain them through the workday. Over time, the casado has evolved to include a variety of ingredients, each representing the diverse produce of Costa Rica. It remains a popular choice for locals and visitors alike, offering a taste of home-cooked comfort.

## How Locals Eat It

Locals typically enjoy casado during lunchtime, often between 12 and 2 PM, when sodas are lively with activity. The dish is a communal experience, where people gather to share stories over a hearty meal. In Costa Rica, the casado is more than just food; it's a pause in the day, a moment to recharge and connect. While tourists might opt for more familiar international options, locals know that the best casados are found in neighborhood sodas, where the ingredients are fresh, and the flavors are genuine. Accompany your casado with a refreshing glass of natural fruit juice, like tamarindo or guanábana, for the full experience.

## Where to Find It

Sodas are the heart and soul of Costa Rican dining, and they are the best places to find a casado. These small, family-run eateries are scattered throughout cities and towns, offering affordable and delicious meals. In San José, head to the Mercado Central, where you can find numerous sodas serving up steaming plates of casado. For those staying in coastal areas like Tamarindo or Manuel Antonio, local sodas provide a more laid-back atmosphere with the same comforting flavors. If you prefer cooking at home, visit a local market to pick up fresh vegetables, beans, rice, and meats to create your own casado.

## Regional Variations

While the core components of a casado remain consistent, regional variations reflect the diverse culinary influences across Costa Rica. In the Caribbean region, you might find casados featuring coconut-infused rice and beans, adding a tropical flavor to the dish. In the highlands, trout from local rivers might replace the usual chicken or beef, offering a fresh twist. Some regions incorporate local vegetables like chayote or yuca, providing a different texture and taste. Despite these variations, the essence of the casado—a balanced, satisfying meal—remains unchanged, showcasing the adaptability and creativity of Costa Rican cuisine.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

The beauty of the casado lies in its affordability and generous portions. A typical casado costs around 3,500 colones (about $6), making it an excellent choice for budget-conscious travelers. Portions are substantial, often enough to satisfy even the heartiest appetite. When ordering, don't hesitate to ask for recommendations or daily specials, as sodas often offer different meat options depending on the day's fresh produce. If you're not fluent in Spanish, a simple "Un casado, por favor" will suffice, and the friendly staff will guide you through any additional choices, such as the type of meat or preferred sides.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before diving into a casado, it's helpful to understand the cultural significance of this dish. It's not just a meal; it's an embodiment of Costa Rican hospitality and simplicity. Expect a no-frills presentation, as the focus is on flavor and nourishment. While the dish is generally mild, you can add a kick with local hot sauces like Salsa Lizano, a tangy condiment favored by Ticos. For those with dietary restrictions, most sodas are accommodating and can modify ingredients upon request. Remember that lunch is the main meal of the day in Costa Rica, so a casado is designed to be filling and satisfying.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is overlooking sodas in favor of more tourist-oriented restaurants. Sodas offer a more genuine and affordable dining experience, where you can enjoy a true taste of Costa Rican life. Another mistake is not venturing beyond the standard chicken or beef options. Exploring different meats and regional variations can enhance your dining experience. Lastly, some visitors may underestimate the portion size of a casado, leading to wasted food. It's perfectly acceptable to ask for a smaller portion or to share a plate if you're not too hungry.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is typically included in a casado?

A traditional casado includes rice, black beans, a choice of meat (such as chicken, beef, or pork), a side salad, and fried plantains. Some variations may also include tortillas, eggs, or additional vegetables.

### Can I find vegetarian or vegan casado options?

Yes, many sodas offer vegetarian or vegan versions of casado, replacing the meat with grilled vegetables, tofu, or additional beans. It's always a good idea to ask the staff about available options.

### Is casado available for dinner as well?

While casado is primarily a lunch dish, some sodas and restaurants may offer it for dinner. However, it's less common, as Costa Ricans typically have a lighter meal in the evening.

### How does casado compare to other Costa Rican dishes?

Casado is one of the most popular and widely consumed dishes in Costa Rica, known for its balanced flavors and hearty portions. It's a staple meal, unlike more festive dishes like gallo pinto, which is often served for breakfast.
