## Cost Overview and 1-Bedroom Rent Neighborhood Ranges

San José offers a range of living costs, largely influenced by your choice of neighborhood. In central districts, a furnished 1-bedroom apartment typically rents for $600–$900 (~$600-900). If you venture to the outskirts, such as Escazú or Santa Ana, you might find slightly lower prices, but these areas often come with longer commutes.

Groceries for a single retiree range from $300 to $600 (~$300-600) monthly, depending on whether you shop at larger supermarkets or local markets. Utilities, including electricity, water, and trash, generally cost between $120 and $200 (~$120-200), while internet services add another $20 to $60 (~$20-60).

Public transportation is a viable option, with bus fares costing around $0.50–$0.70 (~$0.50-0.70) per ride. For occasional taxi or rideshare services, expect to pay $1–$6 (~$1-6) per trip.

## Residency Pathways for Retirees

Costa Rica offers several residency options for retirees, including the Pensionado and Rentista visas. The Pensionado visa requires proof of a monthly pension, while the Rentista visa demands evidence of a stable income or savings. Specific thresholds for 2026 should be confirmed with Costa Rican immigration authorities, as they are subject to change.

Both visa types allow access to the public healthcare system (CCSS), but many expats opt for private insurance to ensure quicker access to services.

## Healthcare Access and Costs for Foreigners

Costa Rica's healthcare system is a dual model, combining public and private options. Enrollment in the public CCSS system is possible for residents, but private healthcare is often preferred by expats for its efficiency. Private GP visits are affordable compared to the U.S., and private insurance is widely used.
