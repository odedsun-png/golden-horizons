# What a Night in a Private Hospital Costs in Costa Rica

Golden Horizons Status: PLAN AHEAD — GET A CASE-SPECIFIC ESTIMATE

> THE SHORT ANSWER: There is no verified nationwide nightly price in the evidence reviewed for this article. One commercial guide estimates private rooms at approximately $250–400 at Clínica Bíblica and $300–500 at Hospital CIMA, but those are not hospital-issued quotes or the total cost of treatment. The useful number is an itemized estimate for your care, followed by confirmation of what your insurer will pay and what you may need to pay upfront.

“What does a night cost?” sounds like a straightforward question.

In a hospital, it is only the beginning.

The room is a place where treatment happens. It does not tell you which doctors will attend, what tests will be needed, which medicines will be given, or whether your condition will require a different level of care.

For someone considering retirement in Costa Rica, that distinction is more useful than an attractive nightly figure. You need to know not only what a hospital might charge, but how the bill would be paid when you are least able to negotiate it.

The best preparation is not memorizing a national average. It is identifying an appropriate hospital, understanding your coverage, and knowing whom to contact before a planned admission—or during an emergency.

## What the Room Rate Does—and Does Not—Cover

The honest answer is that the sources reviewed do not establish one dependable nightly rate for Costa Rica’s private hospitals.

A commercial guide published in March 2026 gives these approximate room figures:

| Facility named in the commercial guide | Third-party estimate |
|---|---:|
| Clínica Bíblica | $250–400 per night |
| Hospital CIMA | $300–500 per night |

These are not current quotations issued by either hospital. The guide does not establish the exact room category, included services, billing period, or terms applicable to you.

They should not be combined into a claim that a private hospital stay “costs $250–500.” They describe a reported room charge, not the whole admission.

Hospital CIMA’s own estimate page gives a more important qualification: estimates are approximate and may change following medical evaluation and according to the patient’s specific circumstances.

That does not make an estimate useless. It tells you how to use it—as a documented starting point whose assumptions and exclusions need to be clear.

### The five numbers to separate

| Number | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Room charge | The price of a specified room category and billing period |
| Admission deposit | What the hospital asks you to provide initially |
| Estimated treatment cost | The expected cost under stated medical assumptions |
| Insurer’s approved payment | What your particular policy has authorized |
| Patient responsibility | The amount left to you after applicable coverage and adjustments |

A manageable deposit does not prove that the final bill will be manageable. A hospital’s relationship with an insurer does not prove that your treatment is covered.

Ask what the room charge includes. Then ask whether nursing, physician visits, diagnostics, medicines, supplies, procedures, and other services appear within that charge or elsewhere.

Also ask how the billing period works. “One night” should not be left to interpretation: confirm whether the hospital charges by calendar day, a defined admission period, or another method.

Sources: [ExpatEmergency — third-party room estimates](https://expatemergency.com/best-private-hospitals-costa-rica) and [Hospital CIMA — estimates](https://hospitalcima.com/en/contact-us/budgets/).

## Private Care, Public Care, and Your Payment Plan

Costa Rica has the public CCSS system alongside private healthcare providers. For planning purposes, keep your public-system enrollment separate from the question of who pays a private hospital.

Do not assume that being enrolled in CCSS authorizes a private admission at public expense. Establish the applicable arrangement before relying on it.

Private care also requires a more specific question than “Do you accept my insurance?”

At Hospital CIMA, the published insurance instructions explain that coverage depends on the policy and the insurer’s approval. The hospital describes guarantee-letter requirements and says a credit card may be requested for expenses not covered by insurance.

Its international-insurance guidance also warns that some insurers may require patients to pay at the time of service and claim reimbursement afterward.

That is the difference between having insurance and having a workable payment plan.

### Three facilities to contact—not three interchangeable quotes

For a comparison in the San José metropolitan area, useful direct starting points include:

- Hospital Clínica Bíblica, which provides an online estimate-request system.
- Hospital CIMA in Escazú, which publishes estimate, payment, and insurance information.
- Hospital Internacional La Católica in San Antonio de Guadalupe, Goicoechea, which lists hospitalization, emergency, intensive-care, and surgical services.

This is a contact list, not a ranking of quality, price, or suitability.

Confirm the campus, relevant service, physician arrangements, and payment process. A quotation for one facility or treatment should not be assumed to apply elsewhere.

## The Questions to Ask Before You Need a Bed

For planned care, start with the treatment rather than a generic room inquiry.

Tell the hospital what your physician has recommended, which facility you are considering, and whether you would be self-pay or insured. Use the hospital’s secure process for any medical records.

Then ask for an estimate that answers:

### What is being priced?

- What treatment, procedure, or admission does the estimate assume?
- Which room category and length of stay are included?
- Is the care inpatient, observation, or another category?
- What would cause the estimate to change?
- How long is the quotation valid?

### What is included?

- Room and nursing services.
- Attending physician and specialist fees.
- Anesthesia or other professional services, if relevant.
- Laboratory tests and imaging.
- Medicines, supplies, and equipment.
- Emergency assessment before admission.
- Applicable taxes and administrative charges.

Do not assume every category is separate—or included. Ask the hospital to identify the arrangement.

### What would I pay, and when?

- Is an admission deposit required?
- How is it calculated?
- Could further payments be requested during the stay?
- Does the insurer pay the hospital directly?
- What authorization or guarantee is needed?
- Which deductibles, coinsurance, exclusions, or limits could apply?
- How are unused deposits or later adjustments handled?

### What happens if the plan changes?

- How are extra days priced?
- What if a higher level of care is necessary?
- Would additional physicians bill separately?
- How will the patient or authorized contact receive updated estimates?

Finally, confirm the billing currency and accepted payment methods.

CIMA states that it accepts colones and U.S. dollars, along with specified card and transfer arrangements. It says transfers must be made before admission and receipt confirmed. Other hospitals may have different procedures.

A currency conversion in your retirement spreadsheet is not the exchange rate or payment arrangement the hospital has agreed to use.

Sources: [CIMA — estimates](https://hospitalcima.com/en/contact-us/budgets/) and [CIMA — payment methods](https://hospitalcima.com/centro-de-informacion/formas-de-pago/).

## The Trade-Off

The appeal of private care is having another route to investigate for the treatment and provider arrangements you want.

The financial responsibility is making that route workable.

You may have a comprehensive policy and still need to satisfy authorization requirements. You may have a favorable estimate and still face additional treatment. You may be able to pay a deposit without being prepared for the eventual patient balance.

None of that is a reason to dismiss private care. It is a reason to plan around the admission rather than the bed.

For an American retiree, Medicare deserves a separate check. Original Medicare generally does not cover healthcare outside the United States, apart from narrow exceptions. Do not assume a Costa Rican private admission will be paid because you continue paying U.S. premiums.

Likewise, a healthcare discount membership should not be treated as insurance simply because it reduces some prices. Establish exactly what the product promises and what remains your responsibility.

The aim is not to predict every medical expense. It is to avoid discovering the payment process while you are ill.

## The Golden Horizons 30-Day Test

1. Identify appropriate care near your intended home.  
   Confirm the relevant facilities, campuses, and services. Do not assume the best-known hospital name is automatically the right choice.

2. Ask about the admission and estimating process.  
   For planned treatment, request a case-specific written estimate. If you have no treatment planned, ask for the payment and insurance procedures rather than expecting a meaningful total for an unspecified stay.

3. Separate the hospital estimate from your responsibility.  
   Record the expected charges, insurer authorization, exclusions, deductible, coinsurance, and any deposit.

4. Establish how payment would work.  
   Confirm direct billing versus reimbursement, accepted payment methods, required documents, and an insurer contact available when needed.

5. Prepare a practical information sheet.  
   Keep medication and allergy information, policy details, emergency contacts, and relevant provider numbers accessible. Ask facilities which identification and records they require.

6. Review the whole arrangement.  
   Check how your local care, CCSS enrollment where applicable, private insurance, travel coverage, and U.S. coverage fit together. Record unresolved questions instead of assuming another system will pay.

This is a planning exercise, not a deadline or a reason to postpone treatment. In an emergency, seek urgent assistance; do not wait to complete a cost comparison.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does one night in a private Costa Rican hospital cost?

There is no verified national price in the evidence reviewed here. A commercial guide estimates room charges of $250–400 at Clínica Bíblica and $300–500 at CIMA, but those are not hospital quotations or complete admission costs. Obtain current, case-specific information directly.

### Does a room charge include doctors, tests, and medicines?

Do not assume so. Ask the hospital to identify which services are included and which are billed elsewhere. A room quote without its inclusions is not enough to estimate the whole stay.

### If the hospital accepts my insurer, am I covered?

Not necessarily. Coverage depends on your policy, the treatment, authorization, and payment arrangements. CIMA’s instructions explicitly refer to insurer approval and guarantee documentation.

### Could I need to pay even with insurance?

Yes. Depending on the arrangement, you may owe deductibles, coinsurance, excluded expenses, or upfront payments while awaiting reimbursement. Confirm both the hospital’s requirements and the insurer’s response.

### Does the hospital estimate guarantee the final price?

Not automatically. CIMA says estimates may change according to medical evaluation and patient circumstances. Ask what could change the total and how updates will be communicated.

### Will CCSS or Medicare pay the private bill?

Do not assume either will. Confirm any applicable CCSS arrangement directly. Original Medicare generally does not cover care abroad except in limited circumstances.

### How much emergency money should I keep available?

This article cannot establish one safe amount for every retiree. Your required access to funds depends on policy terms, deposits, exclusions, reimbursement timing, and circumstances. Use those details—not a nightly room figure—to plan.
