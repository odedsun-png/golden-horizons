# Nepal - Healthcare Quality for Expats in Nepal

Nepal offers a unique cultural experience for expats, but it's important to understand the healthcare landscape before moving. Healthcare quality varies significantly between urban centers like Kathmandu and rural areas. Expats are not covered by Nepal's constitutional guarantee of free basic and emergency health services, making private healthcare and international insurance essential. Medical evacuation is advisable for serious medical issues due to limited services outside major cities.

## Retirement Snapshot

- **Estimated Monthly Healthcare Costs**: USD 100–400 for individual insurance premiums
- **Healthcare Quality**: Mixed public-private system; quality index approx. 44–52/100
- **Access to Services**: Limited outside Kathmandu; private hospitals offer higher standards
- **Visa/Residency**: Residential Visa for retirees aged 60+; renewable annually
- **Transport**: Ambulance services unreliable outside Kathmandu
- **Safety Advisory**: Level 2 – Exercise increased caution
- **Best Fit**: Expats willing to invest in private healthcare and insurance
- **Biggest Warning**: Limited public healthcare access for expats

## What the Monthly Budget Actually Looks Like

Expats in Nepal should budget for private healthcare and international insurance. Consultation costs range from USD 7 to 36, while hospital stays can cost between USD 36 and 145 per day. Private rooms can reach up to USD 360. Insurance premiums vary from USD 100 to 400 per month for individuals, highlighting the need to plan for healthcare expenses.

## Where You Would Live / Housing Reality

While specific housing costs are not verified, expats typically reside in urban areas like Kathmandu where healthcare services are more accessible. Private hospitals in Kathmandu, such as CIWEC Clinic and Grande International Hospital, offer higher standards of care with English-speaking staff.

## Healthcare Reality

Nepal's healthcare system is a mix of public and private facilities. Expats must rely on private hospitals for quality care, as they are excluded from the constitutional guarantee of free basic health services. The healthcare quality index ranges from 44 to 52 out of 100, indicating variability in service quality. International health insurance is crucial, particularly for emergencies and potential medical evacuation.

## Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Expats aged 60 and above can apply for a Residential Visa, renewable annually, but it does not lead to permanent residence or citizenship. The U.S. Department of State advises exercising increased caution due to unrest and limited medical services. Medical evacuation is recommended for serious conditions, given the unreliable ambulance services outside Kathmandu.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is healthcare free for expats in Nepal?**
No, expats are excluded from the constitutional guarantee of free basic and emergency health services.

**What is the quality of healthcare in Nepal?**
The healthcare quality index ranges from 44 to 52 out of 100, with better services available in private hospitals in urban areas.

**Do I need health insurance in Nepal?**
Yes, international health insurance is essential, especially for emergencies and potential medical evacuation.
