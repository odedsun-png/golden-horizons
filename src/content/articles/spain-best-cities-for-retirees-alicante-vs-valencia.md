# Spain - Best Cities for Retirees: Alicante vs Valencia — A Local's Perspective

Alicante and Valencia, two jewels on Spain's eastern coast, offer distinct lifestyles for retirees considering a move abroad. Alicante boasts over 300 days of sunshine each year, making it a haven for those seeking a sunny retirement. Valencia, Spain’s third-largest city, seamlessly combines modern living with historical features. This article will help you weigh the pros and cons of retiring in either Alicante or Valencia.

## What $2,000/Month Actually Buys in Alicante

With a monthly budget of $2,000, Alicante offers a comfortable lifestyle that many retirees find appealing. In neighborhoods like El Campello or Albufereta, you can rent a two-bedroom apartment with sea views for around $850 to $1,000. This leaves room in your budget for utilities, groceries, and dining out. For instance, a three-course meal at a mid-range restaurant in Alicante typically costs about $25 per person.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/32480904/pexels-photo-32480904.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

The local markets, such as Mercado Central, provide fresh produce at reasonable prices. Picture picking up a kilo of tomatoes for under $2 or enjoying locally sourced seafood at a fraction of the cost you'd pay in the U.S. The public transport system, including buses and trams, is efficient and affordable, with a monthly pass costing around $40.

However, Alicante isn't without its limitations. While the city is active and welcoming, English proficiency among locals can vary, which might pose a challenge if you don’t speak Spanish. Moreover, healthcare facilities in Alicante, although adequate, are not as advanced as those found in larger Spanish cities. Nonetheless, the coastal lifestyle can outweigh these considerations for many.

## Valencia's Healthcare and Public Transport: What to Expect

Valencia stands out with its robust healthcare system, which is among the best in Spain. The city is home to Hospital Universitari i Politècnic La Fe, one of the top medical facilities in the country. Retirees can expect high-quality care with relatively short waiting times, especially if you opt for private insurance, which can be quite affordable.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/35072546/pexels-photo-35072546.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Public transportation in Valencia is comprehensive and user-friendly. The metro and bus systems cover the city extensively, with a monthly transport pass costing around $55. Neighborhoods like Ruzafa and El Carmen offer both modern and historical settings, each accessible via public transport.

While Valencia offers a cosmopolitan lifestyle, it comes with a slightly higher cost of living than Alicante. For example, renting a comparable apartment in central Valencia might cost up to $1,200 monthly. However, this price reflects the city’s active cultural life, with numerous festivals, museums, and dining options.

A potential downside is the city’s pace, which might not appeal to those seeking a quieter retirement. Despite this, many find that the cultural offerings and excellent healthcare in Valencia justify the higher expenses.

## Social Life and Community: Alicante vs. Valencia

Alicante offers a slower-paced lifestyle, which often translates to a tight-knit community feeling. In areas like San Juan Beach, expats and locals mingle at beachside cafes and during community events. The city’s smaller size makes it easier to form lasting relationships, as you’re likely to bump into familiar faces regularly. The Explanada de España, a promenade lined with palm trees, is a favorite spot for leisurely walks and friendly chats.

In contrast, Valencia’s larger population means a more diverse social scene. The city is known for its active festivals, such as Las Fallas, providing numerous opportunities to engage with both locals and other expats. The historic Barrio del Carmen is a popular area for socializing, featuring a range of bars, cafes, and cultural venues.

Valencia also has a more international community, with many English-speaking expats and locals, making it easier to integrate if you don’t speak Spanish fluently. However, this larger scale can sometimes make Valencia feel less personal than Alicante.

One surprise for many is the extent of green spaces in Valencia, such as the Turia Gardens, which were once a riverbed. These areas provide a peaceful retreat within the city, challenging the assumption that urban living must be noisy and chaotic.
