## Quick Taste

As the sun rises over Batumi's lively port, locals gather at a seaside café to enjoy a piping hot Adjarian Khachapuri. This boat-shaped bread is filled with gooey cheese, topped with a raw egg yolk, and finished with a generous pat of butter. The dish is a sensory delight: the crisp edges of the bread contrast with the molten cheese and the creamy egg, creating a harmonious blend of textures and flavors. For about GEL 12-15 (approximately $4-5), this dish offers a glimpse into the everyday life of those who call this Georgian coastal city home.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Adjarian Khachapuri hails from the Adjara region of Georgia, where Batumi serves as its popular heart. This dish reflects the region's maritime culture, with its boat-like shape symbolizing the vessels that dot the Black Sea. Traditionally, khachapuri was a humble meal for sailors, providing a hearty and sustaining option before a long day at sea. Over time, it has evolved into a staple across Georgia, celebrated for its simplicity and the comfort it brings. The dish's roots in the seafaring lifestyle of Batumi's residents make it an integral part of the local culinary identity.

## How Locals Eat It

Locals in Batumi enjoy Adjarian Khachapuri as a breakfast staple or a hearty snack throughout the day. The ritual involves tearing off pieces of the bread and dipping them into the cheese and egg mixture, ensuring each bite is perfectly balanced. It's not uncommon to see people sharing a khachapuri with friends, accompanied by a glass of chacha, Georgia's potent grape brandy. For a more subdued experience, a cup of Turkish-style coffee pairs beautifully with the dish's richness. The communal aspect of eating khachapuri reflects the warmth and hospitality inherent in Georgian culture.

## Where to Find It

For those looking to experience Adjarian Khachapuri like a local, head to the central Batumi market. Here, vendors offer freshly baked khachapuri at reasonable prices, often lower than those found in tourist-heavy areas. Alternatively, small family-run bakeries scattered throughout the city provide a more intimate setting to enjoy this dish. In the evening, many restaurants along Batumi Boulevard serve khachapuri as part of their menu, where you can dine with a view of the Black Sea. ## Regional Variations

While Adjarian Khachapuri is well-known in Batumi, other regions of Georgia boast their own versions of khachapuri. In Imereti, a round, flat version is filled with cheese and sometimes herbs, offering a simpler yet equally satisfying experience. The Mingrelian variant adds an extra layer of cheese on top, creating a gooey, enjoynt delight. In the mountainous regions, khachapuri may be made with a blend of cheeses, incorporating tangy sulguni for a sharper flavor. Each variation reflects the local ingredients and tastes, providing a distinct twist on the traditional recipe.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

Expect to pay around GEL 12-15 (about $4-5) for a standard Adjarian Khachapuri in Batumi. Portions are generous, often large enough to serve as a meal for one or a shared snack for two. When ordering, don't hesitate to ask for extra butter or cheese if you're in the mood for a richer experience. If you're dining with friends, consider ordering a few different types of khachapuri to sample the regional variations. For those cooking at home, local markets offer fresh ingredients like sulguni cheese and dough, allowing you to recreate this dish in your own kitchen.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before diving into Adjarian Khachapuri, be prepared for its richness. The combination of cheese, egg, and butter makes for a decadent dish that can be quite filling. It's best enjoyed fresh from the oven when the cheese is still bubbling and the bread retains its crispness. If you're watching your calorie intake, consider sharing with a friend or pairing it with a light salad. For those with dietary restrictions, some bakeries offer variations with less cheese or alternative fillings, ensuring everyone can partake in this Georgian delight.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is not mixing the egg yolk and butter into the cheese before eating. This step is crucial for achieving the dish's signature creamy texture. Another pitfall is ordering khachapuri in tourist-heavy areas, where prices can be inflated and the quality may not reflect the traditional experience. Instead, seek out local bakeries or markets for a more genuine taste. Lastly, don't rush through your meal; enjoy the flavors and the communal aspect of dining, which is central to Georgian culture.
