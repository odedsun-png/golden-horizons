# Morocco - Hidden Desert Towns and Blue Villages to Explore — A Local's Perspective

Chefchaouen, known as the "Blue Pearl" of Morocco, is an active town in the Rif Mountains, where nearly every building is painted in varying shades of blue. With a population of around 42,000, this town offers a unique cultural experience and a slower pace of life compared to the busy cities of Morocco. This article will help you decide if retiring to Morocco's lesser-known towns and villages, like Chefchaouen, is right for you.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want a culturally rich, tranquil lifestyle amidst stunning landscapes.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to spend around $1,200 to $1,500 for a modest lifestyle, including rent, food, and transportation.
- **Biggest advantage:** Affordable cost of living with rich cultural experiences.
- **Biggest warning:** Language barriers and limited healthcare facilities in smaller towns.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Chefchaouen - The Blue Pearl of Morocco

Chefchaouen is not just famous for its blue-painted streets; it’s a town where you can enjoy a peaceful retirement with a touch of Moroccan culture. Rent for a two-bedroom apartment can range from $300 to $500 per month, depending on the location and amenities. The local medina is a treasure trove of artisan shops, cafes, and markets where you can buy fresh produce and handmade crafts.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1527338611623-4e242563220a?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwxMXx8TW9yb2Njb3xlbnwwfDB8fHwxNzgxMDgwODY3fDA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

However, Chefchaouen's healthcare facilities are limited. The nearest major hospital is in Tetouan, about a 1.5-hour drive away. For serious medical needs, traveling to a larger city like Casablanca or Rabat might be necessary. Despite this, the town's serene environment, combined with its cultural richness, makes it an appealing option for those seeking a slower pace of life.

## Merzouga - Gateway to the Sahara

Merzouga, a small village located near the edge of the Sahara Desert, offers retirees a unique desert experience. Known for its proximity to the Erg Chebbi dunes, Merzouga is ideal for those who enjoy tranquility and breathtaking landscapes. Accommodation options range from traditional desert camps to small guesthouses, with prices starting at around $200 per month.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1527960299979-ae13298358b6?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8TW9yb2Njb3xlbnwwfDB8fHwxNzgxMDgwODY3fDA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

The village is relatively remote, with the nearest hospital located in Errachidia, approximately a two-hour drive. This can be a significant downside for those with ongoing health concerns. Transportation is limited, with buses and shared taxis being the most common modes of travel. However, the stunning desert views and the opportunity to immerse yourself in Berber culture are significant draws for adventurous retirees.

## Asilah - Coastal Calm and Artistic Flair

Asilah, a coastal town on the Atlantic Ocean, is renowned for its whitewashed buildings and active art scene. With a population of about 30,000, Asilah offers beach life and cultural activities. The town hosts an annual arts festival, drawing artists from around the world, and has a busy medina with galleries, shops, and cafes.

Renting a one-bedroom apartment in Asilah can cost between $250 and $400 per month. The town is more accessible than some of the other locations discussed, with a train station providing connections to larger cities like Tangier and Casablanca. However, retirees should be aware of the seasonal influx of tourists, which can disrupt the otherwise calm atmosphere. The local healthcare facilities are basic, so major medical needs would require travel to Tangier.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Retirees considering Morocco should verify healthcare access, as smaller towns like Chefchaouen and Merzouga have limited facilities. Private insurance is advisable to cover potential medical needs that require travel to larger cities. Visa requirements for retirees can vary, so consulting with Moroccan consulates or legal experts is crucial for understanding residency options.

Tax obligations for U.S. citizens living abroad can be complex. It's important to understand both U.S. and Moroccan tax laws, possibly consulting a tax advisor familiar with international tax issues. Safety in Morocco is generally good, but retirees should stay informed about local conditions and any travel advisories.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Assuming English is widely spoken; learning basic Arabic or French is beneficial.
- Underestimating the impact of remote healthcare access in smaller towns.
- Not budgeting for potential travel to larger cities for medical care.
- Overlooking the need for a reliable transportation plan.
- Ignoring the cultural adjustment period required for living in a new country.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is healthcare accessible in Morocco's smaller towns?

Healthcare facilities are limited in smaller towns like Chefchaouen and Merzouga. For major medical needs, travel to larger cities such as Casablanca or Rabat may be necessary. Private insurance is recommended.

### What is the cost of living in Chefchaouen?

The cost of living in Chefchaouen is relatively affordable, with monthly expenses ranging from $1,200 to $1,500. This includes rent, food, and transportation.

### How can I travel within Morocco?

Transportation within Morocco includes buses, shared taxis, and trains in larger towns. In more remote areas, transportation options may be limited, so planning is essential.
