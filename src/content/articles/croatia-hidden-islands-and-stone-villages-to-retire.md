# Croatia - Hidden Islands and Stone Villages to Retire — A Local's Perspective

With over 1,200 islands scattered along the Adriatic Sea, Croatia offers more than just a postcard-perfect coastline. The allure of its hidden islands and stone villages is backed by a surprisingly affordable lifestyle, where $1,500 a month can cover your essential expenses. This article will help you understand the real costs and everyday experiences of retiring in this Mediterranean location.

## What $1,500/Month Actually Buys in Hvar

Picture settling down on the island of Hvar, known for its lavender fields and medieval architecture. Here, $1,500 a month can cover a comfortable lifestyle. Renting a modest apartment in the town of Stari Grad, one of the oldest towns in Europe, costs around $500 to $700 monthly. This leaves room for utilities, groceries, and dining out. For instance, your utility bills, including electricity, water, and internet, hover around $150. Groceries, sourced from local markets like Stari Grad Market, will set you back about $300 — and that includes fresh produce, local cheeses, and meats.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/37447234/pexels-photo-37447234.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Dining out is another affordable pleasure. A meal at a local konoba (a traditional Croatian tavern) costs about $10, offering you a taste of local specialties like pašticada, a slow-cooked beef stew, or fresh seafood. However, during the peak tourist season, prices can spike slightly, especially in dining.

While Hvar offers a serene lifestyle, it does come with its limitations. The island’s popularity means it can get crowded during summer months, which might not be ideal if you prefer solitude. Additionally, the island’s limited public transport can make getting around a bit challenging, especially if you don’t own a car. The ferry connections to the mainland are frequent but can be less convenient during the off-season. Nonetheless, the tranquility and beauty during the quieter months make it an appealing choice for those who enjoy a slower pace of life.

## Healthcare Options on Korčula

Korčula, another beautiful island, is not just about its picturesque views and rich history. It also offers reliable healthcare options for retirees. The island has a well-equipped health center, Dom Zdravlja Korčula, providing primary care services. For more specialized treatments, you might need to travel to the mainland, with the nearest major hospital being KBC Split. However, the local healthcare services are sufficient for regular check-ups and minor health issues.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/32414900/pexels-photo-32414900.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

In terms of health insurance, Croatia offers a public healthcare system, which expatriates can access by paying into the local health insurance fund. The cost is generally affordable, averaging around $75 per month. This grants you access to public healthcare facilities, which are generally well-regarded for their standard of care, although there can be language barriers as not all staff are fluent in English.

If you prefer private healthcare, Croatia has several private clinics and hospitals, especially in larger cities like Split and Dubrovnik. These facilities often have English-speaking staff and shorter waiting times, but come at a higher cost. Maintaining an international health insurance plan to cover private healthcare expenses and any emergencies that might require evacuation to another country is recommended.

Living on Korčula, with its combination of accessible healthcare and a serene lifestyle, provides a balanced retirement option. The island’s slower pace and community-oriented way of life offer a supportive environment for maintaining your health and well-being as you age.

## Everyday Life on Vis Island

Vis Island is where you’ll find traditional Croatian life and a close-knit community. The island's appeal lies in its authenticity — from the daily routines to the festivals that bring everyone together. Unlike some of the more tourist-heavy islands, Vis maintains a peaceful atmosphere year-round, making it an ideal retreat.

One surprising aspect of living on Vis is the strong sense of community. Local markets like the one in Vis Town are not just places to buy fresh produce but social hubs where you can catch up with neighbors and hear about local happenings. The cost of living is slightly lower here compared to Hvar, with rental prices averaging around $400 to $600 a month for a small apartment.

Transportation on the island is straightforward. While public transport is limited, most residents rely on bicycles or scooters to get around. The island is small enough that everything you need is within a short ride or walk. This slower pace is perfect for those who want to enjoy a leisurely lifestyle without the activity of larger tourist destinations.

One challenge you might face on Vis is the limited variety of entertainment and shopping options. The island is more suited to those who appreciate outdoor activities such as hiking and visiting historical sites. However, the occasional trip to Split, accessible by a two-hour ferry, can fulfill any cravings for an active city experience.

Vis is also a place where you can engage with Croatian culture and traditions. The island hosts several festivals throughout the year, such as the Vis Regatta, which draws sailing enthusiasts from all over. Participating in these events can enrich your retirement experience, offering a deeper connection to your new home.
