# Strategy - Don't Move Abroad Yet: Take This 30-Day Retirement Test First

**Golden Horizons Status:** 🟡 WORTH TESTING

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** A 30-day in-country test can provide tangible insights into daily life in a potential retirement destination, reducing the risk of premature relocation. This test should focus on non-touristic, routine experiences such as groceries, housing logistics, utilities, local transit, and neighborhood interactions. However, it is not a substitute for formal residency or long-term financial planning.

## Retirement Snapshot

**Planning-budget lens:** A 30-day test should emphasize testing ordinary daily life, including housing rental, local food, utilities, and transit costs, plus assessing neighborhood life and safety.

**Primary decision:** Whether a longer stay or formal relocation is justified based on experiential evidence gathered during the test.

**Residency:** Residency implications are country-specific; the test is a “test before commitment” rather than a residency approval path.

**Healthcare:** Access and proximity are central; assess how easily routine care can be obtained and what gaps exist.

**Housing / daily life:** Focus on rentability, neighborhood feel, housing quality, and ease of conducting routine tasks.

**Safety:** Observe daily-life safety context during the test; avoid broad national rankings without local evidence.

**Best fit:** The strategy is best for Americans aged 60–75 who want to test a lifestyle shift without committing to a long-term move.

**Biggest warning:** Do not treat a 30-day test as a substitute for formal residency, healthcare access planning, or long-term financial arrangements.

## Golden Horizons Decision Scorecard

- **Housing practicality:** Test rental processes and housing quality by renting a furnished unit for the duration of the test.
- **Daily-life practicality:** Evaluate grocery shopping, utilities, and internet reliability to understand routine living conditions.
- **Safety evidence:** Observe neighborhood safety and community interactions during the test period.
- **Healthcare access:** Locate and assess proximity to healthcare facilities and ease of accessing routine care.
- **Transportation:** Use local transit to gauge ease of movement and reliability.

**GH Takeaway:** A structured 30-day test can provide valuable insights into daily life abroad, helping retirees make informed decisions about potential relocation.

## The Planning-Budget Reality

A 30-day test should focus on understanding the practical costs of daily life, including housing, groceries, utilities, and transportation. While healthcare costs are not included in the core test expenditures, they must be budgeted separately when planning for a potential move.

## The Residency Reality

Residency pathways are country-specific and should not be assumed based on a short-term test. The 30-day test is designed to evaluate daily life rather than secure residency approval. Retirees should research specific residency requirements for their target destination before making any long-term commitments.

## Practical Comparison

The 30-day test framework focuses on evaluating housing availability, grocery and daily living costs, utilities and internet reliability, local transportation access, and neighborhood integration. These factors provide a realistic picture of what living in a new country might entail, without relying on tourism or superficial impressions.

## Who This May Fit

**Worth testing if:**
- You want to experience daily life in a potential retirement destination.
- You prefer to evaluate practical living conditions before committing to a move.
- You are open to testing healthcare access and community integration.

**May not fit if:**
- You expect the test to guarantee residency or long-term healthcare solutions.
- You are not prepared to handle potential language barriers or cultural differences.
- You seek immediate certainty about long-term living conditions.

## The Trade-Off

**What you may gain:**
- A clearer understanding of daily life abroad.
- Reduced risk of premature relocation.
- Evidence-based decision-making.

**What you may give up:**
- Certainty about residency eligibility.
- Long-term healthcare coverage assurance.
- Immediate financial planning clarity.

## The Golden Horizons 30-Day Test

1. Rent a furnished one-bedroom unit for 28–30 days in a target area to test housing practicality and lease mechanics.
2. Shop for groceries and basic supplies to establish daily-cost baselines and routine shopping rhythms.
3. Use local public transportation to gauge ease of movement, scheduling, and reliability.
4. Visit typical neighborhoods and everyday public spaces (cafés, markets, banks, post office) to assess social life and community integration.
5. Assess healthcare proximity and access by locating nearby clinics/hospitals and testing appointment scheduling for routine care.
6. Test internet service, postal/mail access, and essential services (banking, utilities) to ensure long-term functional viability.
