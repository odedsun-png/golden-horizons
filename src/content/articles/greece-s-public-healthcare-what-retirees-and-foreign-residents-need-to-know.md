# Greece’s Public Healthcare for Retirees and Foreign Residents: What You Should Know

**Golden Horizons Status:** 🟡 WORTH TESTING — VERIFY HEALTHCARE

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Greece is a Level 1 destination, and private hospitals such as Hygeia Hospital and Athens Medical Center cater to international patients. Original Medicare usually pays nothing for care in Greece, and you still carry a $1,736 Part A and $283 Part B deductible at home in 2026. Non-EU pensioners can join Greece’s public scheme only through optional insurance that costs 8.5% of pension income. The catch: you need a pensioner residence permit and an AMKA number first, so do not count on public care until EFKA confirms it in writing.

## Retirement Snapshot

**Primary decision:** Whether to rely on private insurance, Greece’s optional public insurance for non-EU pensioners, or a mix of both.  
**Best fit:** Retirees who will arrange coverage before moving and meet a hospital and an insurer in person first.  
**Biggest warning:** The EU registration pages do not describe American retirees. Your status decides your access.  
**Residency:** Confirm the income bar and insurance rule for your permit with a Greek consulate. Published figures differ from site to site.  
**Healthcare:** Public care runs through ESY hospitals and EFKA registration. Private hospitals fill the gaps for those who pay or are insured.

## Public Care in Greece: What Applies to You

Greece’s public hospitals sit in the national health system, ESY. EFKA handles social insurance, and the AMKA number is the social security number you present at the point of service.

The official EU contact point explains the rules for EU citizens: pensioners with a Greek pension are covered in Greece, and others rely on an S1 form from the country that pays their pension ([eu-healthcare.eopyy.gov.gr](https://eu-healthcare.eopyy.gov.gr/en/healthcare-in-greece/accessing-health-services-in-greece-for-eu-citizens/moving-to-greece-for-eu-citizens/)). That page covers EU citizens only. A U.S. Social Security pension does not create an S1 form.

For non-EU pensioners, a Greek news report from July 2025 describes optional insurance. You must live in Greece legally and permanently, hold a pensioner residence permit, have an AMKA, and have no other public coverage. The monthly contribution is 8.5% of your pension income, medicines carry a 25% co-pay, and the certificate renews every 12 months ([argophilia.com](https://www.argophilia.com/news/optional-health-insurance-for-third-country-pensioners-terms-and-procedures/243223)).

Rules like these change. Ask EFKA to confirm them before you count on them.

## What Medicare Pays at Home and Abroad

Original Medicare usually does not cover health care outside the U.S. Narrow exceptions exist, such as an emergency where a foreign hospital is closer than the nearest U.S. one. Medicare drug plans do not cover prescriptions bought abroad ([medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/travel-outside-the-u.s.)).

At home in 2026, Part A has a $1,736 deductible per benefit period, Part B has a $283 yearly deductible, and the standard Part B premium is $202.90 a month ([medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs)).

For many Americans over 65, staying on Medicare for major surgery can be cheaper than paying out of pocket in Athens. Greece makes more sense for everyday care, a second home base, or planned private coverage than as a place to replace Medicare for a big operation.

## Private Hospitals in Athens

Hygeia Hospital is a private hospital in Athens, part of the Hellenic Healthcare Group, with Joint Commission International accreditation and an International Patient Service ([hygeia.gr](https://www.hygeia.gr/en/)).

Athens Medical Center in Marousi belongs to Athens Medical Group, which runs an International Patient Center with multilingual staff and English-language medical records ([athensmedicalgroup.com](https://athensmedicalgroup.com/international)).

Neither site lists prices or accepted insurers. Ask each hospital for a written quote for the care you expect to need, and ask your insurer for written confirmation that the hospital is in its network.

## Travel Advisory

The State Department rates Greece Level 1: Exercise Normal Precautions, last updated October 22, 2025. It notes that petty crime is common in tourist areas and that large demonstrations can disrupt transportation with little notice ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/content/tsg_aem/us/en/home/international-travel/travel-advisories/destination.grc.html)).

## Practical Comparison

### Public healthcare access

**What the evidence says:**  
EU-citizen rules do not apply to you as an American. Non-EU pensioners can join through optional insurance at 8.5% of pension income, after getting a pensioner residence permit and an AMKA.

**What to check next:**  
Ask an EFKA office: "Can I enroll in optional insurance as a third-country pensioner, and what is the current contribution rate?"

### Private hospital access

**What the evidence says:**  
Hygeia Hospital and Athens Medical Center both run international patient services. Neither site posts prices or insurer lists.

**What to check next:**  
Email both international patient desks for a written quote on a consultation, a scan, and a one-night stay.

### Medicare as a fallback

**What the evidence says:**  
Original Medicare usually pays nothing in Greece. At home you face a $1,736 Part A and $283 Part B deductible in 2026.

**What to check next:**  
Call your Medigap insurer and ask whether it pays emergency care abroad and what the limit is.

### Residence permit and insurance

**What the evidence says:**  
Greece’s financially independent person permit is the usual route for non-EU retirees, and private health insurance is typically a condition. Income figures quoted online vary widely by site.

**What to check next:**  
Ask the Greek consulate that serves your state for the current monthly income figure and the minimum insurance coverage in writing.

## The Trade-Off

**What you may gain:**
- Two private hospitals with English-language international patient desks in Athens.
- A Level 1 advisory, the lowest the State Department assigns.
- A route into the public scheme for pensioners who qualify.

**What you may give up:**
- Medicare’s protection, which usually stops at the U.S. border.
- Quick access to public care, since the AMKA and permit come first.
- Certainty about price, since the hospitals post no fees.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can retirees use Greece’s public healthcare system?

Yes, if they qualify. A non-EU pensioner with a pensioner permit and an AMKA can join optional public insurance at 8.5% of pension income. ([argophilia.com](https://www.argophilia.com/news/optional-health-insurance-for-third-country-pensioners-terms-and-procedures/243223))

### Does Medicare cover me in Greece?

Usually not, and your 2026 Part B deductible is $283 at home. Original Medicare covers care abroad only in narrow cases, and Part D does not cover drugs bought outside the U.S. ([medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/travel-outside-the-u.s.))

### Is Greece safe for older travelers?

The State Department rates Greece Level 1, its lowest level, with petty crime common in tourist areas. ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/content/tsg_aem/us/en/home/international-travel/travel-advisories/destination.grc.html))

### Are there private hospitals in Athens that serve foreign patients?

Yes, at least 2 in Athens. Hygeia Hospital holds Joint Commission International accreditation and has an International Patient Service. ([hygeia.gr](https://www.hygeia.gr/en/))

### Do EU healthcare rules apply to American retirees?

No. The official EU contact point covers EU citizens only, with S1 forms and EU-AMKA registration in 5 steps. ([eu-healthcare.eopyy.gov.gr](https://eu-healthcare.eopyy.gov.gr/en/healthcare-in-greece/accessing-health-services-in-greece-for-eu-citizens/moving-to-greece-for-eu-citizens/))
