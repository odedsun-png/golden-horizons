## The Health Rule That Changes the Retirement Math

The key distinction is easy to miss when you first start looking at Colombia: Pensionado visa holders generally cannot enroll in EPS. Resolución 5477 of 2022 instead ties this visa route to qualifying private international health coverage.

That turns the usual question on its head. Rather than asking what an EPS premium covers, you will want to ask which private plan will accept you at your age, what it excludes, and how it works when you need care.

Reported private-plan premiums commonly fall around $60-$400 per person per month. That is a planning range, not a promise. Age, medical history, deductibles, benefit limits, and the particulars of the policy all influence the final quote.

Medicare does not normally pay for routine care in Colombia. Keep Medicare decisions separate from your Colombian coverage plan, and make sure you understand what care your private policy pays for abroad.

The premium gets your attention. The details are what matter on a difficult day.

## What Your Private Policy Needs to Do for You

Before you fall in love with a price, ask how the policy handles an ordinary primary-care appointment and a less ordinary emergency admission. Ask about specialist referrals, diagnostic testing, prescriptions, hospital stays, and emergency transport.

Then ask which facilities can bill the insurer directly. In Bogotá, Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá and Clínica del Country are practical places to begin those conversations. In Medellín, Hospital Pablo Tobón Uribe is another useful starting point.

The question is not simply whether a hospital accepts international patients. It is whether *your specific plan* works there in the way you expect.

Some plans may require you to pay first and seek reimbursement. Others may offer direct billing. That difference can matter far more than a polished brochure or a reassuring sales conversation.

Get the answer in writing before you choose a policy. Private coverage is not an optional flourish for this visa category. It belongs beside the Pensionado application, your housing plans, and your list of doctors and clinics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a foreign retiree on a Pensionado visa enroll in EPS in 2026?

Generally, a Pensionado visa holder should not plan on using EPS. Resolución 5477 of 2022 generally requires qualifying private international health coverage for this visa path. Bring your passport, visa paperwork, and policy wording to the Colombian consulate or an immigration professional before filing your application. (cancilleria.gov.co)

### What is a realistic private-insurance premium for a retiree aged 65 to 70?

Reported 2026 ranges commonly run around $60-$400 per person per month. The difference often reflects age, deductible, outpatient benefits, hospital access, and exclusions rather than a simple “better” or “worse” plan. Ask for a written quote using your exact age, not a generic estimate for someone in their 60s. (earlyretireabroad.com)

### If EPS is unavailable, where can a foreign retiree get care?

Private international insurance is the usual route for Pensionado visa holders, with care arranged through approved hospitals and clinics. In Bogotá, start by checking the plan’s relationship with Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá or Clínica del Country; in Medellín, ask about Hospital Pablo Tobón Uribe. Request the self-pay price for a primary-care visit when you contact each facility, even if you expect to use insurance. (minsalud.gov.co)

### What is the current U.S. travel advisory for Colombia?

The U.S. State Department lists Colombia at Level 3, “Reconsider Travel,” dated March 31, 2026. That does not describe every street or city the same way, but it does make area-specific planning essential for a retiree choosing a home and healthcare providers. Check the advisory again immediately before a scouting trip or move. (travel.state.gov)

### How should I compare two Colombian private-health plans?

Compare at least 2 written quotes side by side, beginning with the monthly premium and deductible. Then look at approved hospitals, emergency care, prescriptions, outpatient visits, pre-existing conditions, and whether the provider bills the insurer directly. A plan near $400 may provide a different set of benefits and limits than one near $60, so ask for the policy schedule rather than relying on a sales summary. (colombiahealthcare.com)
