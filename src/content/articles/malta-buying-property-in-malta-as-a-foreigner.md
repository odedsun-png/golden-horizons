# Malta - Buying Property in Malta as a Foreigner — A Local's Perspective

Malta has seen a 4.7% increase in property prices over the past year, making it one of the more stable real estate markets in Europe. Known for its Mediterranean climate and rich history, Malta has become a magnet for those looking to retire abroad. For anyone considering making the move, understanding the intricacies of purchasing property here is crucial. This article will guide you through what you can expect when buying a home in Malta, from costs to neighborhoods to legalities.

## What You Can Expect to Pay for Property in Sliema

In Sliema, one of Malta's most sought-after neighborhoods, the average price per square meter is around €4,500. This busy area is popular due to its waterfront views, array of amenities, and proximity to Valletta. For a two-bedroom apartment in Sliema, you might be looking at spending between €250,000 and €400,000. If you're considering a more luxurious option with sea views, prices can climb up to €700,000 or more.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/33124330/pexels-photo-33124330.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Sliema’s Tower Road is particularly in demand, offering everything from restaurants to retail shopping along its promenade. While this area provides an active lifestyle, it also comes with its share of noise and traffic, especially during peak tourist seasons. If tranquility is what you seek, you might want to consider properties a few streets back, which are typically quieter and slightly less expensive.

However, buying property in Malta isn't just about the initial purchase price. Expect additional costs like notary fees, which can range from 1% to 3% of the purchase price, and a stamp duty of 5%. These are important figures to factor into your budget as you plan your retirement.

## Healthcare and Residency Requirements

To reside in Malta, foreign property buyers must meet specific criteria. If you're planning to stay for more than three months, you'll need to apply for a residency permit. This requires evidence of adequate financial resources and health insurance. St. James Hospital, located in Sliema, is a reputable facility offering a range of services, and expats need to have private health insurance, as Malta's public healthcare system, while comprehensive, can have long waiting periods for non-residents.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/31997350/pexels-photo-31997350.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

The residency process can be daunting without local guidance. Many expats use services from agencies like Frank Salt or Belair Property, which can assist with both property purchases and handling residency paperwork. While Malta is part of the EU, and thus enjoys standardized healthcare agreements, understanding the private healthcare landscape is vital if you're making Malta your new home.

Moreover, Malta offers the Global Residence Program, which could be a perfect fit for retirees. This program requires a minimum property purchase amount of €275,000 in the south of Malta or Gozo, or €220,000 in other areas, and a minimum annual tax of €15,000. The program is designed to attract financially independent individuals, offering a more streamlined residency process.

## Understanding the Local Culture and Lifestyle

Malta’s culture is influenced by its history as a crossroads of civilizations. English is widely spoken, which eases the transition for many retirees. However, Maltese, a Semitic language with Italian influences, is the national language and used in everyday conversation. Taking a few lessons can go a long way in integrating with the local community.

One common misconception is that Malta is solely a summer destination. While its beaches are a major attraction, Malta's winters, though mild, can be damp and windy. This is something to consider when choosing a property, as older buildings may lack adequate heating or insulation.

The local lifestyle is relaxed but lively, with a strong emphasis on community and family. Markets like the Marsaxlokk Fish Market offer fresh produce and seafood, making it easy to enjoy a Mediterranean diet. This market is not just a place to shop but a social hub where you can meet locals and fellow expats alike.

Driving in Malta can be a challenge due to narrow roads and heavy traffic, especially in popular areas like Sliema and Valletta. Public transport is a viable option, with an extensive bus network connecting most towns and villages. For those looking to explore further, the ferry services to Gozo provide a scenic and stress-free way to travel.

Malta’s festivals, like the annual Carnival or the village festas, are lively experiences that offer insight into the island's traditions. Participation in these events can enrich your experience and help you feel more at home. While Malta offers a peaceful retirement, engaging with its dynamic culture is one of the joys of living here.
