## The Story Behind the Dish

Llapingachos have a rich history rooted in Ecuador's highlands, where potatoes are a staple crop. Originally a humble dish, they have become a popular part of Ecuadorian cuisine. The key ingredients are mashed potatoes mixed with cheese, usually a local variety like queso fresco, and then fried to a golden brown. Traditionally, llapingachos are served with a peanut sauce, known as salsa de maní, which adds a nutty richness. This dish reflects the resourcefulness of Ecuadorian cooking, turning simple ingredients into something deeply satisfying.

The inclusion of cheese in llapingachos is a nod to the Spanish influence on Ecuadorian cuisine. Over time, the recipe has evolved, incorporating local ingredients and flavors, but it remains a cherished dish that showcases the fusion of indigenous and European food traditions. Understanding the history of llapingachos offers insight into the broader food landscape of the region.

## How Locals Eat It

Llapingachos are typically enjoyed as a breakfast or brunch item, though they can be found at any time of day. Locals often pair them with fried eggs, avocado, and chorizo, creating a hearty meal. In many households, llapingachos are a family affair, with everyone pitching in to mash the potatoes and shape the cakes. On the streets, they're a popular snack, served hot from roadside stalls or markets. A common accompaniment is a side of ensalada, a simple salad made with lettuce, tomatoes, and onions, adding freshness to the meal.

## Where to Find It

In Quito, head to the Mercado Central or the La Floresta neighborhood, where you can find vendors serving traditional llapingachos. In Cuenca, the San Francisco Market is a great spot to try these potato cakes.
