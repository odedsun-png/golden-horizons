# Bali - What $2,200 a Month Gets You in Ubud — A Local's Perspective

In Ubud, Bali, $2,200 a month can offer a comfortable lifestyle for retirees seeking a tranquil yet culturally rich environment. With the cost of renting a one-bedroom villa starting at approximately $500 per month, the remaining budget allows for dining, healthcare, and leisure activities. This article will help you decide if Ubud is the right fit for your retirement plans, considering both the benefits and limitations of living in this unique Balinese town.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want a peaceful, culturally immersive experience.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to spend around $2,200 for housing, food, healthcare, and leisure.
- **Biggest advantage:** Affordable cost of living with access to nature and culture.
- **Biggest warning:** Healthcare quality and availability can vary.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Housing Costs and Options in Ubud

In Ubud, the cost of housing is a significant draw for retirees. A one-bedroom villa typically costs between $500 and $800 per month, depending on the location and amenities. Areas like Penestanan and Sayan offer traditional Balinese architecture with modern conveniences, making them popular choices for expats. These neighborhoods provide a serene environment with easy access to Ubud's active cultural scene.

However, be aware of the limitations. Rental contracts can be complex, often requiring a year's rent upfront. Additionally, utilities such as electricity and internet are usually not included in the rent, adding around $100 to $150 to your monthly expenses. Thoroughly read and understand any rental agreements and consider hiring a local agent to assist with negotiations.

## Daily Living Expenses in Ubud

Groceries and dining out are generally affordable in Ubud. A typical meal at a local warung (small restaurant) costs about $3, while a dinner at a mid-range restaurant might set you back $10 to $15. For those who enjoy cooking, fresh produce is available at the Ubud Market, where a week's worth of fruits and vegetables can cost around $20.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1449480881392-716d0ea24a47?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8SW5kb25lc2lhfGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODEzMzk5Njd8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Transportation is another essential aspect to consider. Many retirees opt for renting a scooter, which costs approximately $50 per month, as it offers the flexibility to explore the area easily. However, handling the narrow and often busy roads can be challenging for those not accustomed to riding scooters. Alternatively, local taxis and ride-sharing services like Grab are available but can become costly if used frequently.

## Healthcare and Safety Considerations

Healthcare facilities in Ubud are improving, but they may not meet the standards some retirees are accustomed to. The Ubud Clinic and Ari Canti Hospital are the primary healthcare providers in the area, offering basic medical services. For more specialized care, a trip to Denpasar, about an hour and a half away, may be necessary.

Private health insurance is highly recommended to cover potential medical expenses and ensure access to better facilities. Safety in Ubud is generally good, with low crime rates, but it's wise to take standard precautions, such as securing your home and being mindful of your belongings in crowded areas.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Before moving to Ubud, ensure you understand the healthcare system and have appropriate private insurance. The Indonesian government requires all foreigners to have health insurance, and verify what is covered under your plan. Visa rules can be complex; retirees often opt for the Retirement Visa, which requires proof of pension and health insurance, among other criteria.

Tax obligations for U.S. citizens living abroad can be intricate. Consult with a tax professional familiar with both U.S. and Indonesian tax laws to avoid any pitfalls. Safety in Ubud is generally reassuring, but always verify the walkability and emergency access in your chosen neighborhood.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Overlooking the complexity of rental agreements.
- Underestimating the cost and availability of healthcare.
- Failing to secure adequate private health insurance.
- Ignoring visa requirements and renewal processes.
- Not budgeting for transportation costs.
- Assuming all areas have the same level of safety.
- Neglecting to consult tax professionals for dual tax obligations.

## Before You Move Checklist

- Verify visa or residency rules and apply for the Retirement Visa.
- Confirm healthcare access and secure private health insurance.
- Understand your tax obligations in both the U.S. and Indonesia.
- Review and negotiate rental agreements carefully.
- Assess walkability and transportation options in your neighborhood.
- Research safety and emergency access in your area.
- Prepare for language barriers and consider learning basic Indonesian.
- Plan for potential cultural adjustments and lifestyle changes.
