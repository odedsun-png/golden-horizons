## Quick Taste

Pescado zarandeado is a whole fish, often red snapper or snook, marinated in chili, lime and garlic, then grilled over an open fire. It tastes smoky and savory with a little citrus, and it is usually eaten with warm tortillas and lime. ## The Story Behind the Dish

Pescado Zarandeado has its roots in the coastal regions of Nayarit and Sinaloa, where fishing is a way of life. The term "zarandeado" refers to the method of grilling the fish on a zaranda, a type of grill basket that allows the fish to be flipped easily over an open fire. The marinade, often a closely guarded family recipe, varies slightly from one household to another, adding a personal touch to this cherished dish.

## How Locals Eat It

Locals typically enjoy Pescado Zarandeado as a communal meal, often at beachside palapas where the fish is cooked fresh from the sea. It's common to see families and friends gathered around a table, sharing a whole fish and passing around bowls of salsa, chopped onions, and cilantro. The fish is usually served with corn tortillas, allowing diners to make their own tacos, and is often accompanied by a cold beer or a refreshing agua fresca. Eating Pescado Zarandeado is as much about the social experience as it is about the food, with laughter and conversation enhancing the flavors of the meal.

## Where to Find It

While Pescado Zarandeado can be found in upscale restaurants throughout Mexico, the most traditional experience is at a local beachside eatery. Look for beachside restaurants in Nayarit and Sinaloa, including the Mazatlán area. Ask a local host which place they go to, and eat early in the day when the catch is freshest.

## Regional Variations

Though Pescado Zarandeado is most closely associated with Nayarit and Sinaloa, variations exist along the Pacific Coast. Cooks may use different fish depending on the catch of the day, and each cook has their own marinade. These regional differences make Pescado Zarandeado a dish that reflects the diversity and creativity of Mexican coastal cuisine.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before diving into Pescado Zarandeado, it's helpful to know that this dish is all about balance. The smoky flavor from the grill complements the tangy marinade, creating a harmonious taste that is both bold and refreshing. Be prepared for a hands-on dining experience, as you'll likely be using your hands to pull apart the tender fish and fill your tortillas. If you're making it at home, ensure your grill is hot enough to achieve that signature charred flavor. And remember, the best Pescado Zarandeado is made with fresh, local fish, so ask where the fish came from.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is expecting Pescado Zarandeado to be a quick meal. The grilling process takes time, and rushing it can result in a less flavorful dish. Patience is key to achieving the perfect balance of smokiness and tenderness. Another mistake is overlooking the importance of the marinade. Some visitors might skip this step when cooking at home, but the marinade is essential for infusing the fish with its characteristic flavor. Finally, tourists often stick to touristy areas, missing out on the traditional experience found in local beachside eateries where the dish is prepared with care and tradition.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What type of fish is best for Pescado Zarandeado?

Red snapper and snook are the fish most often used. The cook will use what the day's catch offers, so ask.

### Can I make Pescado Zarandeado without a grill?

While a grill is ideal for achieving the smoky flavor, you can replicate the dish using an oven with a broiler setting. Just be sure to watch the fish closely to prevent it from drying out.

### How spicy is Pescado Zarandeado?

The level of spiciness in Pescado Zarandeado can vary. The marinade typically includes chilies, but you can adjust the amount to suit your taste. If you're sensitive to spice, ask for a milder version when ordering.

### Is Pescado Zarandeado gluten-free?

The fish and corn tortillas do not contain gluten, but marinades vary by cook. If you avoid gluten, ask what is in the marinade.
