# What $2,100 a Month Could Buy in Dumaguete, Philippines (A Practical Reality Check)

**Golden Horizons Status:** 🟡 WORTH TESTING — VERIFY COSTS

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** A $2,100 monthly budget appears more than sufficient for a modest Dumaguete lifestyle based on documented rent, food, utilities, transit, and occasional dining estimates. A one-bedroom averages about $278 outside the center or $298 in the center, while the documented core monthly budget range is about $616–$796. That calculation excludes private healthcare, insurance, taxes, flights, and residency-related costs.

## What Everyday Life Has to Cover

A cappuccino at about $2.36 and a local transit ride at about $0.22 are pleasant details. But retirement is built around the less glamorous rhythm of rent, grocery runs, utility bills, medical planning, and the question of whether you can get through an ordinary month without watching every receipt.

On the available Dumaguete estimates, the basics fit comfortably. A one-bedroom apartment averages about $298 per month in the city center and about $278 outside the center. These are citywide averages, not quotes for a particular furnished apartment or lease term—but the narrow difference suggests you may be better served by choosing for access to everyday services than by chasing the last few dollars of rent savings.

Food prices help keep the monthly math gentle. A liter of milk is about $1.73, a dozen eggs about $1.83, a pound of chicken about $1.58, and a pound of potatoes about $1.00. A modest solo grocery allowance of roughly $150–$250 can work, depending on how much you cook at home and how closely your preferred foods resemble a local grocery basket.

Utilities are the line item worth watching. Basic utilities for a larger apartment average about $95 monthly, with broadband around $26.80 and a mobile plan around $9.90. A monthly public-transport pass averages about $11.60, making a car-free routine much easier to contemplate if your daily needs stay within town.

## Healthcare and Residency: The Costs You Need to Price Yourself

Dumaguete has private healthcare options, including Holy Child Hospital and ACE Dumaguete Doctors Hospital. Private care is commonly part of expatriate life, and private insurance is commonly part of the planning picture.

The important call is not simply whether a hospital exists nearby. It is whether the facility, payment process, insurance arrangement, and services fit your own needs. Ask hospitals for current self-pay pricing for the care you expect to use, and ask insurers exactly what a policy covers in Dumaguete before treating low rent as a complete affordability answer.

The Philippines offers long-stay and retirement-oriented residence arrangements, including the Special Resident Retiree’s Visa route and other visa options. But a manageable grocery bill and permission to remain are separate matters. Confirm the route that fits your situation directly with the Philippine Retirement Authority or the relevant immigration authority, including current documents, fees, financial requirements, and insurance expectations.

## The Trade-Off

Dumaguete’s appeal is easy to see in the daily rhythm: modest rents, inexpensive meals, low-cost transportation, and a medium-sized city pace that can feel refreshingly manageable.

The trade-off is less visible until you need it. High-end healthcare options are more limited than in major metropolitan areas, English-speaking staff may vary outside major hospitals, and expatriate services are more concentrated around hospital districts. The rent may be easy. Building a healthcare plan you trust is the work.
