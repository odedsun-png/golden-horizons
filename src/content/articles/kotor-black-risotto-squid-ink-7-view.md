## The Story Behind the Dish

Black risotto, known locally as "crni rižot," is a staple of Montenegrin coastal cuisine, deeply rooted in the Adriatic's maritime tradition. This dish is a celebration of the sea, primarily featuring squid ink, which lends the risotto its signature black hue and a distinctive briny taste. The addition of Adriatic mussels and other seafood like cuttlefish or prawns enhances its maritime essence. Historically, black risotto was a humble fisherman's meal, utilizing readily available ingredients from the day's catch. Today, it stands as a symbol of Kotor's food heritage, reflecting the region's reliance on the sea for sustenance and flavor.

The popularity of black risotto has grown over the years, attracting food enthusiasts from around the world who are eager to taste this intriguing blend of flavors. Kotor's version of black risotto is particularly renowned for its use of fresh, locally sourced ingredients. The Adriatic mussels, harvested from nearby waters, are known for their plumpness and sweetness, which perfectly complement the earthy, umami notes of the squid ink. This combination not only highlights the region's food traditions but also emphasizes the importance of sustainable fishing practices that have been passed down through generations.

The dish's rich flavor profile comes from squid ink, which imparts a distinctive taste that may not appeal to everyone. It's not overly spicy, but the natural saltiness of the seafood is prominent. For those sensitive to salt, requesting a lighter seasoning may be advisable. The freshness of ingredients is generally reliable in Kotor, especially in reputable restaurants and markets. However, visitors should always choose dining venues that prioritize hygiene and quality.

Before diving into a plate of black risotto, it's important to be aware of a few key details.
