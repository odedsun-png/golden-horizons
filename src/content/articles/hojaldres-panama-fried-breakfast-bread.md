## Quick Taste

Picture yourself in a lively Panamanian café as the morning sun streams through the windows, casting warm light on the golden hojaldres piled high on your plate. These are not your average breakfast breads; they’re crisp on the outside with a soft, chewy interior that begs for a bite. Hojaldres, Panama’s fried bread, are typically enjoyed with a sprinkle of powdered sugar or a dollop of savory cheese, making them a versatile morning treat. Here, you’ll find them sold for about $1 to $2 a piece, offering an affordable way to enjoy a piece of local culture as you sip your morning coffee and watch the city come alive.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Hojaldres have a long history in Panama, tracing back to the country's colonial past when Spanish culinary influences melded with local ingredients and techniques. This simple yet satisfying bread is made from a dough of flour, water, and a pinch of salt, which is then deep-fried to perfection. Traditionally, Panamanians have served hojaldres as a breakfast staple, but their popularity has grown, and they now appear at various meals throughout the day. This humble dish reflects Panama’s cultural blend, where European and indigenous influences meet, creating something distinctly Panamanian.

## How Locals Eat It

In Panama, hojaldres are a breakfast favorite, often paired with a steaming cup of coffee or hot chocolate. Locals might enjoy them sweet, with a dusting of sugar, or savory, topped with cheese or accompanied by scrambled eggs and beans. You’ll find that many Panamanians enjoy hojaldres as part of a larger breakfast spread, sharing the table with dishes like chorizo, plantains, and fresh fruit. For a true local experience, try ordering hojaldres with a side of queso blanco, a mild white cheese that complements the bread’s crisp texture perfectly.

## Where to Find It

For the best hojaldres, head to Panama City’s local markets and street stalls. Mercado de Mariscos is a popular spot where vendors serve freshly fried hojaldres alongside seafood delicacies. In the neighborhood of Casco Viejo, you’ll find small cafes and street vendors offering hojaldres as part of their breakfast menu. For those staying longer, supermarkets like Riba Smith and El Rey sell ready-to-fry dough, allowing you to make hojaldres at home. This is a great option if you’re considering extended stays in Panama and want to integrate local flavors into your daily routine.

## Regional Variations

While hojaldres are a staple across Panama, regional variations add a twist to this simple bread. In the Chiriquí province, hojaldres might be served with a side of local honey, adding a sweet note to the savory dough. In contrast, the Azuero Peninsula is known for its savory take, often incorporating herbs and spices into the dough before frying. These subtle differences reflect the diverse food landscape of Panama, where each region adds its own flair to traditional dishes, making every bite a new experience.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

Hojaldres are an economical choice for breakfast, typically costing between $1 and $2 each. If you’re dining in a local café, expect to pay a bit more, around $3 to $5, especially in tourist-heavy areas. Portions are generous, with one or two hojaldres usually enough to satisfy most appetites. When ordering, don’t hesitate to ask for a side of cheese or eggs to round out your meal. If you’re buying dough from a supermarket, a pack sufficient for several servings will cost about $5, making it a cost-effective option for those planning to cook at home.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before diving into a plate of hojaldres, it’s worth noting that these treats are best enjoyed fresh and hot. The texture is at its peak right after frying, offering a delightful contrast between the crispy exterior and soft interior. If you’re trying them from a street vendor, look for stalls with a steady stream of locals, as this usually indicates freshness and quality. Also, don’t be shy about experimenting with toppings; locals often add their own twist, whether sweet or savory, to personalize their breakfast experience.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is assuming that all hojaldres are created equal. In reality, the quality can vary significantly between vendors and locations. Another error is eating them cold, which diminishes the bread’s signature texture. To avoid disappointment, seek out busy vendors where the turnover is high, ensuring you get hojaldres at their freshest. Lastly, some visitors miss out on the full experience by not trying different toppings or accompaniments, so be adventurous and explore the local options.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are hojaldres made of?
Hojaldres are made from a simple dough of flour, water, salt, and sometimes a touch of sugar. The dough is then rolled out and fried until golden brown.

### Are hojaldres sweet or savory?
Hojaldres can be both! They are versatile and can be enjoyed with sweet toppings like sugar or honey, or savory accompaniments like cheese or eggs.

### Where can I buy hojaldres in Panama City?
You can find hojaldres at local markets, street vendors, and cafes throughout Panama City. They are especially popular in areas like Casco Viejo and Mercado de Mariscos.

### Can I make hojaldres at home?
Yes, you can make hojaldres at home. Many supermarkets in Panama sell ready-to-fry dough, making it easy to prepare this dish in your own kitchen.
