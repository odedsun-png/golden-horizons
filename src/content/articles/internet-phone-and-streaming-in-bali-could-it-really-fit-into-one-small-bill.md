# Internet, Phone, and Streaming in Bali: Could One Modest Monthly Bill Cover a Retiree’s Life in Denpasar?

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Internet in Denpasar is about $19 a month. A furnished studio in a normal area starts around $443, while a larger place in an expensive area reaches about $1,318. A planning range for rent, food, utilities, transport, and basic dining is roughly $2,009-$2,900 a month, before visa and healthcare costs. A cappuccino runs about $2.38, and a casual lunch is about $8.50.

A home internet bill of about $19 is the number that catches your eye. It is easy to picture dependable Wi-Fi, calls home, and a quiet evening in without much strain on the budget.

But the internet bill is only the smallest piece of the Bali question. The retirement decision is really about what happens after the coffee, the taxi ride, and the modest dinner: where you rent, how much air-conditioning you use, how you handle healthcare, and what it takes to stay legally. In Denpasar, the everyday prices can feel reassuringly light. The full monthly picture is more complicated—and more useful.

## What the Denpasar Price Board Really Means for Your Month

The housing choice sets the tone. A furnished 45 m2 studio in a normal Denpasar area is about $443 monthly. Move up to a furnished 85 m2 home in a normal area and the reference point becomes about $849; an 85 m2 furnished place in an expensive area reaches about $1,318.

That is the difference between having room in the budget and watching every other choice more closely. Denpasar offers the lower-cost baseline in this research. Seminyak, Canggu, and Sanur are higher-demand expat hubs, where the same retirement income can feel tighter before you have bought a single grocery.

Groceries deserve their own reality check. Expatistan’s single-person monthly estimate is about $1,440—a figure worth testing against your own habits rather than accepting blindly. The same September 2026 data lists about $1.36 for 1 liter of milk, $1.63 for 12 eggs, $1.19 for 1 kg of tomatoes, and $7.44 for 0.5 kg of local cheese.

Apples are about $2.38 per kg, and a carton of beer is about $2.91. These small purchases can still add up when you shop near the more expensive expat areas.

Then there is the bill people tend to overlook until they arrive: electricity. A one-person furnished studio’s monthly utilities are about $30, while utilities for 2 people in an 85 m2 home are about $73. The listed 8 Mbps internet service is about $19. The difference is not abstract—apartment size and air-conditioning habits can change how modest that “small bill” feels.

Getting around can be comparatively light if local transport works for your routine. A month of public transport is about $23, while an 8 km taxi trip is about $4.45. Gasoline is about $0.61 per liter.

Dinner carries the same neighborhood divide as rent: about $14.40 for a modest neighborhood dinner for 2, or about $36 for dinner for 2 at an Italian restaurant in an expat-oriented area. A basic lunch is about $8.50, and a cappuccino is about $2.38.

## The Questions That Begin After You Find a Rental

Indonesia offers KITAS-type paths that retirees may explore. The research does not provide a confirmed income threshold, savings minimum, fee, approval timeline, or document checklist for a retiree application.

Before making a long-term deposit, contact an Indonesian embassy or consulate and ask for the current rules in writing. Ask which documents apply to your visa route, how long the process is taking, what renewals cost, and whether your planned income and savings meet the current standard.

Healthcare is a separate budget line as well. The research supports the general point that foreigners use private and public options, but it does not provide verified prices for a private initial visit, insurance premium, prescription, imaging scan, or hospital care.

The useful first check is practical rather than theoretical: map a 10-15 km route from your prospective Denpasar rental to a clinic, ask for a cash quote for a first appointment, and learn what private coverage would cost for your age. That one errand can tell you more than a dozen sunny online videos.

For Americans, Medicare is not a substitute for a plan abroad. Before leaving the United States for more than a short visit, confirm how you would pay for routine care in Bali and what coverage you would have when returning home.

The research memo also does not establish the current U.S.-Indonesia tax-treaty position for retirees. Ask a U.S. tax professional who works with Americans abroad before relying on any tax assumption.

## The Golden Horizons 30-Day Test

1. Compare 2-3 furnished one-bedroom listings in normal Denpasar areas against 1-2 listings in Seminyak or Canggu, using the $443-$1,318 rent range only as a starting point.

2. Buy milk, 12 eggs, tomatoes, apples, and local cheese from a Denpasar market for 30 days, keeping every receipt to see whether the $1,440 grocery estimate resembles your own habits.
