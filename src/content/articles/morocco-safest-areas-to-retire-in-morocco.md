# Morocco - Safest Areas to Retire in Morocco — A Local's Perspective

Morocco, a North African country with a population of over 36 million, offers a variety of traditional and modern lifestyles. While the crime rate in Morocco is relatively low, safety can vary significantly between regions. This article will help you decide which areas in Morocco are the safest for retirement, considering factors such as local amenities, community atmosphere, and accessibility.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want a culturally rich lifestyle with a focus on safety.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to budget around $1,500 to $2,000 for comfortable living, including rent, utilities, and groceries.
- **Biggest advantage:** Affordable cost of living with a diverse cultural experience.
- **Biggest warning:** Language barriers can be a challenge; French and Arabic are primarily spoken.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Rabat: The Capital City Advantage

Rabat, Morocco's capital, is known for its safety and organized urban planning. With a population of approximately 580,000, it offers a range of amenities including international schools, modern hospitals, and efficient public transport. The Hassan neighborhood is particularly popular for its calm environment and proximity to key services.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/flagged/photo-1555169048-3c4845cfcf1c?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwxMXx8TW9yb2Njb3xlbnwwfDB8fHwxNzc4MTc1NzExfDA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Rabat boasts a well-developed healthcare system, with facilities such as the Cheikh Zaid Hospital offering high-quality medical care. Public transportation, including buses and taxis, is reliable, making it easy to get around the city without a car. A significant advantage of living in Rabat is its political stability, as it's home to many diplomatic missions and government offices.

However, the cost of living in Rabat can be higher compared to other Moroccan cities, especially in terms of housing. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in a safe neighborhood like Agdal can range from $500 to $800 per month. Despite this, Rabat remains a top choice for retirees seeking a balance between safety and urban amenities.

## Essaouira: Coastal Tranquility

Essaouira, a coastal city with a population of around 78,000, is famed for its relaxed atmosphere and historic medina. The city's manageable size and friendly community make it an appealing option for retirees. The medina, a UNESCO World Heritage site, is pedestrian-friendly and offers a variety of local shops and cafes.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1538050558691-dfaad62c8ea0?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8TW9yb2Njb3xlbnwwfDB8fHwxNzc4MTc1NzExfDA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Healthcare in Essaouira is more limited compared to larger cities, but there are several clinics and a regional hospital providing basic services. For more specialized care, residents often travel to Marrakech, approximately 2.5 hours away by car. Public transportation within Essaouira is limited, but taxis are affordable and widely available.

One practical warning for retirees considering Essaouira is the strong coastal winds, especially during the spring months. While these winds are part of the city's appeal, they can be a nuisance for some. Housing is generally affordable, with rental prices for a one-bedroom apartment in the medina starting at around $300 per month.

## Fez: Cultural Heritage and Safety

Fez, known as Morocco's cultural capital, has a population of over 1.1 million and is renowned for its historical significance and architecture. The city is divided into two main areas: the old medina (Fez el-Bali) and the new city (Ville Nouvelle). While the medina is a busy center of activity, the Ville Nouvelle offers a more modern and organized environment.

Safety in Fez is generally good, particularly in the Ville Nouvelle, where many expatriates choose to live. The city is home to the University Hospital Hassan II, which provides a range of medical services. Public transportation is available, but many residents prefer using taxis or private cars due to the city's complex layout.

A common assumption is that living in the medina might be overwhelming due to its labyrinthine streets and dense population. However, many retirees find its active culture and community spirit rewarding. Housing in Fez is affordable, with rents in the Ville Nouvelle for a one-bedroom apartment ranging from $250 to $500 per month.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Before making the move to Morocco, verify healthcare access and the need for private insurance. While cities like Rabat offer modern medical facilities, smaller towns may require travel for specialized care. U.S. retirees should consult with a healthcare advisor to understand their insurance needs.

Visa and residency rules for Morocco can be complex. U.S. citizens typically receive a 90-day tourist visa upon arrival, but long-term residency requires additional documentation. Consult with an immigration expert to understand these requirements.

Tax obligations can also be intricate, with potential implications both in Morocco and the United States. Retirees should seek advice from a tax professional familiar with both U.S. and Moroccan tax systems. Safety and walkability vary by region, so visiting potential areas and understanding local conditions is recommended.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Underestimating the language barrier; learning basic French or Arabic can be beneficial.
- Assuming all healthcare facilities offer the same level of care; check local options.
- Overlooking the need for a local bank account for day-to-day transactions.
- Ignoring the importance of understanding local customs and traditions.
- Failing to verify the specifics of rental agreements and lease terms.
- Assuming all areas have the same level of safety; research neighborhoods thoroughly.
- Neglecting to consider climate differences, especially in coastal and inland areas.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the cost of living in Morocco for retirees?

The cost of living in Morocco is relatively affordable, with monthly expenses ranging from $1,500 to $2,000, depending on lifestyle and location. Rent, utilities, and groceries are generally less expensive than in the U.S.

### Is healthcare accessible in Morocco?

Healthcare in Morocco varies by region. While cities like Rabat have modern hospitals, smaller towns may offer limited services. Verify healthcare access and consider private insurance.

### Are there language barriers in Morocco?

Yes, French and Arabic are the primary languages spoken in Morocco. English may be understood in tourist areas, but learning basic French or Arabic can enhance daily interactions.
