## The Planning-Budget Reality

Social Security payments can continue outside the U.S., but the purchasing power of these payments varies significantly by destination. Housing, groceries, utilities, and healthcare costs are major factors influencing whether SSA income can support a comfortable retirement abroad. It's crucial to evaluate these costs in specific locations rather than relying solely on exchange rates.

## The Residency Reality

Residency and visa eligibility are determined by the destination country's immigration rules, not by the SSA. Prospective retirees should consult official government sources for each location to understand the requirements for long-term stays, including income thresholds and other conditions.

## Practical Comparison

When considering retirement abroad on Social Security income, it's important to compare potential destinations based on cost of living, residency requirements, and healthcare access. Some countries may offer lower housing costs but have stringent residency rules, while others might provide easier residency paths but higher daily expenses. Evaluating these factors in specific locations will help determine the feasibility of retiring abroad on SSA income.

## Who This May Fit

**Worth testing if:**
- You are open to exploring different countries for retirement.
- You can adapt to varying local costs and residency requirements.
- You are prepared to arrange private healthcare coverage abroad.

**May not fit if:**
- You expect SSA income alone to cover all expenses without testing.
- You are unwilling to navigate complex residency rules.
- You rely heavily on Medicare for healthcare coverage.

## The Trade-Off

**What you may gain:**
- Potentially lower living costs in some destinations.
- New cultural experiences and lifestyle changes.
- Opportunities to stretch SSA income further in certain locations.

**What you may give up:**
- Familiarity with U.S. healthcare and Medicare coverage.
- Ease of residency and visa processes.
- Consistent cost of living and service availability.

## The Golden Horizons 30-Day Test

1. Use the SSA Payments Abroad Screening Tool to identify eligible destinations for your benefit amount; document results for at least two countries.
2. Map the destination country’s residency/visa path from official sources; list required income thresholds and restrictions.
3. Build a 1–2 month budget for each destination focusing on housing, groceries, utilities, and transportation; exclude healthcare costs initially.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I continue to receive Social Security benefits if I live abroad?**  
In many cases yes, but it depends on country rules; use the SSA Payments Abroad Screening Tool to confirm for your country. ([ssa.gov](https://www.ssa.gov/international/payments_outsideUS.html?utm_source=openai))

**Does Medicare pay for healthcare outside the U.S.?**  
Medicare generally provides limited coverage outside the U.S., with specific exceptions; most foreign healthcare is not covered by standard Medicare; consider private or local options. ([medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/travel-outside-the-u.s.?utm_source=openai))

**What determines whether I can stay long-term in a destination?**  
Residency/visa eligibility is determined by the destination country’s immigration rules, not SSA; consult official government sources for the destination. ([usa.gov](https://www.usa.gov/social-security-abroad?utm_source=openai))

**How should I think about the cost of living abroad for a fixed Social Security income?**  
Purchasing power varies; housing and healthcare costs are major leverage points; there is no universal answer. ([retirefinder.com](https://retirefinder.com/retire-abroad/cost-of-living/?utm_source=openai))
