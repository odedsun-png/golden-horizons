# Italy - Best Cities for Retirees: Lecce vs Florence — A Local's Perspective

Florence, with a population of around 380,000, is a cultural and historical center, while Lecce, known as the "Florence of the South," has a population of about 95,000. Both cities offer unique experiences for retirees considering a move to Italy, each with its own set of advantages and challenges. This article will help you weigh the benefits and limitations of retiring in either Lecce or Florence.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want cultural immersion and historical appeal.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to budget around $2,500 in Florence and $1,800 in Lecce for a comfortable lifestyle.
- **Biggest advantage:** Florence offers world-class art and museums, while Lecce provides a more relaxed pace with a lower cost of living.
- **Biggest warning:** Florence can be crowded and expensive, especially in peak tourist seasons; Lecce might lack some modern conveniences.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Cost of Living in Florence

In Florence, the average rental cost for a one-bedroom apartment in the city center is approximately €1,200 per month. Groceries and dining out can add another €400–€600 monthly, depending on your lifestyle. The city is well-known for its art and culture, with numerous galleries and events that can fill your days.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1552598715-7eeb9232a2ac?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwxMXx8SXRhbHl8ZW58MHwwfHx8MTc3ODE3OTU4MHww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

The Mercato Centrale is a food lover's paradise, offering fresh produce and local specialties. However, Florence can be costly, especially during the tourist season when prices tend to rise. Public transportation is efficient, with buses and trams covering most of the city, but owning a car is not recommended due to limited parking and traffic congestion.

## Lifestyle and Housing in Lecce

Lecce offers a more affordable lifestyle, with one-bedroom apartments in the city center averaging around €500–€700 per month. The cost of dining out is generally lower than in Florence, with meals at local trattorias costing around €10–€15. Lecce's historic center, with its Baroque architecture, provides a beautiful backdrop for daily life.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1551801319-ca06060f3fcc?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8SXRhbHl8ZW58MHwwfHx8MTc3ODIyOTU3N3ww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

The city is less crowded than Florence, offering a quieter, more relaxed atmosphere. However, public transportation options are limited compared to Florence, so a car might be necessary for exploring the surrounding areas. Be prepared for the slower pace of life and the fact that English is less commonly spoken here.

## Healthcare and Accessibility in Both Cities

Florence boasts several hospitals and clinics, including the renowned Careggi University Hospital, which provides comprehensive healthcare services. In contrast, Lecce's healthcare facilities are smaller but still adequate, with the Vito Fazzi Hospital serving as the main medical center.

Access to healthcare is generally good in both cities, but having private health insurance to cover any gaps in the public system is crucial. Florence's international community means more English-speaking doctors, whereas in Lecce, you might need to learn some Italian to communicate effectively with healthcare providers.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Before moving to Italy, ensure you understand the healthcare system. While EU citizens receive some healthcare benefits, non-EU retirees should have comprehensive private health insurance. Verify the visa requirements, as they can be complex, and ensure you have the proper residency permits.

Tax obligations can be intricate, particularly if you have income from the U.S. and Italy. Consult a tax professional to understand your liabilities in both countries. Safety is generally good in both cities, but always remain aware of your surroundings, especially in tourist-heavy areas of Florence.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Underestimating the cost of living in Florence during peak tourist seasons.
- Failing to learn basic Italian, especially in Lecce, where English is less common.
- Not securing comprehensive health insurance before moving.
- Overlooking the importance of understanding local customs and traditions.
- Assuming all areas have the same level of amenities and infrastructure.
- Ignoring the need for a car in Lecce due to limited public transport.
- Not seeking professional advice on tax and residency issues.

## Before You Move Checklist

- Verify visa or residency rules for non-EU citizens.
- Ensure access to healthcare facilities and consider private insurance.
- Understand your tax obligations in both the U.S. and Italy.
- Check rental agreements and verify lease terms.
- Assess walkability and transportation options in your chosen city.
- Evaluate safety and emergency access in your neighborhood.
- Learn basic Italian to ease communication.
- Plan for seasonal cost fluctuations, especially in Florence.
- Research local customs and integrate into the community.
- Consider the need for a vehicle in Lecce.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it easy to find English-speaking doctors in Lecce?

While Lecce has adequate healthcare facilities, English-speaking doctors are less common than in Florence. Learning basic Italian can be beneficial for communication.

### How does the cost of living compare between Florence and Lecce?

Florence is generally more expensive, especially for housing and dining. Lecce offers a lower cost of living but may lack some modern conveniences.

### What is the best way to get around in Florence?

Florence has an efficient public transportation system, including buses and trams. Due to traffic and parking challenges, owning a car is not recommended.
