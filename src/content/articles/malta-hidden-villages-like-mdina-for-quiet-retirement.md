# Malta - Hidden Villages Like Mdina for Quiet Retirement — A Local's Perspective

Mdina, Malta's former capital, is a city with a population of just about 300 residents. Known as the "Silent City," its narrow, cobbled streets and ancient architecture offer a peaceful atmosphere that is rare in modern urban environments. For retirees seeking tranquility and a slower pace of life, Mdina and similar villages in Malta present a unique opportunity. This article will guide you through what living in this serene setting entails and whether it might be the right choice for your retirement.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want a quiet and historic environment.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to spend around $2,500 for a modest lifestyle, including rent, groceries, and healthcare.
- **Biggest advantage:** The peaceful atmosphere and close-knit community.
- **Biggest warning:** Limited modern amenities and fewer entertainment options.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Life in Mdina

Mdina's history and architecture, with buildings dating back to the medieval period, are notable. The city's pedestrian-only policy means cars are left at the gates, contributing to its nickname, the "Silent City." This makes it an ideal location for those who enjoy walking and exploring on foot. The local shops and cafes are within easy reach, but Mdina lacks large supermarkets, so you'll need to visit nearby Rabat for more extensive shopping needs.

The housing market in Mdina is limited due to its small size and historical significance. Properties available for rent or purchase are often older and may require renovation. Monthly rent for a small apartment can range from €700 to €1,200, depending on the location and condition. While this might seem high for a small town, the unique setting and tranquility often justify the cost for those seeking a peaceful retirement.

One limitation is the lack of modern conveniences. Mdina's infrastructure is not designed for large-scale development, so amenities like high-speed internet and central heating can be inconsistent. Check the specifics of any property before committing to ensure it meets your needs.

## Healthcare and Daily Life in Malta

Healthcare in Malta is known for its high standards, with both public and private options available. The closest major hospital to Mdina is Mater Dei Hospital in Msida, approximately a 20-minute drive away. For routine check-ups and minor ailments, the local clinics in Rabat can provide adequate care. Have private health insurance to cover any gaps in the public system, especially for non-EU residents.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1571673478358-69718ba173c4?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8TWFsdGF8ZW58MHwwfHx8MTc4MDMwMzIyMnww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Public transportation in Malta is reliable, with buses connecting Mdina to other parts of the island. However, services can be infrequent, especially on weekends and holidays. Owning a car might be beneficial for those who want more flexibility, although parking can be challenging within Mdina itself.

A practical warning for retirees considering Mdina is the limited entertainment and social scene. While the city offers a cultural experience with its historical sites and occasional festivals, those looking for active nightlife or diverse dining options might find it lacking. For such activities, traveling to Valletta or Sliema would be necessary.

## Nearby Villages

While Mdina offers a retirement setting, other nearby villages like Rabat and Mosta also provide appealing options. Rabat, located just outside Mdina's walls, offers more modern amenities while still maintaining a quiet atmosphere. The village is home to the famous St. Paul's Catacombs and offers a wider range of housing options, often at more affordable prices than Mdina.

Mosta, known for its impressive Rotunda, offers a larger town feel with more shopping and dining options. It's still quieter than the busy tourist areas of Malta, making it a good compromise for those who want access to amenities without sacrificing peace.

A common assumption is that all of Malta is crowded and tourist-heavy, but these villages prove otherwise. They offer a slower pace and a chance to integrate into local life, which can be more rewarding than living in more tourist-centric areas. However, understand that language barriers can exist, as Maltese is the primary language spoken, with English widely understood but not always used in everyday interactions.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Before moving to Malta, verify healthcare access and private insurance requirements. While Malta offers a robust public healthcare system, non-EU retirees should ensure they have adequate private coverage. Visa regulations can vary, so consulting with a qualified professional or the Maltese consulate is recommended.

Tax obligations for U.S. citizens retiring in Malta can be complex. Understand both U.S. and Maltese tax laws to avoid unexpected liabilities. Safety in Mdina and surrounding villages is generally high, but familiarize yourself with local emergency services and ensure your home has necessary safety features like smoke detectors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is English widely spoken in Mdina?

Yes, English is widely understood in Malta, including Mdina, but Maltese is the primary language. It's helpful to learn basic Maltese phrases for everyday interactions.

### What is the healthcare quality like in Malta?

Malta offers high-quality healthcare with both public and private options. Non-EU residents should have private insurance to cover any gaps.

### How do I get around in Mdina without a car?

Mdina is pedestrian-friendly, and public buses connect it to other parts of Malta. However, a car can offer more flexibility, especially for travel outside the city.

### What are the main cultural activities in Mdina?

Mdina hosts several cultural events throughout the year, including medieval festivals and art exhibitions. The city itself is rich in history, offering plenty of opportunities to learn about the past.

### Are there any modern conveniences in Mdina?

While Mdina maintains its historical appeal, some modern conveniences like high-speed internet can be inconsistent. Verify these details when considering a property.
