# What a Private Cardiologist Visit Costs in Bogotá, Colombia
**Golden Horizons Status:** 🟡 WORTH TESTING — VERIFY COSTS

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Doctor directories list private cardiology visits in Bogotá from about $63 to $142, with a $79 online option. U.S. cash listings for a new-patient cardiology visit run about $129 to $444. The catch is that ECG, echo and lab prices are rarely published, and an echocardiogram was listed near $158. Colombia is Level 3, "Reconsider travel," so confirm every quote in writing.

If you are 65 or older, your first question is probably whether Medicare covers you there. It does not cover routine care outside the United States, so a Bogotá visit is a cash cost.

That makes the number worth testing. Prices below come from public directory listings, not from the hospitals, so treat them as a starting range for your own calls.

## Retirement Snapshot

**Primary decision:**  
Whether paying cash for private cardiology care in Bogotá beats a U.S. cash visit, once tests are added.

**Best fit:**  
Someone who can pay a visit fee of roughly $60 to $140 out of pocket and who will get written quotes for each test.

**Biggest warning:**  
Listed prices come from directories that may be out of date. No hospital price list was found for Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá or Clínica del Country.

**Healthcare:**  
Both Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá and Clínica del Country list cardiology services in Bogotá directories. Bogotá's ombudsman recorded 456 complaints about cardiology appointment delays from January to August 2026, which may push some patients toward private care.

**Housing / daily life:**  
Not priced here. Rent and daily costs were not verified today, so use a Numbeo or Expatistan city page for your own neighborhood check.

**Safety:**  
The State Department rates Colombia Level 3, "Reconsider travel" (March 31, 2026), for crime, terrorism, civil unrest and kidnapping. Its "Do Not Travel" areas include Arauca, Cauca (except Popayán), Valle del Cauca (except Cali) and Norte de Santander. Bogotá is not on that list.

## What Bogotá Cardiology Listings Show

Doctor directories show private cardiology visits from about $60 for the cheapest offices up to about $95 for many specialists. Prices were converted from local currency at the official rate of about 3,170 per dollar on October 16, 2026.

The high end is higher. One Chapinero cardiologist lists a visit near $142, and another listing is near $110. Online consultations appear around $79.

A follow-up price was not found in any listing. Ask whether a return visit within a set window is discounted.

### What tests may add

Tests are where the bill changes. One private profile lists an echocardiogram near $158. ECG and blood-panel prices were not found in any source checked today, so we do not quote them here.

Ask each office for the physician fee, ECG, labs and interpretation as separate lines. Ask whether the quote is for a clinic office or a hospital outpatient department, since hospital settings often add facility fees.

## Access, Insurance, and the Questions to Ask Before You Need Care

Original Medicare has a $283 Part B deductible in 2026, then pays 80% of approved amounts for care in the United States. The Part A inpatient deductible is $1,736 per benefit period. With Medicare and Medigap at home, your out-of-pocket cost can be small, so a Bogotá cash visit is not automatically cheaper for a 65+ reader.

If you have no U.S. coverage, or you are a visitor on a longer stay, cash price is the comparison that matters. U.S. cash listings for a new-patient cardiology visit run about $129 to $444 on one price-transparency site.

Colombia's public system works through EPS plans, which have their own enrollment rules and waits. Several prepaid plans, such as Colmedica, Axa Colpatria and Suramericana, appear in directory listings, but acceptance varies by clinic. Ask an insurer exactly what outpatient cardiology it covers before you rely on a policy.

## The Trade-Off

Private care in Bogotá can give you faster access at a visible cash price. But the price list is thin, and tests are not priced.

**What you may gain:**

- Visit fees that directories list from about $63 to $142.
- Two named private hospitals to call for cardiology.
- Faster access than the public queue, which had 456 delay complaints.

**What you may give up:**

- Medicare coverage, which does not pay for routine care abroad.
- Price certainty on ECG, echo and lab work.
- Any guarantee that directory prices are current.
- A clear safety rating, since Colombia is Level 3.

The part to test first is the written quote, not the headline visit fee.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much is a private cardiologist consultation in Bogotá in 2026?

Directory listings run about $63 to $142, with many near $69 to $95 and online visits near $79. These are directory prices, not hospital price lists, so get a written quote. ([doctoralia.co](https://www.doctoralia.co/cardiologo/bogota/comuna-chapinero))

### How long is the wait for a cardiology appointment in Bogotá?

Bogotá's ombudsman logged 456 complaints about cardiology appointment delays from January to August 2026. That is the public pathway, so ask private offices for their next open date. ([cambiocolombia.com](https://cambiocolombia.com/pais/articulo/2026/10/las-eps-con-mas-quejas-el-calvario-de-conseguir-una-cita-con-un-especialista-en-bogota))

### Does Medicare cover a cardiologist visit in Colombia?

No for routine care. Original Medicare has a $283 Part B deductible in 2026, then pays 80% for U.S. care only. Check any Medigap or private plan in writing. ([advancingstates.org](https://advancingstates.org/hcbs/article/2026-medicare-parts-b-premiums-and-deductibles))

### Is Bogotá safe enough for a medical stay?

The State Department rates Colombia Level 3, "Reconsider travel," as of March 31, 2026. Bogotá is not among the listed "Do Not Travel" areas, but check the page before booking. ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/colombia.html))

### How do Bogotá cardiology costs compare with U.S. costs?
