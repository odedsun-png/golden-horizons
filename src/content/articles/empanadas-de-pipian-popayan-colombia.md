## Quick Taste

In the popular streets of Popayán, Colombia, the scent of frying dough wafts through the air as vendors prepare empanadas de pipián. These golden pastries, filled with a savory mix of potatoes and peanuts, are a local favorite. The empanadas are crisp on the outside, revealing a soft, flavorful filling that’s both hearty and satisfying. Typically enjoyed with a spicy peanut sauce called ají de maní, these empanadas offer a delightful contrast of textures and flavors. Priced between COP 2,000-3,000 (about $0.50-0.75) each, they are an affordable taste of Colombian street food culture, perfect for those considering a longer stay in this pleasant city.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Empanadas de pipián have a rich history rooted in the cultural tapestry of Popayán. The dish traces its origins back to the indigenous communities of the region, where peanuts and potatoes were staple ingredients. Over time, the Spanish influence introduced the concept of empanadas, merging local flavors with culinary techniques from abroad. This fusion resulted in a unique dish that reflects the blend of cultures in Popayán. The name "pipián" refers to the peanut sauce that is integral to the dish, a nod to the indigenous roots that continue to influence the area's cuisine. Today, empanadas de pipián are more than just a snack; they are a symbol of Popayán’s culinary identity.

## How Locals Eat It

Locals in Popayán enjoy empanadas de pipián at any time of the day, whether as a quick breakfast, a midday snack, or a late-night treat. They are often eaten standing at street stalls, where vendors serve them piping hot from the fryer. The empanadas are typically accompanied by a small cup of ají de maní, a spicy peanut sauce that adds a kick to each bite. It’s common to see locals dipping their empanadas generously into the sauce, ensuring that each mouthful is packed with flavor. For those who prefer a milder taste, the empanadas can be enjoyed on their own, allowing the subtle flavors of the potato and peanut filling to shine through.

## Where to Find It

While empanadas de pipián can be found throughout Popayán, the best places to try them are at local markets and street stalls. The Mercado El Morro is a popular spot where vendors line the aisles, frying up fresh batches of empanadas throughout the day. For a more traditional experience, visit the neighborhood of La Pamba, known for its small, family-run stalls that have been serving empanadas for generations. Supermarkets and local grocery stores also stock the ingredients needed to make these empanadas at home, with potatoes and peanuts readily available for those who wish to try their hand at cooking.

## Regional Variations

While empanadas de pipián are a staple in Popayán, variations of the dish can be found in other regions of Colombia. In the Cauca Valley, for example, the empanadas might include additional ingredients like chicken or beef, offering a heartier version of the classic. Some cooks experiment with the spice level of the ají de maní, adding more chili peppers for an extra kick. In contrast, other regions might opt for a sweeter version of the peanut sauce, balancing the savory filling with a hint of sweetness. Despite these variations, the essence of the dish remains the same, celebrating the humble potato and peanut in a delicious pastry shell.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

Empanadas de pipián are an economical choice for anyone exploring Popayán. Typically priced between COP 2,000-3,000 (about $0.50-0.75) each, they offer a budget-friendly option for sampling local flavors. When ordering, it’s common to ask for a few empanadas at a time, as they are relatively small and easy to eat. Locals often suggest pairing them with a cold drink, such as a fresh juice or a local beer, to complement the flavors. If you’re ordering from a market stall, don’t hesitate to ask for extra ají de maní, as the vendors are usually happy to oblige.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before diving into a plate of empanadas de pipián, it’s helpful to know a bit about the ingredients and preparation. The filling is made from mashed potatoes mixed with ground peanuts, seasoned with spices like cumin and paprika. The dough is typically made from cornmeal, giving the empanadas their characteristic golden color and crispy texture. When visiting a street stall, be prepared for a casual dining experience, often standing or sitting on a nearby bench. It’s also worth noting that some stalls may offer variations of the dish, so don’t be afraid to ask about different fillings or sauces available.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is underestimating the spice level of the ají de maní. While it adds a wonderful depth of flavor, it can be quite spicy for those unaccustomed to it. Start with a small amount and adjust to your taste. Another mistake is assuming all empanadas are the same; variations in filling and preparation can significantly alter the taste. Lastly, some visitors overlook the importance of freshness. Empanadas de pipián are best enjoyed hot and fresh, so seek out stalls where they are made to order rather than pre-cooked and left to sit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main ingredient in empanadas de pipián?
The main ingredients in empanadas de pipián are potatoes and peanuts. The potatoes are mashed and mixed with ground peanuts to create a savory filling that is both hearty and flavorful. This filling is then encased in a cornmeal dough and fried until crispy.

### How spicy is the ají de maní sauce?
The spice level of ají de maní can vary depending on the vendor, but it is generally considered to be moderately spicy. It adds a flavorful kick to the empanadas, but if you’re sensitive to spice, it’s best to start with a small amount and add more as desired.

### Can I find empanadas de pipián outside of Popayán?
While empanadas de pipián are most commonly associated with Popayán, they can occasionally be found in other parts of Colombia, particularly in regions with a strong culinary connection to the city. However, for the most traditional experience, it’s best to try them in Popayán itself.

### Are empanadas de pipián suitable for vegetarians?
Yes, empanadas de pipián are typically vegetarian, as the filling is made from potatoes and peanuts. However, it’s always a good idea to confirm with the vendor, as some variations might include additional ingredients like meat or chicken.
