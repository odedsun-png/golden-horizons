## Quick Taste

Empanadas de pipián are small, crisp pastries from Popayán, in Colombia's Cauca region. The filling is a pipián paste of red potato, achiote, onion, garlic, cinnamon and peanut, according to El Tiempo. They are most often dipped in ají de maní, a peanut-based sauce. We did not verify a current price, so ask the vendor before you order.

![Empanadas de Pipián, Colombia](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8279711/pexels-photo-8279711.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fit=crop&h=627&w=1200)

## The Story Behind the Dish

Pipián is closely tied to Cauca. El Tiempo reports that the dish is in the process of gaining its own denomination of origin, and the same pipián also fills the region's tamales. Popayán has held UNESCO gastronomy designation since 2005, according to Colombia.com. We found no reliable source on the dish's exact origins, so we make no claims about them here.

## How Locals Eat It

They are small and usually sold by the half dozen or dozen, and El Tiempo notes "no se puede comer solo una." The best-known pairing is ají de maní, though the same article suggests trying other sauces that contrast with the pipián filling. Pipián dishes are common at local eateries from breakfast onward, according to Colombia.com.

## Where to Find It

Look for them at local eateries and bakeries in Popayán's historic center. We did not verify specific stalls or markets, so ask your hotel or host which places they trust, and choose ones where the empanadas come out hot.

![Where to find Empanadas de Pipián, Colombia](https://images.pexels.com/photos/36905236/pexels-photo-36905236.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fit=crop&h=627&w=1200)

## What to Know Before You Try It

Sources differ on the dough. Some describe a thin, crisp shell and others a corn-based dough, so ask the vendor. The filling contains peanuts, so tell the vendor about any nut allergy before you order. Ask whether the ají is spicy, and start with a small amount.

## Safety Note for Visitors

The U.S. State Department rates Colombia Level 3, Reconsider Travel (March 30, 2026). Its Do Not Travel list covers Arauca, Cauca (excluding Popayán), Valle del Cauca (excluding Cali) and Norte de Santander. Popayán is excluded from the Cauca warning, but the rest of the department is not. Check the advisory before you plan any trip outside the city.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is in empanadas de pipián?
Per El Tiempo, the filling is a paste of red potato, achiote, onion, garlic, cinnamon and peanut. They are fried and usually served with ají de maní.

### Are empanadas de pipián vegetarian?
The filling described by El Tiempo contains no meat, but we did not confirm how each vendor makes it. Ask about lard, broth or meat before you order.

### Is it safe to visit Popayán?
Colombia is Level 3 overall, and Cauca department is on the Do Not Travel list except for Popayán itself. Read the full advisory and plan trips outside the city carefully.
