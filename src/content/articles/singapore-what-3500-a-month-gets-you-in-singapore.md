# Singapore - What $3500 a Month Gets You in Singapore — A Local's Perspective

Living in Singapore, a city-state known for its high cost of living, can be a daunting financial prospect. With the average rent for a one-bedroom apartment in the city center hovering around SGD 2,500 ($1,850 USD), retirees need to budget wisely. This article helps you understand what a monthly budget of $3,500 USD could realistically cover in Singapore, including housing, healthcare, and daily expenses, allowing you to decide if this dynamic city is the right fit for your retirement.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want urban convenience and excellent healthcare.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to spend around $3,500 USD on housing, utilities, food, and healthcare.
- **Biggest advantage:** Access to world-class healthcare facilities and a safe, clean environment.
- **Biggest warning:** High housing costs and potential for unforeseen expenses.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Housing Costs in Singapore

Renting a one-bedroom apartment in Singapore’s city center, such as areas like Orchard Road or Marina Bay, can cost around SGD 2,500 ($1,850 USD) to SGD 3,500 ($2,590 USD) per month. For those willing to live slightly further from the city center, neighborhoods like Jurong East or Tampines offer more affordable options, with rents around SGD 1,800 ($1,330 USD) to SGD 2,400 ($1,780 USD).

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1720941001787-f2f21c86f569?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwxMXx8U2luZ2Fwb3JlfGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODI1NDk1NzB8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Living in a Housing Development Board (HDB) flat is another option. These government-subsidized apartments are prevalent and offer a more local experience. A three-room HDB flat in areas such as Bedok or Ang Mo Kio could cost around SGD 2,000 ($1,480 USD) monthly. However, securing an HDB rental requires patience and dealing with a competitive market.

While Singapore offers a variety of housing options, the high demand and limited supply can make finding the right place challenging. Be prepared for a rigorous application process and ensure you understand the terms of your lease, including any maintenance fees or restrictions.

## Healthcare and Insurance

Singapore’s healthcare system is among the best globally, with facilities like Singapore General Hospital and Mount Elizabeth Hospital providing top-notch care. Public healthcare is accessible to residents through the Central Provident Fund (CPF) and MediShield Life, but retirees without permanent residency will need private health insurance.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1552790920-0ba96dc99d01?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8U2luZ2Fwb3JlfGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODI1NDk1NzB8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Private insurance costs can vary significantly, from SGD 200 ($150 USD) to over SGD 1,000 ($740 USD) per month, depending on coverage. Evaluate your healthcare needs and budget accordingly. While the quality of care is excellent, the cost of private health services can be steep, especially for specialized treatments.

Access to healthcare is generally straightforward, with numerous clinics and hospitals spread across the city. However, retirees should be aware that medical costs can escalate quickly without adequate insurance coverage. Consider consulting with a professional to understand the best insurance options for your situation.

## Cost of Living and Daily Expenses

Groceries in Singapore can be pricey, especially for imported goods. A weekly grocery bill for two can range from SGD 100 ($74 USD) to SGD 200 ($148 USD), depending on dietary preferences. Local markets, such as Tekka Market, offer fresh produce at more competitive prices, providing an opportunity to save.

Dining out is a popular pastime, with hawker centers offering affordable meals for as low as SGD 5 ($3.70 USD). Upscale dining, however, can easily cost SGD 50 ($37 USD) or more per person. Public transportation is efficient and affordable, with monthly passes costing around SGD 120 ($89 USD).

Utilities, including electricity, water, and internet, typically add up to about SGD 200 ($148 USD) monthly. While Singapore’s cost of living can be high, strategic budgeting and lifestyle adjustments can make it manageable. One common assumption is that living in Singapore means sacrificing a comfortable lifestyle, but retirees can enjoy a fulfilling life with careful planning.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Before moving to Singapore, verify healthcare access and insurance requirements. Private insurance is necessary for non-residents and can vary widely in cost and coverage. Check visa or residency rules, as retirees typically need a Long-Term Visit Pass or other appropriate visa to stay long-term.

Tax obligations can be complex. Singapore has a territorial tax system, which means income earned outside Singapore is generally not taxed, but U.S. citizens must still file U.S. taxes. Safety is a strong point in Singapore, with low crime rates and a robust legal system. However, retirees should familiarize themselves with local laws and emergency procedures.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Underestimating housing costs and rental market competitiveness.
- Failing to secure adequate private health insurance before arrival.
- Overlooking the cost of imported groceries and dining out.
- Ignoring the need for a long-term visa or residency permit.
- Assuming all healthcare costs are covered without checking insurance details.
- Forgetting to budget for transportation and utility expenses.
- Neglecting to account for U.S. tax obligations while abroad.
