# Bosnia and Herzegovina - Best Cities for Retirees: Sarajevo vs Mostar — A Local's Perspective

Sarajevo, the capital of Bosnia and Herzegovina, has a population of approximately 275,000, while Mostar, known for its iconic Stari Most bridge, is home to around 105,000 residents. These two cities offer distinct lifestyles and environments for retirees considering a move to the Balkans. Sarajevo provides a busy urban experience with diverse cultural influences, whereas Mostar offers a more relaxed, scenic setting. This article will help you decide which city might suit your retirement lifestyle.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want a combination of urban life and historical appeal.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to spend around $1,200–$1,500 in Sarajevo and $900–$1,200 in Mostar.
- **Biggest advantage:** Affordable cost of living with rich cultural experiences.
- **Biggest warning:** Language barriers and bureaucratic challenges.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Sarajevo: Urban Life with Cultural Depth

Sarajevo's public transport system includes trams, buses, and trolleybuses, with a monthly pass costing around 53 BAM (about $30). The city is known for its active cultural scene, with events like the Sarajevo Film Festival attracting international attention. Bascarsija, the old bazaar, is a must-visit for its Ottoman-era architecture and local crafts.

Healthcare in Sarajevo is accessible, with the Clinical Center of the University of Sarajevo being a major hospital. However, private health insurance is advisable for expats, as public healthcare may not meet Western standards.

Housing options range from modern apartments in the city center to more affordable options in suburbs like Ilidza. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in the city center averages around 500 BAM ($290), while outside the center, it can drop to 350 BAM ($200).

One limitation is the language barrier, as English is not widely spoken outside tourist areas. It's beneficial to learn some Bosnian to ease daily interactions.

## Mostar: Scenic Beauty and Relaxed Living

Mostar is famous for its UNESCO-listed Old Bridge and picturesque Old Town. The city offers a slower pace of life compared to Sarajevo, with a strong emphasis on outdoor activities. The Neretva River provides opportunities for rafting and fishing, while nearby mountains are perfect for hiking.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1697378853988-2b2db1a26ca4?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8Qm9zbmlhfGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODQxOTExNzV8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Healthcare facilities in Mostar include the University Clinical Hospital Mostar, which provides essential services. However, for specialized care, residents may need to travel to Sarajevo or even abroad. Private health insurance is recommended to cover any gaps in public healthcare.

Housing in Mostar is generally more affordable than in Sarajevo. A one-bedroom apartment in the city center can be rented for about 400 BAM ($230), while in the outskirts, it can be as low as 250 BAM ($145).

A practical warning is the summer heat, which can be intense, with temperatures often exceeding 100°F (38°C). This can be a challenge for those not accustomed to such climates.

## Comparing Community and Lifestyle

While Sarajevo offers a busy environment with Eastern and Western influences, Mostar provides a more laid-back lifestyle with a strong sense of community. Both cities have active expat communities, but Sarajevo's is larger and more diverse.

A common assumption is that Sarajevo is overwhelmingly urban, but the city is surrounded by green hills and offers access to nature just a short drive away. Conversely, Mostar's reputation for being purely scenic overlooks its growing cultural scene, with several museums and galleries showcasing local art.

Public transport in Mostar is limited compared to Sarajevo, so owning a car might be necessary for ease of travel. However, the city is highly walkable, with most amenities within a reasonable distance.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Healthcare in Bosnia and Herzegovina requires careful planning. While public healthcare is available, private insurance is advisable for comprehensive coverage. Verify the availability of English-speaking doctors and specialists, especially if you have ongoing medical needs.

Visa and residency rules can be complex. U.S. citizens can stay for up to 90 days without a visa, but longer stays require a temporary residence permit. Consulting with a local immigration lawyer or official source is recommended to manage these requirements.

Tax obligations should be reviewed with both U.S. and local tax advisors to avoid double taxation. Bosnia and Herzegovina has agreements with some countries, but confirm your specific situation.

Safety in both cities is generally good, though it's wise to be cautious in crowded areas to avoid petty theft. Walkability varies, with Sarajevo offering more public transport options, while Mostar is better suited for walking.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Underestimating the language barrier; learning basic Bosnian is helpful.
- Overlooking the need for private health insurance.
- Failing to verify visa and residency requirements before arrival.
- Assuming all healthcare needs can be met locally.
- Ignoring potential summer heat in Mostar.
- Not budgeting for unexpected expenses like travel for specialized healthcare.
- Overestimating the availability of English-speaking locals outside tourist areas.

## Before You Move Checklist

- Verify visa or residency rules for stays longer than 90 days.
- Assess healthcare access and consider private insurance.
- Consult with tax professionals about obligations in both countries.
- Check rent and lease terms for housing in your chosen city.
- Evaluate walkability and transportation options.
- Research safety and emergency services availability.
- Consider language barriers and plan to learn basic Bosnian.
- Investigate community activities and expat networks.
- Plan for extreme weather conditions, especially in Mostar.
- Ensure access to emergency funds for unexpected situations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the cost of living compare between Sarajevo and Mostar?

Sarajevo is slightly more expensive than Mostar, with higher rent and transportation costs. However, both cities offer affordable living compared to many Western countries.

### Is healthcare adequate for retirees in these cities?

Both cities have essential healthcare facilities, but private insurance is recommended for comprehensive coverage. Specialized care may require travel to larger cities or abroad.

### What are the visa requirements for U.S. retirees?

U.S. citizens can stay for 90 days without a visa. For longer stays, a temporary residence permit is necessary. Consult with an immigration expert to ensure compliance.
