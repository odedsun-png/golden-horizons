# Strategy - Before You Retire Ask Yourself: Does It Have to Be in America?

**Golden Horizons Status:** 🟡 WORTH TESTING

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Many U.S. retirees consider living abroad due to potential cost savings and lifestyle changes. However, healthcare access, residency rules, and Social Security/Medicare implications vary by country and require careful planning. This option is worth testing if you're open to navigating complex legal and financial landscapes.

## Retirement Snapshot

**Planning-budget lens:** The focus is on testing retirement abroad rather than declaring universal affordability, with a decision framework addressing U.S. tax/SSA/Medicare considerations.

**Primary decision:** Compare the lived reality of retirement abroad (climate, daily life, services) vs. staying in the U.S., focusing on practical steps.

**Residency:** Country-specific residency rules and visa pathways require verification through official sources.

**Healthcare:** Research public/private system access and proximity to providers, noting that Medicare typically does not cover care abroad.

**Housing / daily life:** Long-term housing and community practicality are essential; tourism attractions alone do not establish retirement feasibility.

**Safety:** Country-level advisories should be considered with local data for a grounded assessment.

**Best fit:** Readers who value climate variety, cost-of-living trade-offs, and are willing to navigate complex residency/tax/healthcare landscapes with careful pre-move scouting.

**Biggest warning:** The mismatch between U.S. retirement benefits and foreign-country rules can produce gaps; explicit planning and testing are essential.

## Golden Horizons Decision Scorecard

- **Residency clarity:** Country-specific and requires early verification through consulates.
- **Healthcare access:** Varies significantly; Medicare typically does not cover care abroad.
- **Cost evidence:** Requires local testing beyond rent; consider groceries, utilities, and transportation.
- **Social Security implications:** Payments may continue abroad under certain conditions; verify with SSA tools.
- **Daily-life practicality:** Evaluate climate, community infrastructure, and service access.

**GH Takeaway:** Testing retirement abroad is viable for those willing to navigate complex legal and financial landscapes, with careful pre-move planning essential.

## The Planning-Budget Reality

Retirees should anticipate possible gaps between U.S. benefits and foreign healthcare/residency rules. Plan a contingency budget and a 30-day test to verify local costs and access in a candidate location. Evidence from IRS/SSA/Medicare guidance supports the framework but does not provide a universal budget number.

## The Residency Reality

Residency pathways and visa requirements are country-specific and must be verified through official government sources. Readers should consult destination consulates and immigration portals early in the decision process.

## Practical Comparison

- **Climate and daily life:** Evaluate how climate affects outdoor activities and seasonal routines using official climate data.
- **Healthcare access:** Assess public/private mix, provider proximity, and coverage gaps.
- **Residency rules and taxes:** Understand the physical presence/bona fide residence tests and foreign income exclusions.
- **Social Security:** Verify ongoing payment eligibility abroad with SSA tools.
- **Cost of living:** Consider groceries, utilities, transportation, and dining beyond rent.

## Who This May Fit

**Worth testing if:**
- You value climate variety and lifestyle changes.
- You're open to navigating complex residency and tax landscapes.
- You plan to conduct thorough pre-move scouting.

**May not fit if:**
- You prefer straightforward residency and tax arrangements.
- You rely heavily on Medicare coverage.
- You have strong ties to family in the U.S.

## The Trade-Off

**What you may gain:**
- Potentially lower cost of living, new lifestyle, and climate variety.

**What you may give up:**
- Complex residency/tax requirements and gaps in U.S. benefit coverage.

## The Golden Horizons 30-Day Test

- Rent a short-term stay in a target area to gauge climate, groceries, and healthcare access.
- Contact local expat groups for housing, services, and healthcare recommendations.
- Check official visa/residency pathways for the candidate country and begin pre-application steps.
- Verify Social Security payment rules for the chosen country using SSA’s Payments Abroad Screening Tool.
- Consult IRS/foreign income exclusion guidance to understand tax implications during a test period.
- Visit a local clinic/hospital network to assess accessibility and language support.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I receive Social Security payments abroad, and under what conditions?**
Yes, under certain conditions. Verify with the SSA Payments Abroad Screening Tool.

**Does Medicare cover care if I live outside the U.S., and what gaps exist?**
Medicare typically does not cover care abroad; research local healthcare options.

**What are the primary residency/visa pathways for a long-term retirement abroad?**
These are country-specific; consult destination consulates for guidance.

**How should I think about taxes if I live abroad for part of the year?**
Understand the physical presence/bona fide residence tests and consult IRS guidance.

**What constitutes “ordinary daily life” abroad for a retiree?**
Evaluate climate, community infrastructure, and service access in your target area.
