## Emergency-care access in Greece: who is covered and how it works in practice

In Greece, emergency care is accessible through both public and private healthcare systems. EU/EEA visitors with an EHIC or GHIC card can access medically necessary treatment under the same conditions as Greek residents. This means that if you're from an EU country, you can expect similar treatment to locals in public hospitals, often without upfront costs.

For non-EU visitors, the situation is different. While public hospitals are available, access isn't guaranteed to be free. Some facilities may require payment or rely on private insurance coverage. It's crucial to have travel insurance that covers medical emergencies to avoid unexpected expenses.

Public hospitals like Evangelismos Hospital in Athens and AHEPA University Hospital in Thessaloniki are key access points for emergency care. They provide essential services, but private clinics may charge co-pays or fees, especially for non-EU visitors.

## How EHIC/GHIC and 112 EKAV pathways shape visitor and resident experiences

The EHIC and GHIC cards play a significant role in accessing healthcare in Greece for EU/EEA visitors. These cards allow you to receive necessary medical treatment at the same cost as residents, which can be a significant advantage in emergencies.

The 112 emergency number is a vital resource, providing free access to emergency services across Greece. When you call 112, the EKAV dispatch system ensures that you receive prompt medical attention and transport to the nearest hospital if needed. This system is reliable and widely used, offering peace of mind to both residents and visitors.

However, if you're a non-EU visitor, having travel insurance with medical evacuation coverage is essential. This ensures that you can access private facilities if necessary, without incurring prohibitive costs.

## What retirees should budget for and how to prepare for possible costs

Retirees considering a move to Greece should plan for potential healthcare costs, especially if they are non-EU citizens. While public emergency services are accessible, private clinics may require co-pays or full payment for services.

It's advisable to budget for travel insurance that includes medical coverage and evacuation. This can range from €50 to €150 (~$55 to $165) per month, depending on the coverage level and provider. Additionally, understanding the local healthcare landscape, including the proximity of hospitals and clinics, can help manage expectations and prepare for any medical needs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do tourists in Greece get free emergency care under EHIC/GHIC?

Yes, tourists from EU/EEA countries can access medically necessary treatment under EHIC/GHIC terms, but charges may apply, especially for private facilities. Non-EU tourists may incur out-of-pocket costs. (Source: [eu-healthcare.eopyy.gov.gr](https://eu-healthcare.eopyy.gov.gr/en/healthcare-in-greece/accessing-health-services-in-greece-for-eu-citizens/emergency-care-for-ehic-holders/))

### What is the emergency number in Greece and is it free?

112 is the free emergency number in Greece, providing urgent help and EKAV dispatch to hospital care if needed. (Source: [civilprotection.gov.gr](https://civilprotection.gov.gr/en/112))

### Are uninsured foreigners guaranteed free access to public hospitals in Greece?

Uninsured access varies; public-hospital emergency departments exist, but universal free care is not guaranteed. Some programs exist for uninsured/vulnerable groups. (Source: [moh.gov.gr](https://www.moh.gov.gr/articles/health/anaptyksh-monadwn-ygeias/3999-prosbash-twn-anasfalistwn-sto-dhmosio-systhma-ygeias%3Ffdl%3D10015))

### What should a retiree planning longer stays in Greece consider for healthcare?

Proactively obtain travel insurance with medical-evacuation coverage; understand EHIC/GHIC status if from the EU; identify a public hospital network in your preferred city. (Source: [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/greece/health))

### Where are reputable hospitals located in major Greek cities for emergency care?

Athens and Thessaloniki have multiple public hospitals with ERs; private options exist but may involve co-pays. (Source: [eu-healthcare.eopyy.gov.gr](https://eu-healthcare.eopyy.gov.gr/en/healthcare-in-greece/accessing-health-services-in-greece-for-eu-citizens/emergency-care-for-ehic-holders/))
