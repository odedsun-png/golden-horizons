# Greece's Emergency Care for Visitors and Residents: What Retirees Need to Know About Access, Costs, and Practical Realities

> EUR5-15 (~$5.50-16) gets you through a Greek public hospital ER with your AMKA number. Without it, Evangelismos Hospital in Athens charges EUR50-150 (~$55-163) for an uninsured ER visit. Private hospitals like Metropolitan General or Hygeia run EUR150-400 (~$163-435) for emergency intake. A GP visit at a public PEDY health center costs EUR0-5 (~$0-5.50) for residents. Private insurance through Ethniki Asfalistiki or Interamerican runs EUR80-200 (~$87-218)/month depending on age. Medicare doesn't cover you here — and re-enrollment penalties hit 10% per year you're gone.

## Retirement Snapshot

**Primary decision:**
Whether to rely on Greece's public ESY system as your primary emergency safety net — or carry private insurance for faster access and English-speaking staff.

**Best fit:**
American retirees who want Mediterranean living with healthcare costs 60-80% below US rates, and who don't mind navigating a system where English isn't guaranteed at public hospitals.

**Biggest warning:**
You're American, not EU. EHIC/GHIC cards don't apply to you. You need either an AMKA number (through legal residency) or private insurance — there's no middle ground. Public ERs will treat you regardless, but the bill arrives later.

**Planning-budget lens:**
Healthcare runs EUR80-250 (~$87-272)/month total — private insurance plus occasional co-pays. Compare that to $500-800/month for US Medicare Advantage with similar coverage.

**Residency:**
Greece's financially independent visa requires EUR2,000/month (~$2,180) in provable income from pensions, Social Security, or investments. Processing takes 2-4 months through the Greek consulate, then a residence permit through the local Aliens Bureau.

**Healthcare:**
The ESY (National Health System) covers ER visits, hospital stays, prescriptions, and specialist referrals through public hospitals. Private insurance fills the gaps: shorter waits, English-speaking doctors, and modern facilities.

## How Greece's Emergency System Actually Works for You

Here's the part most guides skip: Greece has two completely separate emergency tracks, and which one you land in depends on your insurance status, not how sick you are.

**Track 1: Public ESY hospitals.** Call 166 (EKAV ambulance) or walk into any public hospital ER. Evangelismos Hospital in Athens, AHEPA University Hospital in Thessaloniki, and Venizeleio Hospital in Heraklion are the major trauma centers. With an AMKA number (your Greek social security equivalent, issued when you get a residence permit), an ER visit costs EUR5-15 (~$5.50-16). Without AMKA, the hospital bills you directly — typically EUR50-150 (~$55-163) for an ER visit, more for imaging or procedures.

**Track 2: Private hospitals.** Metropolitan General in Athens, Hygeia Hospital in Maroussi, and Thermi Clinic in Thessaloniki have English-speaking emergency departments with shorter waits. Emergency intake runs EUR150-400 (~$163-435) without insurance. With private coverage through Ethniki Asfalistiki, Interamerican, or Generali Greece, your co-pay drops to EUR20-50 (~$22-55).

The emergency number is **166** for ambulance (EKAV) and **112** for general emergencies. Response times in Athens and Thessaloniki average 8-15 minutes. On the islands, it can be 20-45 minutes, and helicopter evacuation to a mainland hospital isn't uncommon for serious cases — another reason private insurance with medevac coverage matters.

## The AMKA Number and What Your Healthcare Budget Looks Like

The AMKA is to Greece what the número de utente is to Portugal — your gateway to public healthcare. Without it, you're paying private rates for everything.

You get your AMKA at a KEP (Citizens' Service Center) once you have a valid residence permit. Bring your passport, residence permit, and AFM (tax number). Processing takes 1-3 days — much faster than Portugal's equivalent.

**Per-visit costs through ESY (with AMKA):**
- GP visit at a PEDY center: EUR0-5 (~$0-5.50)
- Specialist referral: EUR5-10 (~$5.50-11)
- ER visit: EUR5-15 (~$5.50-16)
- Prescriptions: EUR1-8 (~$1.10-8.70) per box (subsidized)
- MRI through public hospital: EUR50-80 (~$55-87) with referral
- Hospital stay (public ward): EUR0-15 (~$0-16)/day

**Per-visit costs at private hospitals (without insurance):**
- GP at Metropolitan General or Hygeia: EUR50-80 (~$55-87)
- Specialist at a private clinic: EUR60-120 (~$65-131)
- ER intake: EUR150-400 (~$163-435)
- MRI at a private facility: EUR200-400 (~$218-435)

**Monthly fixed costs:**
- Private insurance (Ethniki Asfalistiki basic, age 60-70): EUR80-150 (~$87-163)
- Private insurance (Interamerican comprehensive, age 60-70): EUR120-200 (~$131-218)

**For comparison — the same care in the US:**
- ER visit: $250-500 co-pay with insurance
- MRI: $500-1,500
- Hospital stay: $2,000-5,000/day
- Monthly premium (Medicare Advantage): $500-800

Total monthly healthcare spend if you see a GP twice through the public system and fill two prescriptions: EUR90-170 (~$98-185) including insurance. In the US, the same coverage costs 3-5x more.

## Practical Comparison

### Public ESY vs private hospitals for emergency care

**What the evidence says:**
Public ERs at Evangelismos or AHEPA cost EUR5-15 with AMKA, but waits run 2-6 hours and English-speaking staff isn't guaranteed. Private ERs at Metropolitan General or Hygeia cost EUR150-400 without insurance but see you within 30-60 minutes with English-speaking doctors. Most expat retirees carry private insurance and use public for routine care, private for emergencies.

**What to check next:**
Get quotes from Ethniki Asfalistiki and Interamerican for your age bracket. Ask specifically about ER co-pays at private hospitals — some plans cap them at EUR20-50.

### Greece vs Portugal for retiree healthcare

**What the evidence says:**
Portugal's SNS costs EUR5-10 per GP visit; Greece's PEDY centers cost EUR0-5. Portugal's private insurance runs EUR40-80/month; Greece's runs EUR80-200 due to a smaller market. Portugal has a 3-6 month AIMA backlog for residency; Greece's residence permit processes in 2-4 months. Portugal has more established expat healthcare infrastructure; Greece has lower everyday costs but fewer English-speaking public doctors.

**What to check next:**
If English-language medical care is a priority, compare private hospital options in your target city. Athens and Thessaloniki have more English-speaking private hospitals than most Portuguese cities outside Lisbon.

### Athens vs Thessaloniki vs islands for healthcare access

**What the evidence says:**
Athens has 15+ public hospitals and 10+ private hospitals within 30 minutes of any neighborhood. Thessaloniki has AHEPA and Papageorgiou public hospitals plus Thermi Clinic and Interbalkan private. The islands have basic health centers (Kentro Ygeias) but serious emergencies require helicopter evacuation to the mainland — Santorini and Mykonos see this regularly in summer.

**What to check next:**
If you have a chronic condition requiring specialist access, Athens or Thessaloniki is the safer choice. If you're healthy and want island life, confirm helicopter medevac coverage in your insurance policy.

### AMKA enrollment: what happens while you wait

**What the evidence says:**
AMKA processing takes 1-3 days at a KEP office — significantly faster than Portugal's 2-4 week número de utente or Spain's SIP card process. The residence permit itself takes 2-4 months, and you can't get AMKA without it. During the wait, private insurance is your only healthcare option.

**What to check next:**
During your 30-day test, visit a KEP office with your passport to understand the AMKA application process. Ask about PAAYPA (temporary AMKA) which may be available with your visa receipt.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do American tourists in Greece get free emergency care?

Greek public ERs will treat you regardless of insurance status — they won't turn you away. But "free" is misleading. Without an AMKA number (which requires a residence permit), you'll be billed as an uninsured patient. At a public hospital like Evangelismos in Athens, that typically runs EUR50-150 (~$55-163) for an ER visit. Private ERs like Metropolitan General charge EUR150-400 (~$163-435). EHIC/GHIC cards only work for EU/EEA citizens — as an American, they don't apply to you. Travel insurance with emergency coverage is essential. (civilprotection.gov.gr)

### What is AMKA and how do I get it as an American retiree?

AMKA is your Greek social security number — it's the key that unlocks EUR5-15 ER visits and EUR0-5 GP appointments instead of paying EUR50-400 as an uninsured patient. You get it at any KEP (Citizens' Service Center) once you have a valid residence permit, AFM (tax number), and passport. Processing takes 1-3 days, far faster than Portugal's equivalent. The catch: you need the residence permit first, which takes 2-4 months to process through the Aliens Bureau after you arrive with your visa. (gov.gr)

### What happens to my Medicare if I move to Greece?

Medicare doesn't cover healthcare outside the US — full stop. Once you've been abroad for 6+ consecutive months, you're effectively off the system. If you return to the US later, you can re-enroll during the general enrollment period (January-March), but you'll face late-enrollment penalties of 10% per year you were unenrolled, added permanently to your Part B premium. Greece's public healthcare costs EUR90-170/month total (insurance plus co-pays), compared to $500-800 for US Medicare Advantage — but the Medicare bridge-burning is permanent until you come back. Factor that into your math. (medicare.gov)

### How do Greek emergency response times compare to the US?

EKAV ambulance response (call 166) averages 8-15 minutes in Athens and Thessaloniki — comparable to most US metro areas. On the islands, expect 20-45 minutes, with helicopter evacuation to the mainland for serious cases (Santorini, Mykonos, and smaller islands see this regularly). The 112 general emergency number works throughout Greece and connects to multilingual dispatchers. Private ambulance services in Athens can respond in 5-10 minutes for EUR100-200 (~$109-218). (civilprotection.gov.gr)

### Should I get private insurance even if I qualify for public healthcare?

Yes, and here's why: public ER waits run 2-6 hours, English-speaking staff isn't guaranteed, and specialist referrals through ESY can take 2-8 weeks. Private insurance through Ethniki Asfalistiki (EUR80-150/month) or Interamerican (EUR120-200/month) gets you into private hospitals like Hygeia or Metropolitan General within 30-60 minutes, with English-speaking doctors and modern facilities. Most expat retirees carry both — AMKA for routine prescriptions and check-ups, private insurance for anything urgent. The total monthly cost of EUR90-170 is still a fraction of US healthcare spending. (ethniki.gr, interamerican.gr)
