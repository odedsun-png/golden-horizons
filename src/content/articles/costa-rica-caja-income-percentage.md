# Costa Rica's Caja System: You Pay 7-11% of Income and Everything Is Covered

> Costa Rica's public healthcare system, the Caja Costarricense de Seguro Social (CCSS), requires residents and expats to contribute 7–11% of their declared monthly income. This contribution grants access to universal healthcare through a network of public facilities like Hospital Calderón Guardia in San José. Enrollment is typically mandatory for residents, and the exact percentage depends on your residency type and income level. Retirees should budget for these contributions as part of their monthly expenses.

## El marco de la Caja Costarricense de Seguro Social (CCSS) y costos de afiliación para extranjeros

Costa Rica's CCSS is a unified system that finances both health insurance and pensions. For foreigners with residency or voluntary insured status, contributions typically range from 7% to 11% of declared monthly income. This percentage varies based on residency category and income level.

Enrollment in the Caja is generally mandatory for residents. Foreigners usually enroll as part of a long-term residency arrangement, such as temporary or permanent residency streams. The CCSS provides a broad public healthcare network intended to deliver universal coverage, though access can vary due to wait times and capacity constraints.

Retirees relying on Caja contributions should budget for a monthly payment within the 7%–11% range. The exact rate depends on residency type, declared income, and official CCSS rules for that year.

## Residencia, visas y acceso práctico a la Caja: qué necesitas saber

Enrollment in the Caja is closely tied to residency status. Foreign residents typically enroll through official channels, with specific visa types determining eligibility and payment obligations. For example, the Rentista visa is a common pathway for retirees, requiring proof of income and a commitment to contribute to the Caja.

To enroll, you'll need to provide documentation such as proof of residency, income declarations, and possibly other visa-specific requirements. It's crucial to verify the current requirements with Costa Rican immigration authorities or consular guidance, as these can change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What percentage of income is commonly paid to Caja by retirees with residency?

Retirees with residency typically pay 7–11% of their declared monthly income to the Caja. (InterNations)

### Which hospitals are part of Costa Rica’s CCSS network?

Calderón Guardia Hospital in San José is part of the CCSS network, along with other public facilities. (Kiplinger / OECD)

### Do foreigners need a special visa to enroll in Caja?

Yes, residency or insured status determines enrollment and payments. Verify the specific visa type with official authorities. (The Costa Rica Handbook)

### Is Caja coverage universal for retirees in Costa Rica?

The CCSS aims for universal health coverage through the public system, though capacity varies by region. (OECD; World Bank background)
