# Morocco’s 90-Day Stay: The Simple Extension Question Every Long-Stay Visitor Should Check

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Morocco’s standard tourist stay is capped at 90 days, and an extension may be requested through a local police authority before that period ends. An e-visa, where available, also expects a passport valid for at least 180 days and does not create residency rights. In Casablanca, a modest solo monthly plan can run about $1,075-$1,760 before healthcare, while a monthly transit pass is about $25-$60. The extension is possible, but it is not automatic—and a longer life in Morocco needs a separate residency plan.

Ninety days is long enough to learn the difference between enjoying Casablanca and living there. You can find your coffee order, settle into a grocery routine, ride the tram enough times to know whether it suits you—and still arrive at the question that matters most: what happens when day 90 approaches?

Morocco’s tourism guidance offers a hopeful but carefully worded answer. An extension may be requested at a police station. That is useful. It is not a promise. Approval, timing, and paperwork can depend on your nationality and the local authority reviewing the request.

For an American considering a winter abroad or a longer retirement experiment, that distinction shapes the whole plan.

## The 90-Day Rule: Possible Is Not Automatic

Morocco’s visitor framework allows a tourist stay of up to 90 days. VisitMorocco says an extension may be requested at a police station. The important word is “may”: it leaves room for local processing and case-specific eligibility rather than offering every visitor an automatic second 90 days.

If you think you may need more time, start early. Keep your passport and entry records organized, then visit the Casablanca police prefecture before day 60 to ask what it requires for your nationality, purpose of stay, proof of funds, and onward-travel plans. The practical issue is not simply whether an extension exists. It is whether the local authority can consider your request before your permitted stay expires.

For travelers using Morocco’s e-visa system, Accès Maroc describes a travel authorization, not a long-term residence document. Its guidance includes a passport-validity expectation of 180 days, and eligibility applies only to particular nationalities and travel circumstances.

That makes a 30-day scouting stay especially valuable. You can enjoy the city, certainly—but you can also find the relevant office, ask current questions in person, and avoid reaching day 80 with a lease in mind and no clear legal path.

### Three Months Is a Visit. A Longer Life Needs a Separate Plan.

An extension can offer more time, but it does not replace a residence permit. Moroccan immigration authorities treat residency as a separate matter, generally requiring a recognized basis for residence and documentation showing how you will support yourself.

The research does not provide a verified retirement-income threshold, savings threshold, or residency processing timeline for Americans. Start with a document folder containing your passport, entry record, passport photos, proof of funds or income, and onward-travel details. Then ask the police prefecture and Accès Maroc which documents apply to your case before arranging a stay beyond 90 days.

The sources used here also do not establish the current U.S.-Morocco tax-treaty position. Do not make a tax decision based on a tourist-stay plan; get current advice before moving income, investments, or pension payments to Morocco.

You do not have to solve every paperwork question before your first trip. You do need to separate the two decisions: a 90-day visit is one thing; a longer-term legal life in Morocco is another.

## Casablanca Costs: What Your Everyday Budget Has to Cover

The visa question may be the headline, but rent is what makes a longer stay feel real. In Casablanca’s city-center area, a one-bedroom is estimated at about $580-$630 a month. In the outside-center area, a comparable one-bedroom is roughly $340-$370.

That gap can change the character of your stay. A city-center address may put more of daily life close at hand; an outside-center apartment may leave more room for insurance, visits home, or the occasional restaurant meal without second-guessing the bill.

Food, utilities, and transport are the other pieces of the picture. A monthly food plan for one person is estimated at $200-$350. Basic utilities, internet, and mobile service can add about $150-$350, depending on the apartment, usage, and provider. Local transit remains a modest line item, but the apartment you choose will determine how often you rely on it.

### A Typical Day in Casablanca

Picture a Tuesday like this: a cappuccino at a local Casablanca café, about $2.10, followed by a tram or bus ride of about $1. At a neighborhood market, a liter of milk runs around $1 and a dozen eggs about $2—the small, useful prices that begin to tell you what a normal grocery week may feel like.

The local currency is the Moroccan dirham, and exchange rates move. Keep your working budget in dollars, then check the rate again when you are ready to sign a lease or make a longer commitment.

## Longer Stays, Healthcare, and the Reality Between Errands

For a 90-day visit, private travel or international medical coverage is the prudent starting point. For a longer stay, private insurance is commonly advised because public-system access can depend on residency status. The research does not verify a specific Casablanca hospital, clinic, insurer, or private consultation price, so price private coverage before treating healthcare as a fixed budget line.

Morocco’s General Department of Passports and Nationality handles the policy side of residency, while local police authorities handle practical in-country questions. Ask both about the documents needed before you make plans around an extension or longer stay.

Then there is Casablanca itself. This is Morocco’s largest urban area, with traffic, crowded sidewalks, and a pace that can feel far from a quiet U.S. suburb. The best test is not a beautiful afternoon in a café. Spend at least 5 weekdays where you might rent: use transit, buy groceries, handle a small errand, and walk the route home after dinner.

The research does not support a citywide safety ranking for Casablanca. Test the street, building entrance, transit stop, and walking route you would actually use at 8 p.m., not just the view from a café at noon.

A 90-day stay can teach you a great deal. It can also reveal that a place may be appealing without being the place where you want to settle.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long can a tourist stay in Morocco before needing to extend?

Morocco’s standard tourist stay is up to 90 days. VisitMorocco says an extension may be requested at a police station, but it does not promise approval for every visitor. Visit the local police authority well before day 90 so you have time to learn its requirements. (VisitMorocco)

### Can Americans retire in Morocco without a formal retirement visa?

There is no memo-supported universal retirement visa allowing every American to remain after 90 days. A longer stay generally needs a separate residency pathway, with documentation that can include a valid passport and proof of income or savings; an e-visa is not a residence permit. Accès Maroc also states a 180-day passport-validity expectation for the e-visa process. (Accès Maroc)

### What does a one-bedroom outside central Casablanca cost?

Numbeo’s September 2026 Casablanca data place an outside-center one-bedroom at roughly $340-$370 per month after conversion. That lower rent can leave more room in a $1,075-$1,760 core monthly plan. Inspect the actual building, street, and lease terms before deciding. (Numbeo)

### What should I expect to spend on utilities and communications?

For an apartment of roughly 915 square feet, Casablanca data indicate basic utilities around $50, internet near $43, and a mobile plan around $10. Together, those three items can approach $103 before changes in usage or exchange rates. Building condition and cooling needs can shift your result. (Wise)

### Is Casablanca affordable enough for a careful long-stay visitor?

Casablanca can be workable when housing, groceries, transit, and meals stay within a roughly $1,075-$1,760 monthly core range before healthcare. A casual lunch is commonly estimated at $4-$6, while dinner for two at a mid-range café is about $31. The larger uncertainty is whether an extension or residency route is accepted after the initial 90 days. (Expatistan)
