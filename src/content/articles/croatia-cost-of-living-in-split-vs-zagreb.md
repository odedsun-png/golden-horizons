# Croatia - Cost of Living in Split vs Zagreb — A Local's Perspective

Croatia's capital, Zagreb, and the coastal city of Split offer contrasting lifestyles and expenses that might surprise those considering a move. While Zagreb is the political and economic heart of the country, Split boasts Mediterranean charm and proximity to the Adriatic Sea. This article will help you evaluate the cost of living in these two cities, focusing on housing, healthcare, and daily expenses.

## Housing Costs: Comparing Split's Seaside Living to Zagreb's Urban Environment

In Split, renting a modest one-bedroom apartment in the city center will set you back around 500 to 700 Euros per month. Popular neighborhoods like Bačvice, known for its beach access, command higher prices, especially during the tourist season. On the other hand, Zagreb offers a more affordable urban living experience. In neighborhoods like Trešnjevka, a similar apartment can be found for approximately 400 to 600 Euros per month.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/31132717/pexels-photo-31132717.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Split's housing market is heavily influenced by tourism, which means prices can fluctuate significantly. It's not uncommon for rents to spike during the summer months, when tourists flock to the coast. This seasonal variation can be a challenge for those on a fixed retirement income. In contrast, Zagreb's rental market remains relatively stable throughout the year, providing more predictability for budgeting.

For those considering purchasing property, Zagreb might be the more economical choice. In Zagreb, you can find a decent apartment for around 2,000 to 2,500 Euros per square meter, whereas in Split, prices can soar to 3,000 Euros per square meter or more, especially in sought-after areas like Meje or Marjan Hill. However, the allure of waking up to sea views in Split is undeniable, and for many, it's a worthwhile trade-off.

One limitation to consider is that Split's older buildings, particularly those in the city center, may lack modern amenities such as elevators or central heating. Conversely, Zagreb's housing stock often includes more modern conveniences, which can make a significant difference in comfort, especially during the colder months.

## Healthcare Access and Quality: A Tale of Two Cities

Healthcare in Croatia is generally of good quality, with both Split and Zagreb offering access to public and private medical facilities. In Zagreb, the University Hospital Centre Zagreb is one of the largest and most advanced hospitals in the country, providing a wide range of specialized services. Split, while smaller, is served by the University Hospital of Split, which is well-regarded but may not offer the same breadth of specialized care as its counterpart in the capital.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/33884215/pexels-photo-33884215.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

For those relying on public healthcare, both cities require enrollment in the Croatian Health Insurance Fund (HZZO). The cost is typically around 70 Euros per month, which provides access to a wide range of services with minimal out-of-pocket expenses. However, retirees should be aware that public healthcare facilities can experience long wait times, particularly in Split during the summer months when the population swells with tourists.

Private healthcare is an option in both cities, with clinics like Poliklinika Medikol in Zagreb and Poliklinika Matulić in Split offering faster access to services. Private insurance can cost between 100 and 200 Euros per month, depending on the level of coverage. This can be a worthwhile investment for those who prefer shorter wait times and more personalized care.

One surprising aspect of Split's healthcare landscape is the growing trend of medical tourism. The city has become a hub for dental and cosmetic procedures, attracting international patients with competitive prices and quality care. This means that even private healthcare providers can be booked up during peak tourist seasons, which is something to consider when timing any elective procedures.

## Daily Life Expenses: From Coffee to Culture

Daily life in Split and Zagreb offers different rhythms and costs. In Split, a cappuccino at a local café like Zbirac near Bačvice beach will cost around 1.50 to 2 Euros, which is slightly higher than the 1 to 1.50 Euros you'd pay in Zagreb's bustling café scene on Tkalčićeva Street. Dining out in Split can also be pricier, particularly at waterfront restaurants where a meal for two might run 40 to 60 Euros, compared to 30 to 50 Euros in a typical Zagreb eatery.

Grocery shopping reveals further contrasts. Split's Green Market, or Pazar, offers fresh produce and local specialties, but prices can be higher than those at Zagreb's Dolac Market, reflecting the cost of importing goods to the coast. However, Split's market boasts an impressive array of seafood, a staple of the local diet, which might appeal to those who relish a Mediterranean lifestyle.

Transportation costs also differ between the two cities. Zagreb's public transport system is more extensive, with monthly passes costing around 50 Euros. In Split, the bus system is less comprehensive, and a monthly pass is about 40 Euros. However, the compact nature of Split means that many residents find they can walk or cycle to most destinations, potentially reducing transport expenses.

Surprisingly, cultural activities in Zagreb are often more accessible than in Split. The capital hosts a myriad of events, from theater performances to art exhibitions, often at lower prices than similar events in Split. For retirees who enjoy an active cultural life, Zagreb's offerings can be a significant draw.

Ultimately, choosing between Split and Zagreb involves weighing the appeal of seaside living against the conveniences of urban life. Each city has its own unique set of advantages and challenges, and understanding these can help retirees make an informed decision about where to spend their golden years.
