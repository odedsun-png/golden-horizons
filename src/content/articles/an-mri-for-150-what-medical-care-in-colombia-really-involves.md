# An MRI for $150? What Medical Care in Bogotá Really Involves

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Bogotá can look workable for a retiree, but the headline MRI price is not confirmed and must be quoted clinic by clinic. A short private doctor visit averages about $49, while a furnished 85-square-meter one-bedroom runs about $1,156 in a normal area or $1,462 in a higher-cost area. Utilities and internet can total about $147, and a monthly transit pass about $60. The available 2026 evidence puts core monthly spending around $1,100-$1,450 at the lower end or $1,700-$2,400 with a pricier home and more private-care spending.

A $49 private doctor visit is the sort of number that makes you pause over your morning coffee. So does a $2.47 cappuccino, or a furnished apartment that may cost less than a typical U.S. one-bedroom. But medical care is not a menu price. The question is not simply whether Bogotá feels affordable on a good day. It is whether you can reach the clinic you would use, understand the bill, arrange coverage, and get a clear quote before you need a scan.

That is where the MRI headline needs a little restraint. This research does **not** confirm an MRI in Bogotá for $150. It does show a city with documented everyday costs, private-care entry points, and several neighborhoods worth testing before you make residency or healthcare plans.

## What Your Monthly Budget Has to Cover Before Healthcare

In Bogotá, housing sets the mood of the whole budget. A furnished 85-square-meter apartment in a higher-cost area such as Chicó, Rosales, or near Parque 93 is listed at about $1,462 a month. A comparable furnished place in a normal area is listed at about $1,156.

That $306 difference is not merely about the view from the window. It may affect how much room you have for taxis to appointments, meals out, or an unexpected private-pay medical bill. When you look at any apartment, ask about *estrato*, the local tier that can affect utility charges, along with building fees and what the rent actually includes.

The Colombian peso is the local currency, but the figures here are presented in U.S. dollars using the October 5, 2026 conversion basis in the research. Exchange rates move, of course, which means a fixed-income budget deserves a little breathing room.

**Estimated core monthly spend:** $1,100-$1,450 in a lower-spend scenario with a normal-area home, conservative groceries, and modest dining; $1,700-$2,400 with a higher-cost home, fuller grocery spending, more meals out, and possible private-care spending. These estimates do not include a confirmed health-insurance premium.

**Remaining buffer vs the $2,000 budget:** About $550-$900 in the lower-spend scenario before unverified healthcare costs. In the higher-spend scenario, the cushion may be limited once healthcare or emergencies enter the picture.

**How your money compares to the US:** A Bogotá one-bedroom can sit below the cited U.S. one-bedroom range of $2,000-$2,400. The cited U.S. grocery range for one person is $400-$600, while a casual U.S. lunch is commonly $12-$20. Private health-insurance premiums for Americans aged 60-64 are cited at $250-$1,000 monthly—one reason the medical side of the comparison can change quickly.

### Residency is its own decision, not a footnote to the budget

Colombia has residency pathways for foreign retirees, but the available research does not establish one universal retirement-visa income rule. A low-cost lunch or reasonable apartment does not answer the separate question of whether your documents and coverage meet current requirements.

Before applying, ask the Colombian consulate or official migration authority for the active 2026-2027 rules. Get answers in writing about the documents connected to your income source and any health-insurance requirements.

A practical process is slower than a spreadsheet:

1. Spend 30 days in Bogotá before choosing Chicó, Rosales, Parque 93, or Usaquén as a longer-term base.
2. Request current residency instructions directly from the Colombian consulate serving your U.S. address.
3. Ask two clinics whether they accept private insurance, direct payment, or both for foreign patients.
4. Keep written quotes for a standard consultation, imaging, and any follow-up appointment.
5. Compare a normal-area rental quote with a higher-cost neighborhood quote, including utilities and building fees.
6. Recalculate your monthly budget using the exchange rate on the day you expect to pay rent.

Bogotá may work beautifully on paper. The paper should include a current visa answer and a clinic quote, not just a restaurant menu.

## What Private Medical Care Can—and Cannot—Be Priced From Here

The clearest documented private-care price is about $49 for a brief, 15-minute doctor appointment. That is a useful starting point for ordinary care. It is not a complete map of what a diagnosis, scan, specialist, prescription, or hospital stay might cost.

The headline’s MRI figure is not verified by this research. MRI pricing varies by clinic, whether contrast is used, whether a radiologist’s report is included, and whether you pay directly or use insurance. A reasonably priced consultation is one thing; imaging and follow-up care are separate decisions that should be quoted before you build them into a retirement budget.

### Public care, private care, and the unanswered middle

Private care has one clear price anchor: about $49 for a 15-minute doctor visit. The research describes public care as generally affordable, but it does not provide a public consultation fee, a foreign-resident enrollment cost, or an appointment timeline.

So this is not yet a clean public-versus-private price comparison. You can price a short private visit. You cannot responsibly price public enrollment, emergency care, imaging, or a specialist visit from the evidence in this memo.

Use a scouting month to contact providers in Chicó and Usaquén and request written answers. Ask what a first consultation costs, what a routine MRI costs, whether the quote includes the reading, and whether your proposed insurance is billed directly or requires payment first.

### Keep Medicare in your U.S. planning folder

Medicare is not a substitute for a Colombia healthcare plan. This memo does not provide a detailed Medicare coverage rule for Americans receiving care in Bogotá, so confirm your Medicare and supplemental-plan terms directly with Medicare.gov or your insurer before you leave the U.S.

Ask what happens if you return to the U.S. for treatment and whether you would face a coverage gap while abroad. Bogotá may offer a manageable $49 private-pay consultation, but one low office-visit price should not carry the weight of an entire healthcare plan.

### The route to care matters, too

A 15-minute private appointment may be affordable, but distance can turn a simple visit into a long medical day. When you inspect an apartment, map the route to two clinics while you are checking the kitchen, the elevator, and the nearby grocery options.

A home near your preferred providers may be worth more than a slightly lower rent farther away. The useful test is not whether Bogotá has care in the abstract. It is whether you can reach and afford the care you are likely to need on an ordinary Tuesday.
