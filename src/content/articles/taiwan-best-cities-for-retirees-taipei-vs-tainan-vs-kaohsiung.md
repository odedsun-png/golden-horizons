# Taiwan - Best Cities for Retirees: Taipei vs Tainan vs Kaohsiung — A Local's Perspective

Taiwan's healthcare system ranks as one of the most efficient globally, with the National Health Insurance (NHI) covering over 99% of the population. This universal healthcare system is a major draw for retirees considering Taiwan as a retirement destination. However, the choice of city can significantly impact your retirement experience. This article will help you decide between Taipei, Tainan, and Kaohsiung based on practical aspects like cost of living, healthcare access, and lifestyle preferences.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want diverse city experiences in Taiwan.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to budget $1,500 to $2,500 depending on the city and lifestyle.
- **Biggest advantage:** High-quality healthcare access across all three cities.
- **Biggest warning:** Language barriers can be a challenge; Mandarin or Taiwanese proficiency is beneficial.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Taipei: The Modern Capital

Taipei, with a population of around 2.6 million, is Taiwan's busy capital. The city is known for its modern amenities, excellent public transportation system, and active cultural scene. The Taipei Metro (MRT) is efficient and covers most areas, making it easy to get around the city without a car. Districts like Da'an and Xinyi offer high-end shopping and dining options, though they come with higher living costs.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1583654979589-aa7a6053a0d6?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwxMXx8VGFpd2FufGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODQ2MjMyMzR8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

In terms of housing, expect to pay around NT$30,000 (approximately $1,000) for a one-bedroom apartment in central areas. While prices can be steep, the convenience of city living and access to world-class healthcare facilities like National Taiwan University Hospital can make it worthwhile. However, the pace of Taipei might not be for everyone, especially those seeking a quieter retirement.

## Tainan: The Historical Heart

Tainan, Taiwan's oldest city, offers a stark contrast to Taipei's modernity. Known for its historical sites and traditional Taiwanese culture, Tainan is a slower-paced city ideal for retirees seeking a more relaxed lifestyle. The cost of living is significantly lower here, with monthly expenses ranging from NT$20,000 to NT$40,000 ($650 to $1,300).

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1583889659384-64d9df2347ba?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8VGFpd2FufGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODQ2MjMyMzR8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Healthcare access in Tainan is excellent, with facilities like Chi Mei Medical Center providing comprehensive services. However, public transportation is less developed than in Taipei, and you may need a scooter or car for convenience. The language barrier can also be more pronounced here, as fewer locals speak English compared to Taipei.

## Kaohsiung: The Southern Port City

Kaohsiung, Taiwan's second-largest city, is located in the south and offers urban and coastal living. The city is known for its friendly locals and active arts scene. Housing is more affordable than in Taipei, with rents for a one-bedroom apartment in the city center averaging around NT$18,000 ($600).

Kaohsiung's healthcare system is robust, with Kaohsiung Medical University Hospital being a notable facility. The Kaohsiung MRT is expanding, but the city is less walkable than Taipei, so a car or scooter might be necessary. The climate is warmer year-round, which can be a draw for those who prefer milder winters. However, air quality can be an issue, especially in industrial areas.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Healthcare in Taiwan is generally accessible and affordable due to the National Health Insurance scheme. However, as a retiree, you will need to enroll in the NHI, which requires a valid visa. Verify the specific visa requirements for retirees, as Taiwan does not have a dedicated retirement visa, and options like the Gold Card or investment visas may be necessary.

Tax obligations can be complex, as Taiwan taxes both residents and non-residents on their global income. Consulting a tax professional familiar with both U.S. and Taiwanese tax laws is advisable to avoid unexpected liabilities.

Safety is generally high across Taiwan, but it's always wise to check local crime statistics and emergency services access in your chosen city. Walkability varies, with Taipei being the most pedestrian-friendly.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Underestimating the language barrier; learning basic Mandarin or Taiwanese can be very helpful.
- Assuming all cities have the same cost of living; Taipei is notably more expensive.
- Neglecting to explore healthcare options; ensure you understand how to access and use the NHI.
- Failing to account for climate differences; Kaohsiung is warmer and more humid than Taipei or Tainan.
- Overlooking transportation needs; consider whether you need a vehicle in Tainan and Kaohsiung.

## Before You Move Checklist

- Confirm visa or residency rules suitable for retirement.
- Ensure you can enroll in Taiwan's National Health Insurance.
- Consider private insurance for additional coverage.
- Consult a tax professional about U.S. and Taiwanese tax obligations.
- Verify rent and lease terms, particularly in Taipei.
- Assess walkability and transportation options in your chosen city.
- Evaluate local safety and emergency service access.
- Learn basic Mandarin or Taiwanese to ease communication.
- Research climate conditions and prepare accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the cost of living in Taipei compared to Tainan and Kaohsiung?

Taipei is the most expensive, with monthly budgets ranging from $1,500 to $2,500. Tainan and Kaohsiung are more affordable, with costs ranging from $650 to $1,300.

### How does healthcare access differ between these cities?

All three cities have excellent healthcare facilities, but the availability of English-speaking staff may vary. Taipei generally offers more options for English speakers.

### Is public transportation available in all three cities?

Taipei has the most extensive MRT network. Kaohsiung's MRT is developing, while Tainan has limited public transport, making private transportation more necessary.

### What are the language barriers like in Taiwan?

While English is more commonly spoken in Taipei, learning basic Mandarin or Taiwanese is recommended for smoother day-to-day interactions, especially in Tainan and Kaohsiung.
