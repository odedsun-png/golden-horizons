# Greece - Safest Greek Islands for Retirement — A Local's Perspective

Greece is home to over 6,000 islands, yet only about 227 are inhabited. Among these, a select few stand out for retirees seeking not just beauty but safety and community. Choosing the right island can be challenging, but focusing on safety can simplify your decision-making process. This article will guide you through some of the safest Greek islands where you can enjoy your retirement years with peace of mind.

## What $2,000/Month Actually Buys on Syros

With a monthly budget of around $2,000, you’ll find Syros not only affordable but also rich in amenities. In the main town of Ermoupoli, renting a two-bedroom apartment could cost you around €600 ($700) per month. This leaves plenty of room in your budget for utilities, food, and leisure activities. The island offers a range of dining options, from traditional Greek tavernas to more international cuisine, all within a reasonable price range. Shopping at the local market on Miaouli Square, where fresh produce and local delicacies are abundant, won’t break the bank either.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/31054095/pexels-photo-31054095.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Syros is not just affordable but also incredibly safe. The local police presence is reassuring without being intrusive, and the crime rate is low. The community here is tight-knit, and residents look out for each other. While language can sometimes be a barrier, many locals speak English, which helps when you’re settling in.

However, Syros is less touristy than some of its neighbors, which means fewer direct flights, especially in the off-season. The island’s ferry connections are reliable but can be affected by bad weather, so planning ahead is crucial if you need to travel frequently.

## Healthcare and Accessibility on Rhodes

Rhodes, the largest of the Dodecanese islands, offers excellent healthcare facilities, which is a primary concern for many retirees. The General Hospital of Rhodes is well-regarded, providing a range of services from routine checkups to specialized care. For those who prefer private healthcare options, the Euromedica General Clinic in Rhodes Town is another reputable choice. Most doctors in Rhodes speak English, alleviating any concerns about language barriers in medical situations.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/19784664/pexels-photo-19784664.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

In terms of accessibility, Rhodes is well-connected with an international airport offering direct flights to major European cities. Public transport is efficient, with buses running regularly between the main towns and villages. For daily commuting or exploring, renting a car is quite affordable, and the island’s roads are generally in good condition.

Rhodes also boasts an active expat community, which can be both a comfort and a resource. Regular meet-ups and group activities make it easy to build a social network. However, during the peak tourist season, the island can become quite crowded, which might not appeal to everyone looking for a quieter lifestyle.

## The Peaceful Lifestyle of Sifnos

Sifnos offers a slower pace of life that many retirees find appealing. With a population of around 2,500, it’s one of the smaller Cycladic islands, known for its peaceful atmosphere and friendly locals. The community is welcoming, and it’s easy to integrate into local life through various social and cultural events, particularly in the main town of Apollonia.

Financially, Sifnos is quite manageable. A modest apartment can be rented for about €500 ($580) per month, leaving room in your budget for enjoying the island’s culinary delights, like the renowned Sifnos chickpea soup, at local eateries. The island is also famous for its pottery, and many retirees find joy in taking up this craft.

In terms of safety, Sifnos is one of the safest places in Greece. The local police force is small but effective, and crime is almost nonexistent. However, the island’s healthcare facilities are basic. For anything beyond routine care, you’d need to travel to the nearby island of Milos or back to the mainland. This travel requirement might be a dealbreaker for those with ongoing health concerns.
