# Cuenca vs. Medellín on $1,500 a Month: Which City Fits a U.S. Retiree?

**Golden Horizons Status:** 🟡 WORTH TESTING — VERIFY COSTS

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Cuenca fits $1,500 a month for one person. Medellín barely does. A one-bedroom outside the center costs $412 in Cuenca and $640 in Medellín, which puts core costs at about $912 versus $1,449 and leaves about $588 versus $51. The catch: Medicare Part B ($202.90) erases Medellín's cushion, and Colombia's pension visa bar is about $1,590, above this budget.

## Retirement Snapshot

**Primary decision:**
Whether a Cuenca or Medellín base fits a single retiree once rent, insurance and a residency income bar are priced.

**Best fit:**
Cuenca suits a renter who wants room in the budget. Medellín suits a retiree with income well above this budget who wants a larger city.

**Biggest warning:**
Medellín's leftover cushion is about $51 before health insurance, flights home or deposits. Numbeo's figures are crowd-sourced averages, not lease quotes.

**Safety:**
The State Department rates Ecuador Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution (updated August 17, 2026). It rates Colombia Level 3, Reconsider Travel (updated March 30, 2026).

**Residency:**
Ecuador's retiree visa asks for a pension of at least three basic salaries, or $1,446 a month. Colombia's pensionado visa asks for three minimum wages, about $1,590 a month.

**Healthcare:**
Both cities have private hospitals. Original Medicare usually does not pay for care abroad, so price private insurance before you decide.

## Cuenca vs. Medellín: What the Same Budget Covers

Start with rent. Numbeo's Cuenca page, updated November 18, 2026, lists a one-bedroom at $412 outside the center and $555 in the center. Its Medellín page, updated the same day, lists $640 outside the center and $1,292 in the center.

Both are crowd-sourced. Cuenca rests on 717 entries from 45 contributors over 12 months, and Medellín on 882 entries from 51 contributors. Treat them as a planning anchor, then ask for real quotes.

Numbeo also estimates a single person's monthly costs without rent: $500 in Cuenca and $810 in Medellín. Add rent outside the center and you get the core figures below.

**Cuenca, rent outside the center:** about $912 a month, leaving about $588 of the budget

**Cuenca, rent in the center:** about $1,055 a month, leaving about $445

**Medellín, rent outside the center:** about $1,449 a month, leaving about $51

**Medellín, rent in the center:** about $2,102 a month, a shortfall of about $602

These totals leave out health insurance, flights home, deposits and taxes. They are sums of Numbeo averages, not a quote from any landlord.

Some everyday prices explain the gap. A monthly transit pass is $16.00 in Cuenca and $75.01 in Medellín. Basic utilities for a 915-square-foot apartment run $39.83 versus $163.25. A meal at an inexpensive restaurant is $3.50 versus $9.07, and a mid-range dinner for two is $30.00 versus $45.37.

## Visas, Healthcare and Safety in Cuenca and Medellín

Low prices do not give you the right to stay. Ecuador's foreign ministry lists the retiree visa at a pension of at least three basic salaries. The 2026 basic salary is $482, so the bar is $1,446 a month. The page shows a $50 application fee, a $270 issuance fee, a 50% discount at age 65 and over, and a two-year visa. Its last update was December 31, 2024, so confirm it with a consulate.

Colombia's pensionado visa asks for a lifetime pension of at least three minimum wages. A Colombian law firm's page dated September 29, 2026 puts that at 5,252,715 in local currency, about $1,590 at Numbeo's November 18 exchange rate. That is above a $1,500 budget before you pay rent. The firm is not the government, so check the Cancillería page before you file.

On healthcare, Original Medicare usually does not cover care outside the United States, according to medicare.gov. At home in 2026, the Part B premium is $202.90 a month, the Part B deductible is $283 and the Part A inpatient deductible is $1,736. After Part B, the Cuenca cushion shrinks to about $385 and Medellín goes about $152 negative.

For safety, read the advisory text, not just the level. Ecuador's names no Azuay or Cuenca restriction. Its Do Not Travel list covers parts of Guayaquil and the provinces of El Oro, Los Ríos, Guayas and Esmeraldas. Colombia's Do Not Travel list names Arauca, Cauca, Valle del Cauca outside Cali and Norte de Santander. It does not list Antioquia, where Medellín sits, but the whole country is Level 3. It names Medellín for landslide risk. It also bars U.S. government employees from hailing street taxis or using public transportation.

## Practical Comparison

### Rent: The Number That Splits the Two Cities

**What the evidence says:**
Numbeo lists $412 outside the center in Cuenca and $640 in Medellín. In the center the gap widens to $555 versus $1,292.

**What to check next:**
Ask two local agents in each city for written quotes on a 12-month furnished one-bedroom, with deposit and utilities spelled out.

### Daily Costs: Food, Transit and Utilities

**What the evidence says:**
Milk is $1.03 a liter in Cuenca and $1.50 in Medellín. A dozen eggs cost $2.23 versus $2.94. Utilities are $39.83 versus $163.25.

**What to check next:**
Price six staples at a market and a supermarket in each city, and ask landlords for 12 months of electricity bills.

### Healthcare Access and Cost

**What the evidence says:**
The UK Foreign Office lists Clínica Santa Ana, Hospital Monte Sinaí and Hospital Universitario del Río as private options in Cuenca. Hospital Pablo Tobón Uribe in Medellín describes an international patient unit.

**What to check next:**
Call a hospital in each city and ask for a self-pay price for a new-patient visit. Ask an international insurer for a quote that includes evacuation.

### Residency Income Bar

**What the evidence says:**
Ecuador's bar works out to $1,446 a month. Colombia's works out to about $1,590.

**What to check next:**
Email each consulate and ask which documents prove a lifetime pension, and whether Social Security counts.

### Safety Level by Place

**What the evidence says:**
Ecuador is Level 2 and Colombia is Level 3. Neither advisory lists Cuenca or Medellín among its Do Not Travel areas.

**What to check next:**
Read both advisories on the day you book, and ask your Embassy contact what changed since the dates above.

## The Trade-Off

**What you may gain:**

- In Cuenca, about $588 left from $1,500, or about $385 after Part B.
- A lower visa bar in Ecuador: $1,446 a month, with a 50% fee discount at 65 and over.
- A Level 2 country advisory, one step below Colombia's.

**What you may give up:**

- Medellín's bigger-city scale, since its core costs run about $537 a month higher.
- Cushion. Medellín leaves about $51, and Medicare Part B turns that into a shortfall.
- Certainty, because Numbeo prices are averages and the Colombian income bar comes from a law firm's page.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Cuenca or Medellín cheaper for one retiree?

Cuenca is cheaper: about $912 a month for rent outside the center plus Numbeo's single-person costs, versus about $1,449 in Medellín. That is roughly $537 less. Both figures exclude health insurance. ([numbeo.com](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Cuenca))

### How much income does Ecuador's retiree visa require?

Ecuador asks for a pension of at least three basic salaries a month. With the 2026 salary at $482, that is $1,446. The fees are $50 to apply and $270 to issue, with a 50% discount at 65 and over. ([gob.ec](https://www.gob.ec/mremh/tramites/concesion-visa-residencia-temporal-jubilado))

### How much pension does Colombia's pensionado visa require?

Colombia asks for a lifetime pension of at least three minimum wages, which a law firm's page dated September 29, 2026 puts at about $1,590 a month. The visa is valid for up to 3 years. Confirm with the Cancillería. ([nexo.legal](https://nexo.legal/colombia-retirement-visa-requirements-2026/))

### Are Cuenca and Medellín safe for Americans?

Ecuador is Level 2 and Colombia is Level 3 in State Department advisories dated August 17, 2026 and March 30, 2026. Neither advisory lists Cuenca or Medellín among its Do Not Travel areas. Check both again before you go. ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/content/tsg_aem/us/en/home/international-travel/travel-advisories/destination.col.html))

### Does Medicare cover me in Ecuador or Colombia?

Usually not. Medicare says it usually does not cover care outside the U.S., with rare exceptions. At home, the 2026 Part B premium is $202.90 a month, and the deductibles are $283 for Part B and $1,736 for Part A. ([medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs))
