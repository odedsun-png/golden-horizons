## Quick Taste

As you wander through the Mercado 20 de Noviembre in Oaxaca, the air fills with the unmistakable aroma of mole negro, a rich and complex sauce that has been simmering for hours. This Oaxacan specialty, often served over tender chicken or turkey, is a symphony of flavors—bitter chocolate, smoky chilies, and a hint of sweetness from plantains. The sauce is velvety, coating each piece of meat with a dark, glossy sheen. For just MXN 70-80 (about $4-5), you can enjoy this culinary masterpiece, a staple in the everyday life of Oaxacans, offering a taste of tradition and comfort.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Mole negro is one of the seven moles of Oaxaca, each with its own blend of ingredients and flavors. Its origins are steeped in history, blending indigenous and Spanish food traditions. The dish is a testament to the region's cultural history, with its recipe handed down through generations. The process of making mole negro is labor-intensive, involving over 30 ingredients, including chilies, spices, nuts, seeds, and chocolate. Each element is carefully roasted, ground, and combined to create a sauce that is both deep and nuanced, reflecting the region's agricultural bounty and culinary ingenuity.

## How Locals Eat It

In Oaxaca, mole negro is more than just a dish—it's a celebration of life and community. Locals often enjoy it during special occasions and family gatherings, served with warm tortillas and a side of rice. It's common to see families sharing mole negro at home, with each household adding its own twist to the recipe. For a more everyday experience, head to a local fonda or market stall, where you'll find it ladled generously over chicken or turkey, accompanied by a simple salad. The Oaxacans' appreciation for mole negro is evident in the way they enjoy every bite, recognizing the labor and love that goes into its preparation.

## Regional Variations

While mole negro is quintessentially Oaxacan, you'll find slight variations in its preparation across the region. Some cooks prefer a spicier version, adding extra chilies for heat, while others emphasize the chocolate, creating a sweeter sauce. In coastal areas, mole negro might be paired with seafood, offering a different take on the traditional dish. Each variation reflects the local ingredients and personal preferences, showcasing the diversity within Oaxacan cuisine. Despite these differences, the essence of mole negro remains the same—a complex, deeply flavored sauce that captures the spirit of Oaxaca.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

When dining in Oaxaca, you can expect to pay around MXN 70-80 (about $4-5) for a generous portion of mole negro. This typically includes a serving of chicken or turkey, accompanied by rice and tortillas. Portions are hearty, often enough to satisfy even the hungriest of appetites. When ordering, don't hesitate to ask for extra sauce—locals know that the mole is the star of the dish. If you're dining in a market, take the opportunity to chat with the vendor about their mole, as they often have fascinating stories about their family's recipe and the ingredients they use.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before you explore a plate of mole negro, it's worth understanding the dish's cultural significance. In Oaxaca, mole is more than just food—it's a symbol of tradition and community. The dish's complex flavors might be overwhelming at first, but take your time to enjoy each bite. The combination of sweet, spicy, and bitter elements is what makes mole negro so special. If you're sensitive to spice, be sure to ask about the heat level before ordering, as some versions can pack a punch. And remember, mole negro is best enjoyed slowly, allowing the flavors to unfold and reveal their depth.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is expecting mole negro to be a quick meal. The dish is meant to be enjoyed, not rushed, so take your time to savor the flavors. Another mistake is overlooking the importance of the accompaniments—tortillas and rice are essential for balancing the rich sauce. Some travelers might also shy away from trying mole negro due to its dark color, but this is a result of the chocolate and chilies, not an indication of burnt or overcooked food. Lastly, don't be afraid to ask for recommendations or variations, as locals are often eager to share their love for mole.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is mole negro made of?
Mole negro is made from a blend of over 30 ingredients, including dried chilies, chocolate, nuts, seeds, and spices. Each ingredient is carefully roasted and ground to create a rich, complex sauce.

### Is mole negro spicy?
Mole negro can vary in spiciness depending on the cook's preference. Some versions are mild, while others have a noticeable kick from the chilies. It's always a good idea to ask about the spice level before ordering.

### Can I find mole negro outside of Oaxaca?
While mole negro is most commonly found in Oaxaca, some restaurants outside the region may offer it on their menu. However, for the most traditional experience, it's best to try it in Oaxaca, where the dish has deep cultural roots.

### How should I eat mole negro?
Mole negro is traditionally served over chicken or turkey, accompanied by rice and tortillas. Use the tortillas to scoop up the sauce and meat, ensuring you get a taste of everything in each bite.
