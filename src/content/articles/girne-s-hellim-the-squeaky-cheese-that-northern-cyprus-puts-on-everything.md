## Quick Taste

On a sun-drenched morning in Girne, Northern Cyprus, you might find yourself at a lively market stall where the aroma of grilled hellim—a semi-hard, salty cheese—fills the air. This cheese, known for its distinctive squeak when bitten, is a staple in Cypriot cuisine and is often enjoyed grilled or fried until golden brown. It’s typically served with fresh tomatoes, cucumbers, and olives, making for a light yet satisfying breakfast or snack. Expect to pay around TRY 20-30 (about $2-3) for a generous portion, making it an affordable treat for anyone considering a longer stay in this Mediterranean locale.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Hellim, or halloumi as it's known elsewhere, has a storied history that dates back centuries. Originating in Cyprus, this cheese was traditionally made from a mixture of sheep and goat milk, although cow's milk is also used today. The cheese's unique texture and high melting point make it ideal for grilling, a method that brings out its savory flavors. In Northern Cyprus, hellim is more than just food; it's a symbol of cultural heritage and local pride, often featured in family gatherings and celebrations.

## How Locals Eat It

In Girne, locals have mastered the art of incorporating hellim into nearly every meal. For breakfast, it's common to find hellim paired with fresh bread and a drizzle of olive oil. Lunchtime might see it tucked into a warm pita with fresh herbs and vegetables. In the evening, it often appears as part of a meze spread, alongside dips and salads. The cheese's versatility allows it to shine in both simple and elaborate dishes, making it a staple in Northern Cypriot homes.

## Where to Find It

While you can find hellim in many restaurants across Girne, the best place to experience its traditional taste is at local markets and small eateries. The Girne Open Market is a great spot to buy freshly made hellim directly from producers. Here, you can also pick up other local ingredients to complement your cheese, such as olives and fresh produce. For those considering a longer stay, supermarkets in the area offer packaged hellim, allowing you to enjoy this cheese at home, grilled or fried to your liking.

## Regional Variations

While hellim is a Cypriot specialty, its preparation can vary slightly across regions. In Northern Cyprus, the cheese tends to be saltier and firmer, which makes it particularly suited for grilling. In contrast, the southern part of the island might offer a milder version. Some producers add mint to the cheese, a traditional practice that enhances its flavor and aroma. Whether enjoyed plain or with herbs, each variation offers a distinct taste of the island’s culinary offerings.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

Hellim is an affordable delight in Northern Cyprus, with prices ranging from TRY 20-30 (about $2-3) for a portion at a local eatery. When ordering, consider pairing it with a glass of local wine or a refreshing ayran, a yogurt-based drink. Portions are typically generous, and the cheese’s flavor means a little goes a long way. If you're purchasing hellim from a market, ask the vendor for grilling tips—locals are often happy to share their favorite ways to enjoy this cheese.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before diving into a plate of hellim, it's worth knowing that its high salt content can be an acquired taste for some. However, this is balanced by its creamy texture when cooked. The cheese is best enjoyed fresh, so if you're buying it from a market, look for a firm texture and a clean, milky aroma. Also, keep in mind that hellim is traditionally made with animal rennet, which might be a consideration for vegetarians.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is not cooking hellim properly. It’s essential to grill or fry the cheese until it’s golden brown to achieve the perfect texture and flavor. Another pitfall is underestimating its saltiness; pairing it with fresh vegetables or bread can help balance the taste. Lastly, many visitors miss out on trying hellim in its various forms, such as in a sandwich or as part of a meze platter, which are excellent ways to experience its versatility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes hellim different from other cheeses?

Hellim's high melting point allows it to be grilled or fried without losing its shape, resulting in a unique texture and squeaky bite that sets it apart from other cheeses.

### Can I buy hellim in the United States?

Yes, hellim is available in some specialty stores and online retailers in the United States, often labeled as halloumi, allowing you to enjoy a taste of Cyprus at home.

### Is hellim suitable for vegetarians?

Traditional hellim is made with animal rennet, but vegetarian versions are available. Check the label or ask the vendor to ensure it meets dietary preferences.

### How should I store hellim?

Hellim should be kept in its brine or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and stored in the refrigerator. It can last for several weeks if stored properly.
