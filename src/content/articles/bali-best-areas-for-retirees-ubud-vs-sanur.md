# Bali - Best Areas for Retirees: Ubud vs Sanur — A Local's Perspective

Ubud, located in the heart of Bali, is approximately 20 miles north of the island's capital, Denpasar, while Sanur is a coastal town just a short 10-minute drive from the same city. Both areas are popular among retirees for their distinct lifestyles and amenities. Ubud is known for its strong local culture and lush landscapes, while Sanur offers a slower beachside feel. This article will help you decide which area might be more suitable for your retirement needs by comparing the cost of living, lifestyle, healthcare, and more in Ubud and Sanur.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want either a cultural immersion (Ubud) or a relaxed beachside lifestyle (Sanur).
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to spend around $1,500–$2,000 in Ubud and $1,800–$2,500 in Sanur for a comfortable lifestyle.
- **Biggest advantage:** Ubud offers an active cultural scene, while Sanur provides easy access to beaches and water activities.
- **Biggest warning:** Ubud's infrastructure can be challenging, whereas Sanur might feel too touristy during peak seasons.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Ubud: A Cultural Hub with a Green Backdrop

Ubud is renowned for its active arts scene, with numerous galleries, workshops, and cultural events. Jalan Raya Ubud is a central street where you can find everything from local crafts to international cuisine. The monthly cost of living in Ubud for a retiree can range from $1,500 to $2,000, which includes rent, food, and leisure activities.

Housing in Ubud varies significantly. You can find a modest one-bedroom villa for around $500 a month, but prices can rise quickly depending on location and amenities. Areas like Penestanan and Nyuh Kuning offer more affordable options, while Sayan and Campuhan are known for luxury properties with impressive views.

One limitation in Ubud is the infrastructure. Roads can be narrow and congested, especially during peak tourist seasons. Public transport is limited, so owning or renting a scooter is common. Also, while Ubud has a few medical facilities, including the Ubud Clinic, they may not meet Western standards for more serious health concerns, requiring trips to Denpasar for advanced care.

## Sanur: Coastal Comfort with Modern Amenities

Sanur is known for its long stretch of beach and calm waters, making it a favorite among those seeking a relaxed lifestyle. The beach promenade is a highlight, perfect for morning walks or cycling. Monthly expenses in Sanur are slightly higher than Ubud, ranging from $1,800 to $2,500, reflecting its coastal location and amenities.

Housing options in Sanur are diverse. You can rent a small apartment for about $600 a month, while a beachfront villa could cost upwards of $1,500. Areas like Mertasari and Sindhu are popular for their proximity to the beach and local markets. The housing market here is competitive, so securing a place well in advance is recommended.

Healthcare in Sanur is more accessible than in Ubud, with facilities like the Bali Mandara Hospital offering a range of services. However, for specialized treatments, travel to Denpasar or even Jakarta might be necessary. A practical warning: Sanur can become crowded with tourists, especially during the high season, which might disrupt the tranquility some retirees seek.

## Lifestyle and Community: Ubud vs Sanur

In Ubud, the pace of life is slower but culturally engaging. The Ubud Palace and the Monkey Forest are central attractions, and the town hosts numerous festivals, like the Ubud Writers & Readers Festival, drawing international crowds. However, the assumption that Ubud is always peaceful can be misleading; the influx of tourists can sometimes make it feel hectic.

Sanur offers a different kind of community experience. It's known for being more family-oriented and has a sizable expatriate community. The Sanur Sunday Market and events at the Sindhu Night Market offer opportunities to mingle with locals and other expats. Contrary to the assumption that beach towns are always active, Sanur maintains a quieter, slower atmosphere compared to other Balinese hotspots like Kuta or Seminyak.

Both Ubud and Sanur have their distinct appeal, but they cater to different preferences. Ubud is ideal for those who enjoy cultural activities and nature, while Sanur suits those who prefer a coastal lifestyle with easy access to modern amenities.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Healthcare in Bali is generally accessible, but the quality varies. In Ubud, medical facilities are limited, so for serious conditions, you may need to travel to Denpasar. In Sanur, healthcare is more readily available, but for specialized treatments, consider traveling to larger cities.

Before moving, understand Indonesia's visa requirements. The Retirement Visa (KITAS) is an option for those over 55, but it requires proof of income and a local sponsor. Consult with an immigration specialist to understand the latest requirements.

Tax obligations can be complex. While Indonesia has a tax treaty with the U.S., consult a tax advisor to understand your liabilities both in the U.S. and Indonesia. Safety in both Ubud and Sanur is generally good, but always be cautious of petty theft and ensure your accommodation has proper security measures.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Overlooking the infrastructure limitations in Ubud.
- Underestimating the cost of living in Sanur, especially during peak tourist seasons.
- Not securing housing in advance, particularly in popular areas.
- Failing to understand the local healthcare limitations and options.
- Ignoring the need for a reliable mode of transport, especially in Ubud.
- Assuming all medical facilities are up to Western standards.
- Not fully understanding visa and tax obligations.

## Before You Move Checklist

- Verify visa or residency rules, especially for the Retirement Visa (KITAS).
- Research healthcare access and consider private insurance.
- Understand your tax obligations in both the U.S. and Indonesia.
- Secure housing and verify rental agreements.
- Assess walkability and transportation options.
- Evaluate safety and security measures in your chosen area.

- Ensure emergency access and know the nearest medical facilities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the cost of living in Ubud and Sanur?

In Ubud, the cost of living ranges from $1,500 to $2,000 per month, while in Sanur, it's between $1,800 and $2,500. These estimates include housing, food, and leisure activities.

### How is healthcare in Ubud compared to Sanur?

Ubud has limited healthcare facilities, with the Ubud Clinic being the primary option. Sanur offers better access, with facilities like Bali Mandara Hospital, but for specialized care, travel to Denpasar or Jakarta may be necessary.

### Are there any visa requirements for retiring in Bali?

Yes, retirees over 55 can apply for a Retirement Visa (KITAS), which requires proof of income and a local sponsor. Consult with an immigration specialist for the latest requirements.

### Is it safe to live in Ubud or Sanur?

Both Ubud and Sanur are generally safe, but petty theft can occur. Ensure proper security measures in your accommodation and remain vigilant.

### What are the main differences between living in Ubud and Sanur?

Ubud offers a cultural and artsy lifestyle with a focus on nature, while Sanur provides a relaxed beachside atmosphere with modern amenities and a family-oriented community.
