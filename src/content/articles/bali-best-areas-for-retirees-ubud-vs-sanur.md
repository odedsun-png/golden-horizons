# Bali - Best Areas for Retirees: Ubud vs Sanur — A Local's Perspective

Sanur, located on the southeastern coast of Bali, is known for its laid-back atmosphere and accessibility, with a population of approximately 30,000 residents. Just 25 kilometers away, Ubud offers a contrasting experience with its lush greenery and cultural vibe. Both areas attract retirees, but they cater to different lifestyles and preferences. This article will help you decide which location might be the best fit for your retirement in Bali.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want a peaceful community with either a cultural or coastal focus.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to spend around $1,500–$2,000 for a comfortable lifestyle, including rent, utilities, and daily expenses.
- **Biggest advantage:** Affordable cost of living compared to the U.S.
- **Biggest warning:** Be aware of the cultural and language differences that may require adjustment.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Sanur: Coastal Living with Convenience

Sanur is a well-established area for retirees, offering modern amenities alongside traditional Balinese culture. The main street, Jalan Danau Tamblingan, is lined with shops, cafes, and restaurants catering to both locals and expatriates. Rent for a modest two-bedroom villa can start at around $700 per month, making it an affordable option for those seeking a coastal lifestyle.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518548419970-58e3b4079ab2?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwxMXx8SW5kb25lc2lhfGVufDB8MHx8fDE3NzgxNjE1NjR8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Sanur's appeal lies in its walkability and accessibility. The beachfront promenade stretches for 5 kilometers, providing a scenic route for daily walks or cycling. Public transport options include local bemos (minibuses) and taxis, though many retirees prefer to rent scooters for convenience. The area is also home to Bali Mandara Hospital, offering a range of medical services, though for more specialized care, a trip to Denpasar might be necessary.

One limitation of Sanur is its relatively quiet nightlife. While this suits those looking for a peaceful retirement, it may not appeal to those seeking active evening activities. Additionally, the influx of tourists during peak seasons can lead to crowded beaches and higher prices in local eateries.

## Ubud: A Cultural Retreat

Ubud, often referred to as Bali's cultural heart, is known for its artistic community and lush surroundings. The town's center is filled with galleries, craft shops, and traditional markets. Rent for a comfortable two-bedroom home in Ubud can range from $500 to $800 per month, depending on proximity to the center and amenities.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1449480881392-716d0ea24a47?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8SW5kb25lc2lhfGVufDB8MHx8fDE3NzgxNjE1NjR8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Healthcare facilities in Ubud include the Ubud Clinic and several smaller medical centers. However, for more comprehensive care, residents often travel to Denpasar. Transportation within Ubud is less developed than Sanur, with limited public transit options, so owning or renting a scooter is common.

A practical warning for potential retirees is Ubud's growing popularity, which has led to increased traffic and congestion, particularly during the tourist season. The town's appeal can sometimes be overshadowed by the activity level of visitors. Additionally, while Ubud offers a cultural experience, it lacks the immediate coastal access that some retirees might prefer.

## Lifestyle and Amenities in Both Cities

Both Sanur and Ubud offer distinct lifestyles, catering to different preferences. Sanur's coastal setting provides easy access to beaches and water activities, while Ubud's inland location offers a more quiet, culturally immersive environment.

A common assumption is that Bali is a cheap place to live. While it's true that the cost of living is lower than in many parts of the U.S., retirees should be prepared for variable expenses. Imported goods, for example, can be pricey, and dining at international restaurants will add up over time.

In terms of community, Sanur tends to attract retirees looking for a more relaxed, beach-oriented lifestyle, whereas Ubud appeals to those interested in arts, yoga, and a slower pace of life. Both areas have active expatriate communities, offering social opportunities and support networks for newcomers.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Healthcare access in Bali varies by location. While Sanur offers proximity to Bali Mandara Hospital, Ubud residents may need to travel to Denpasar for more comprehensive care. Having private health insurance to cover any unexpected medical needs is recommended.

Visa rules for retirees in Indonesia require careful planning. The most common option is the retirement visa, which has specific financial and age requirements. Consulting with an immigration specialist can ensure compliance with local regulations.

Tax obligations can be complex, as retirees must consider both U.S. and Indonesian tax laws. Consulting with a tax professional familiar with international tax issues can help avoid any surprises.

Safety in both Sanur and Ubud is generally good, though retirees should be aware of petty crime, such as pickpocketing. Walkability varies, with Sanur being more pedestrian-friendly due to its flat terrain and paved paths.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Underestimating the language barrier; learning basic Indonesian can be very helpful.
- Assuming all healthcare needs can be met locally; some treatments may require travel.
- Overlooking the cost of imported goods, which can inflate the budget.
- Ignoring the impact of tourist seasons on local life and prices.
- Failing to secure a reliable means of transportation, especially in Ubud.
- Forgetting to verify visa requirements and renewals.
- Not accounting for potential cultural adjustments and integration challenges.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is healthcare in Bali reliable for retirees?

Healthcare in Bali is improving, with facilities like Bali Mandara Hospital in Sanur offering good care. However, for specialized treatments, travel to Denpasar or even abroad might be necessary. It's recommended to have private health insurance.

### What are the main differences between living in Sanur and Ubud?

Sanur offers a beachside lifestyle with modern amenities and a relaxed pace, while Ubud provides a cultural and artistic environment with lush surroundings. Each has its own appeal depending on personal preferences.

### How does the cost of living in Bali compare to the U.S.?

Bali generally offers a lower cost of living compared to the U.S., with affordable rent and daily expenses. However, imported goods and international dining can be costly, so budgeting is important.

### What should I know about transportation in Bali?

In Sanur, walking and taxis are convenient, while in Ubud, owning or renting a scooter is common due to limited public transport. Traffic can be an issue, especially during the tourist season.

### Are there active expat communities in both Sanur and Ubud?

Yes, both areas have active expat communities, offering social opportunities and support networks for retirees. Engaging with these communities can ease the transition to living abroad.
