# Bali - Best Areas for Retirees: Ubud vs Sanur — A Local's Perspective

Bali's cost of living is often surprisingly affordable, with monthly expenses for a couple ranging from $1,500 to $3,000, depending on lifestyle choices. Ubud and Sanur, two popular regions for retirees, offer distinct experiences, each with its own charm and drawbacks. This article will help you decide which area might suit your retirement plans better.

## What $2,000/Month Actually Buys in Ubud

In Ubud, a budget of $2,000 per month can secure a comfortable lifestyle. Housing is one of the most significant expenses, and a two-bedroom villa with a pool in areas like Penestanan or Sayan can cost between $700 and $1,200 per month. These neighborhoods are known for their tranquil environment and proximity to central Ubud, where art galleries, yoga studios, and cultural experiences abound.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8333127/pexels-photo-8333127.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Groceries and dining are affordable. The Ubud Market offers fresh produce at reasonable prices, where a week's worth of fruits and vegetables might set you back just $20. Eating out is also inexpensive; a dinner for two at a mid-range restaurant like Locavore can cost around $50, but local warungs offer meals for as little as $3.

Transportation is another budget-friendly aspect. Renting a scooter, the primary mode of transport, costs about $60 per month. However, traffic congestion and narrow roads can be a limitation, especially for those not accustomed to driving on two wheels. Hiring a car with a driver is an option, albeit more expensive at approximately $500 monthly.

Utilities, including electricity, water, and internet, typically range from $100 to $150 per month. Healthcare services are available but may require trips to Denpasar for more specialized care. The limitation here is that Ubud lacks large hospitals, so for anything beyond basic healthcare, travel is necessary.

Ubud's lifestyle is serene and creatively stimulating, perfect for those who enjoy a slower pace and cultural immersion. However, the rural setting might feel isolated for those accustomed to urban conveniences. The rainy season can also pose challenges with frequent downpours and occasional flooding.

## What $2,000/Month Actually Buys in Sanur

Sanur, on the southeastern coast of Bali, offers a different vibe with beachfront living. With a $2,000 monthly budget, you can find a one or two-bedroom apartment or small house close to the beach, particularly in areas like Jalan Danau Tamblingan, for around $600 to $1,000. These accommodations often include modern amenities and easy access to the beach, shops, and restaurants.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/36663414/pexels-photo-36663414.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Dining in Sanur can be both an affordable and delightful experience. A meal at a local eatery might cost around $5, while more upscale dining at places like Massimo Italian Restaurant would cost approximately $30 for two. Local markets, such as the Sindhu Night Market, offer a variety of affordable local foods and fresh produce.

Transportation options are slightly more urbanized compared to Ubud. Renting a bicycle is popular and costs about $30 per month, thanks to Sanur's flat terrain and dedicated bike paths. Taxis and ride-hailing services are readily available, with fares starting at $1.50 for short trips. The limitation here is that traffic can be heavy, especially during peak tourist seasons.

Utilities are generally around $100 monthly, similar to Ubud. However, Sanur boasts better healthcare facilities with the Bali Mandara Hospital nearby, offering a range of services and emergency care. For more serious medical needs, the larger BIMC Hospital in Kuta is about a 30-minute drive away.

Sanur's appeal lies in its relaxed coastal atmosphere, suitable for those who enjoy beach activities and a more cosmopolitan lifestyle. This area is more tourist-friendly and offers a blend of local and international communities. However, the influx of tourists can sometimes make it feel less authentic than the quieter, more traditional Ubud.

## The Social and Cultural Scene: Ubud vs Sanur

Ubud is known as Bali's cultural heart, with a strong focus on art, spirituality, and wellness. The Ubud Writers & Readers Festival is one of the many cultural events that attract international visitors. Yoga enthusiasts will find a plethora of studios offering classes and retreats, such as The Yoga Barn, which is a hub for wellness activities. However, the emphasis on spirituality and alternative lifestyles might not appeal to everyone.

Conversely, Sanur offers a more laid-back social scene. The beach promenade is a popular spot for morning walks and evening strolls, where locals and expats mingle. The Sanur Village Festival celebrates local culture with music, dance, and food, providing a taste of Balinese traditions in a more relaxed setting. Sanur's social life is less about organized events and more about everyday interactions at cafes and beach bars.

While Ubud's cultural activities provide a deep dive into Balinese traditions, they can sometimes feel intense or overwhelming. Sanur, with its easygoing vibe, might appeal more to those who prefer a balanced mix of relaxation and cultural engagement without the intensity found in Ubud.

Both areas have active expat communities, with social groups and clubs catering to various interests. In Ubud, these might focus on arts and wellness, while in Sanur, they often revolve around beach activities and casual gatherings. The choice between Ubud and Sanur ultimately depends on whether you prefer the artistic and spiritual depth of Ubud or the coastal and leisurely lifestyle of Sanur.
