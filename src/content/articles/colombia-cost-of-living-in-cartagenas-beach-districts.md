# Colombia - Cost of Living in Cartagena’s Beach Districts — A Local's Perspective

Cartagena, with its riveting history and active local life, is not just a tourist hotspot but also a budding retirement destination. As of 2023, you can rent a furnished one-bedroom apartment in one of the city's beach areas for as little as $500 a month. This article will help you decide whether Cartagena's cost of living aligns with your retirement plans and lifestyle preferences.

## What $2,000/Month Actually Buys in Bocagrande

With a budget of $2,000 per month, living comfortably in Cartagena’s Bocagrande area is more than feasible. This budget covers a wide array of expenses, from housing to daily necessities. For those intrigued by life close to the ocean, a furnished one-bedroom apartment can be rented for around $700 to $1,000 monthly with spectacular views of the Caribbean Sea. It's not just the coastline that captivates; Bocagrande is a busy neighborhood where you can enjoy the convenience of nearby supermarkets, pharmacies, and local eateries.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1476514525535-07fb3b4ae5f1?w=1200)

In addition to housing, groceries are relatively affordable. A weekly grocery run for two people, including fresh fruits, vegetables, meats, and other essentials, generally costs about $50. Dining out is another affordable luxury; a typical meal in a mid-range restaurant costs around $20 for two people. For those who enjoy cooking, the Mercado de Bazurto is a local market where you can buy fresh produce at bargain prices.

The public transportation system is a cost-effective way to get around the city. Buses and shared taxis are prevalent, with fares averaging just $0.70 per ride. However, be prepared for crowded conditions during peak hours. For a more comfortable experience, private taxis are widely available, although they are slightly more expensive. Internet and basic utilities such as water, electricity, and gas generally cost around $100 per month.

However, while Bocagrande offers a lot, it’s not without its limitations. The area can be quite tourist-heavy, especially during peak seasons, which might not be ideal if you’re looking for a quieter lifestyle. Additionally, traffic congestion can be a hassle, particularly on weekends.

## Accessing Healthcare in El Laguito

Healthcare in Cartagena is a major consideration, especially for retirees. El Laguito, a neighborhood adjacent to Bocagrande, offers access to reputable medical facilities. One of the key hospitals is Hospital Naval de Cartagena, which is known for its emergency services and a variety of specialties. Another option is Clínica Medihelp Services, which is a private clinic offering modern healthcare services with English-speaking staff.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1528360983277-13d401cdc186?w=1200)

Health insurance is another aspect worth pondering. Many expats opt for local insurance plans, which can cost anywhere between $50 and $100 per month, depending on coverage. These plans cover basic medical needs and some offer dental and vision care. For those who wish to keep their U.S. insurance while overseas, some providers offer international plans, though they tend to be more expensive.

Prescription medications are usually less expensive in Colombia than in the U.S. Many common medications are available over-the-counter, although it is advisable to bring a doctor’s prescription for specific drugs. Pharmacies are widespread in El Laguito, making it convenient to fill prescriptions or seek medical advice.

While the healthcare system is generally reliable, not all facilities in Cartagena offer the same level of care, particularly in more remote areas of the city. Hence, proximity to reputable hospitals like those in El Laguito is a crucial factor when considering where to settle.

## Unexpected Realities of Retiring in Marbella

Marbella, another beach district in Cartagena, offers a different experience. While it doesn’t have the same tourist influx as Bocagrande, it provides a more relaxed environment with a strong local community feel. Here, the cost of living can be slightly lower than in Bocagrande. A comfortable two-bedroom apartment can be rented for about $600 to $900 monthly, with many options providing impressive ocean views.

One thing that might surprise you about Marbella is the local emphasis on community events and gatherings. Markets such as the Plaza de Mercado de Marbella are not just places to shop but community centers where you can engage with locals and other expats. This offers a special opportunity to immerse yourself in the culture and practice your Spanish.

However, Marbella’s tranquility comes with its own challenges. The area is not as developed in terms of infrastructure, and you might find fewer English-speaking locals compared to Bocagrande. This can be both a challenge and an opportunity to improve your Spanish language skills.

Transportation in Marbella is less congested than in Bocagrande, making it easier to get around by bike or on foot. However, the public transportation options are slightly limited, and you might find yourself relying more on private taxis or rideshare services.

In terms of safety, Marbella is considered a safer neighborhood, but like any city, it’s important to remain vigilant. Engaging with local community groups can be a great way to stay informed and connected.
