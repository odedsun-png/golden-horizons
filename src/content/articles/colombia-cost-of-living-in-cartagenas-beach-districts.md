# Colombia - Cost of Living in Cartagena’s Beach Districts — A Local's Perspective

Cartagena's beach districts, such as Bocagrande and El Laguito, offer an appealing lifestyle that can be surprisingly affordable. Did you know that a comfortable retirement here could cost you as little as $1,500 per month? This article delves into the financial and lifestyle aspects of living in Cartagena's beach districts, helping you decide if this active city by the Caribbean Sea is the right fit for your retirement plans.

## What $1,500/Month Actually Buys in Bocagrande

With a budget of $1,500 per month, you can lead a comfortable life in Bocagrande, one of Cartagena's most sought-after beach districts. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in a modern high-rise with sea views can range from $500 to $700. These apartments often come with amenities such as pools, gyms, and security services. Groceries at stores like Carulla or Olímpica might set you back around $200 a month, assuming you cook most of your meals at home.

Dining out is quite affordable too. A meal at a local restaurant, such as La Olla Cartagenera, costs around $5 to $10. If you prefer international cuisine, a meal at a more upscale place might range from $15 to $20. Transportation is another budget-friendly aspect, with a monthly public transport pass costing about $30. Taxis are also economical, usually charging around $2–$3 for short rides within the neighborhood.

However, one limitation to consider is the cost of imported goods. Electronics, branded clothing, and international groceries can be significantly more expensive compared to local options. If you have specific dietary needs or brand loyalties, you may find yourself spending more than anticipated. Despite this, many residents find that the affordable housing and daily expenses more than compensate for these occasional higher costs.

## Healthcare Access and Quality in Cartagena

Healthcare in Cartagena is both accessible and affordable, a crucial consideration for anyone planning retirement abroad. The city boasts several reputable hospitals and clinics, such as Hospital Bocagrande and Clínica Medihelp, which offer a range of services comparable to those in North America. Routine doctor's visits typically cost around $30 without insurance, and many expats opt for private health insurance, which can range from $50 to $100 per month, depending on the coverage.

Pharmacies are abundant and well-stocked, often carrying both local and international brands of medication. If you have specific prescriptions, check their availability before moving. One of the challenges some retirees face is the language barrier, as not all medical staff speak English fluently. However, many hospitals have multilingual staff or can provide translators upon request.

Dental care is another area where retirees can find excellent value. Routine check-ups or cleanings are affordable, often costing less than $50 per visit. More extensive procedures, like crowns or implants, can be done at a fraction of the cost compared to the U.S. While the quality of care is generally high, it's always best to research and choose healthcare providers that meet your personal standards and preferences.

## Cultural and Social Life in El Laguito

El Laguito offers a dynamic social scene enriched by both local culture and a growing expat community. While Cartagena is often associated with its historical Old Town, the beach districts provide a more laid-back lifestyle with plenty of social opportunities. You might be surprised to learn that salsa dancing is not the only popular activity—many locals and expats alike enjoy yoga classes on the beach or join book clubs that meet at cafes like Juan Valdez on Avenida San Martín.

One assumption some might have about Cartagena is that it’s all about the beach and nightlife. While these are certainly highlights, the city also boasts a variety of cultural experiences. The Cartagena International Film Festival, held annually, attracts filmmakers and fans from around the world. Additionally, small art galleries and workshops are scattered throughout the beach districts, offering classes and exhibitions.

Language can be a barrier in social situations, but many locals are eager to practice English, and there are plenty of language exchange events for those interested in improving their Spanish. The combination of cultural activities and opportunities to connect with both locals and fellow expats makes El Laguito an active place to retire.
