# Colombia - Cost of Living in Cartagena’s Beach Districts — A Local's Perspective

Cartagena, with its historical and Caribbean coastline, is a city where retirees can live comfortably on a budget. A couple can enjoy a decent lifestyle in Cartagena's beach districts for around $1,500 to $2,000 a month. This article will explore what living in these neighborhoods means for your budget, healthcare access, and transportation options.

## What $2,000/Month Actually Buys in Bocagrande

In Bocagrande, one of Cartagena’s most sought-after neighborhoods, $2,000 a month can cover your basic living expenses comfortably. Renting a two-bedroom apartment in Bocagrande typically costs between $700 and $1,000, depending on the proximity to the beach and amenities. The area is known for its high-rise buildings, offering impressive views of the sea and easy access to shopping centers and restaurants.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1606602842475-a5852a58b399?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwxMXx8Q29sb21iaWF8ZW58MHwwfHx8MTc3ODEyMjU4OHww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Eating out is relatively affordable; a meal at a mid-range restaurant on Avenida San Martín will set you back about $10 per person. Groceries for a couple might add up to around $300 a month if you shop at local markets like Mercado de Bazurto, where fresh produce is both plentiful and affordable.

Utilities, including electricity, water, and gas, generally cost around $100 a month. Internet and mobile phone plans add another $40 to $60. One limitation here is the cost of imported goods, which can be significantly higher than locally produced items. If you have a taste for specific imported foods or products, expect your grocery bill to climb.

Bocagrande offers a convenient lifestyle with its walkability and access to services. However, the neighborhood can be noisy, especially during peak tourist seasons, which might be a drawback if you're seeking peace and quiet.

## Healthcare Access: Clinics and Hospitals in Cartagena

Cartagena provides a variety of healthcare options, from public hospitals to private clinics. The Medihelp Clinic in the El Laguito area is a popular choice among expats for its quality care and English-speaking staff. A consultation with a general practitioner costs around $30, while specialized care might be a bit pricier, yet still affordable compared to U.S. standards.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1718119617938-2a3b376fb7d6?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8Q29sb21iaWF8ZW58MHwwfHx8MTc3ODEyMjI2OXww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

For more serious medical needs, the Hospital Universitario del Caribe offers comprehensive services, though it is a public hospital and can have longer wait times. Having a mix of public and private healthcare plans, which can cost around $100 to $150 a month for insurance, ensures access to a broader range of services without the potentially lengthy wait times at public facilities.

Pharmacies are widely available across Cartagena, with many medications available over-the-counter. However, verify whether your current prescriptions are readily available or if you need to consider alternatives.

Language can be a barrier in some healthcare settings, so having a basic understanding of Spanish or using a translation app can be immensely beneficial. Cartagena’s healthcare infrastructure is solid, providing peace of mind for those considering calling this coastal city home.

## Transportation and Getting Around: Buses, Taxis, and More

Cartagena offers several transportation options, making it relatively easy to get around without owning a car. The city’s bus system, including the Transcaribe line, is efficient and affordable, with fares around $1 per ride. Buses frequently run along the main roads of Bocagrande and connect to other parts of the city, including the historic center and working-class neighborhoods.

Taxis are another popular option, with a typical fare from Bocagrande to the walled city costing about $4. It's always best to agree on a fare before starting your journey since meters are not commonly used.

For short distances, you might find yourself using motorcycle taxis, known locally as "moto-taxis." While they are a quick way to get through traffic, they come with safety concerns and are not always the best choice for everyone.

Uber operates in Cartagena and provides a more predictable alternative to local taxis, especially for those who prefer the convenience of app-based services.

Walking is a viable option in Bocagrande, with its wide sidewalks and pleasant ocean views, although the heat and humidity can be intense, especially in the afternoon.

A surprising aspect of Cartagena’s transport is the availability of "chiva" buses, colorful, open-air buses that offer an entertaining way to see the city. These are more of a tourist attraction than a practical mode of transport but can be a fun way to spend an evening.

Each mode of transport has its quirks, but collectively, they allow for flexible and affordable movement around the city, making daily life in Cartagena both manageable and enjoyable.
