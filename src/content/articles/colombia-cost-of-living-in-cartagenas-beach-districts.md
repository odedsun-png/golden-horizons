# Colombia - Cost of Living in Cartagena’s Beach Districts — A Local's Perspective

Cartagena, Colombia, with its population of over one million, is a busy city known for its history and beautiful Caribbean beaches. However, the cost of living in its beach districts, such as Bocagrande and El Laguito, can be surprisingly affordable compared to similar coastal cities in the United States. With monthly expenses for a comfortable lifestyle ranging from $1,500 to $2,500, retirees can enjoy a beachfront lifestyle without breaking the bank. This article will help you understand the costs and lifestyle in Cartagena’s beach districts so you can decide if it's the right place for your retirement.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want an affordable beachfront lifestyle.
- **Monthly reality:** $1,500 to $2,500 for housing, utilities, and daily expenses.
- **Biggest advantage:** Low cost of living with access to beaches and city amenities.
- **Biggest warning:** Language barriers and adjusting to local healthcare systems.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Housing Costs in Bocagrande and El Laguito

In Bocagrande, a popular beach district in Cartagena, you can find a one-bedroom apartment for as low as $600 per month. If you prefer a more luxurious setting, a beachfront apartment with impressive views can cost around $1,200 to $1,500 per month. El Laguito, another desirable area, offers similar pricing, with apartments ranging from $500 to $1,300 depending on size and proximity to the beach. These districts are known for their high-rise buildings, many of which come with amenities like swimming pools and gyms.

While these prices are appealing, housing quality can vary significantly. Some older buildings may have issues with maintenance, so it's crucial to inspect properties thoroughly before signing a lease. Additionally, property management can be less responsive compared to what you might be used to in the U.S., so patience is often required.

## Daily Expenses and Lifestyle

Groceries and dining out in Cartagena are generally affordable. A meal at a local restaurant can cost as little as $3 to $5, while a three-course meal for two at a mid-range restaurant might set you back around $30. Groceries for a couple can range from $200 to $400 per month, depending on dietary preferences and whether you choose imported goods.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1718119617938-2a3b376fb7d6?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8Q29sb21iaWF8ZW58MHwwfHx8MTc3ODEyMjI2OXww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Transportation is another budget-friendly aspect of living in Cartagena. Public buses are widely used and cost less than $1 per ride. Taxis are also affordable, with a typical fare within the city center costing around $2 to $5. However, traffic can be heavy, especially during peak hours, so plan your outings accordingly.

One practical warning is the language barrier. While Cartagena is a tourist-friendly city, English is not widely spoken outside tourist areas. Learning basic Spanish can greatly enhance your daily interactions and experience.

## Healthcare Access and Considerations

Cartagena offers a variety of healthcare facilities, including Clínica Cartagena del Mar and Hospital Universitario del Caribe, which are accessible from the beach districts. While healthcare in Colombia is generally of good quality and significantly less expensive than in the U.S., private health insurance is recommended for expatriates to ensure access to the best facilities.

Retirees should be aware that while emergency services are available, they may not be as fast or as efficient as in the U.S. Familiarize yourself with the location of the nearest hospitals and clinics and have a plan in place for medical emergencies.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Before moving to Cartagena, verify your visa requirements. Colombia offers a retirement visa (M-11) that requires proof of a monthly income of at least three times the Colombian minimum wage, currently around $750. Consult the Colombian consulate or a qualified immigration attorney for the most accurate information.

Healthcare access is generally good, but private insurance is recommended to cover any gaps in the public system. Verify your insurance options and ensure they cover your needs adequately. As for taxes, U.S. citizens must continue to file taxes with the IRS. It's wise to consult a tax professional familiar with both U.S. and Colombian tax systems to avoid any unpleasant surprises.

Safety in Cartagena varies by neighborhood. While the beach districts are generally safe, remain vigilant and stay informed about local safety conditions. Walking alone at night in less populated areas is not recommended.
