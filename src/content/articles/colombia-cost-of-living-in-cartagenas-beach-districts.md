# Colombia - Cost of Living in Cartagena’s Beach Districts — A Local's Perspective

Cartagena, Colombia's coastal city, is known for its historical walled city and picturesque beaches. Yet, despite its allure, the reality of living here can be surprisingly affordable. For instance, the cost of living in Cartagena's beach districts can be as low as $1,500 per month for a single person, including rent, groceries, and utilities. This article will help you understand what your money can buy in the beach districts of Cartagena and provide a realistic picture of life here.

## What $1,500/Month Actually Buys in Bocagrande

Bocagrande, a neighborhood known for its high-rise buildings and proximity to the beach, offers a range of living options for retirees. With $1,500 a month, you can rent a modest one-bedroom apartment for approximately $600–$800, depending on the view and amenities. These apartments often come with access to a communal pool or gym, adding to the appeal of this active district.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1476514525535-07fb3b4ae5f1?w=1200)

Groceries in Bocagrande are quite reasonable, with local markets such as Mercado de Bazurto offering fresh produce at lower prices than supermarkets. Expect to spend around $200 a month on groceries if you prefer cooking at home. For dining out, numerous local eateries serve meals for under $10. However, if you enjoy dining at more upscale restaurants, like those along Avenida San Martín, budget for a bit more.

Utilities, including electricity, water, and gas, typically range from $50 to $100 monthly, depending on your usage and whether you run air conditioning frequently. Internet and phone services cost around $30 to $50. While this paints a rosy picture, it's important to acknowledge the limitations. Bocagrande can get quite busy, especially during the tourist season, which brings noise and congestion. Yet, many find the trade-off for beachside living worth it.

## Healthcare and Transportation: Staying Mobile and Healthy

Cartagena offers a range of healthcare options that are both accessible and affordable. The city is home to several reputable hospitals, such as Hospital Universitario del Caribe and Clínica Medihelp, both of which provide quality care. An English-speaking doctor’s visit can cost as little as $50. Prescription medications are also more affordable than in the United States, making healthcare a manageable expense for retirees.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1528360983277-13d401cdc186?w=1200)

Transportation in Cartagena is diverse, with options ranging from buses to taxis and the increasingly popular Transcaribe bus rapid transit system. A bus ticket costs about $0.70, while a taxi ride across town might set you back $4 to $6. For those who prefer a bit more flexibility, ride-sharing apps like Uber and Cabify are available, though slightly more expensive.

If you're considering bringing a car, be aware of the traffic congestion that can occur in the more touristic areas. Parking can also be a challenge in neighborhoods like Bocagrande, where space is at a premium. However, walking is a viable option for many, as the city’s flat terrain and scenic streets make it easy to get around on foot.

## Social Life and Activities: Beyond the Beaches

While Cartagena is famous for its beaches, there's much more to enjoy in this coastal city. The Getsemaní neighborhood, for example, is an active area with a thriving art scene and a variety of cultural activities. Here, you can engage with locals and fellow expats alike at places like Plaza de la Trinidad, where live music and performances are common.

For those interested in learning Spanish, Cartagena offers numerous language schools, such as Nueva Lengua and Centro Catalina. Classes range from $150 to $300 per month, depending on the frequency and class size. These schools provide an excellent opportunity to integrate into the local community and enhance your stay.

Despite the social opportunities, consider the climate. Cartagena is hot and humid year-round, which might be challenging for some. The rainy season, from May to November, can bring heavy downpours, though these often pass quickly. Still, the warm weather allows for year-round outdoor activities, from beach days to exploring the city's historic sites.
