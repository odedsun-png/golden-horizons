# Colombia - Cost of Living in Cartagena’s Beach Districts — A Local's Perspective

Cartagena's beach districts offer a surprisingly affordable lifestyle, with living costs significantly lower than in many U.S. cities. For instance, renting a one-bedroom apartment in Bocagrande, a popular neighborhood, typically costs around $600 a month. This article will guide you through what living in Cartagena's beach districts entails, from housing costs to daily life essentials.

## What $2,000/Month Actually Buys in Bocagrande

With a budget of $2,000 per month, life in Bocagrande can be both comfortable and enjoyable. You can secure a one-bedroom apartment for around $600, leaving you with $1,400 for other expenses. This neighborhood, known for its high-rise buildings and proximity to the beach, offers a variety of amenities within walking distance. Picture yourself enjoying a fresh seafood meal for under $10 at one of the local restaurants, or sipping coffee at a café for just $1.50.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1476514525535-07fb3b4ae5f1?w=1200)

For groceries, head to the Mercado de Bazurto, where fresh produce and local goods are available at a fraction of the cost you might expect in the U.S. A week's worth of groceries for two people can easily total around $50 if you shop wisely. Bocagrande also boasts several supermarkets, such as Carulla and Éxito, which offer a selection of imported and local products.

Transportation is another area where you can save. Cartagena's public transportation system, including buses and shared taxis, is affordable, with fares typically under $1. This, combined with the walkability of Bocagrande, means that you can minimize your transportation expenses.

However, the humidity and heat are factors to consider. Cartagena's climate can be challenging for those not accustomed to tropical weather. While air conditioning can mitigate this, it does increase electricity costs, which average around $100 a month if used regularly. Despite this, $2,000 a month can comfortably cover your needs, allowing you to enjoy the beachfront lifestyle without financial strain.

## Healthcare Options in Cartagena: A Closer Look

Healthcare in Cartagena is both accessible and affordable, which is a significant consideration for retirees. The city is home to several reputable hospitals and clinics, such as Hospital Universitario del Caribe and Clínica Medihelp Services. These facilities provide a range of services, from routine check-ups to specialized treatments, at costs significantly lower than in the United States.

For instance, a visit to a general practitioner might cost around $30, while more specialized services, like dental care, are also reasonably priced. Procedures such as teeth cleanings or fillings typically range from $20 to $50. Many doctors in Cartagena speak English, which can ease communication barriers. Additionally, pharmacies are widespread and medications are often available over-the-counter at a lower cost than in the U.S.

Health insurance is another important factor to consider. Many expats opt for local health insurance plans, which can cost between $50 and $100 per month, depending on the coverage level. These plans generally cover a wide range of services, including emergency care and consultations with specialists.

While the quality of care in Cartagena is high, not all facilities have the same level of equipment or specialization as those in larger cities like Bogotá. For complex medical conditions or surgeries, some may choose to travel to more specialized hospitals within Colombia. Nonetheless, for routine and urgent care, Cartagena's healthcare services are more than adequate and offer peace of mind for those considering retirement in this coastal city.

## Experiencing Daily Life in El Laguito

El Laguito, another beach district in Cartagena, offers a different vibe compared to Bocagrande. Known for its slightly quieter atmosphere, El Laguito is home to many expats and retirees who enjoy its laid-back lifestyle. Renting a one-bedroom apartment here costs around $500, offering another affordable option for those looking to retire in the area.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1718119617938-2a3b376fb7d6?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8Q29sb21iaWF8ZW58MHwwfHx8MTc3ODEyMjI2OXww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

One thing that might surprise you about El Laguito is the community spirit. Weekly markets and social gatherings at local spots like Plaza Bocagrande Centro Comercial bring people together, fostering a sense of belonging and connection. You can find fresh produce, local crafts, and even join yoga classes on the beach for a minimal fee.

Dining out in El Laguito is a pleasant experience, with a variety of local and international restaurants. A three-course meal for two at a mid-range restaurant might set you back just $30. Street food, including arepas and empanadas, is both delicious and inexpensive, perfect for a quick bite while exploring the neighborhood.

One assumption about living abroad is that you might feel isolated, but the expat community in El Laguito is welcoming and active. There are regular meetups and events that provide opportunities for socializing and making new friends. However, language can still be a barrier. While many locals speak some English, learning basic Spanish can enhance your experience and help with daily interactions.

Safety is another factor to consider. While Cartagena is generally safe, like any city, it's wise to stay aware of your surroundings and avoid certain areas at night. That said, El Laguito is considered a safe neighborhood, with a visible police presence and a strong sense of community.
