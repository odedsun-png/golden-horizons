## Quick Taste

LapLap in Vanuatu is a traditional dish crafted from grated root vegetables like taro or yam, mixed with coconut milk, and wrapped in banana leaves before being baked in an earth oven. In Port Vila, LapLap is more accessible and convenient for tourists, offering a variety of accompaniments, while the versions in local villages provide a more traditional flavor and experience. For first-time visitors, Port Vila is ideal due to its ease of access and variety, whereas the villages are perfect for those seeking a deeper cultural experience and larger portions.

## The Story Behind the Dish

LapLap has deep roots in Vanuatu's history, serving as a symbol of community and tradition. This dish is often prepared for special occasions and communal gatherings, where the process of making it is as important as the dish itself. The preparation involves grating the root crops, mixing them with coconut milk, and carefully wrapping them in banana leaves before baking them in an underground oven known as an "umu."

Historically, LapLap has been a centerpiece in ceremonies and celebrations, connecting families and communities. The method of cooking in an earth oven is a practice that has been passed down through generations, preserving the flavors and techniques of the past. This dish not only nourishes the body but also strengthens social bonds, making it an integral part of Vanuatu's cultural identity.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

In Port Vila, the cost of LapLap can vary depending on the location and the accompaniments.
