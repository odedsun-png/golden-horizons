## Quick Taste

LapLap in Vanuatu is a traditional dish made from grated root vegetables like yam, taro, or banana, wrapped in banana leaves, and cooked in an underground oven. In Port Vila, you'll find LapLap that's more convenient for tourists, while local villages offer a version that's closer to its original flavor. For those seeking value, Port Vila offers easier access and more dining options, whereas the villages provide a more traditional experience with larger portions and deeper flavors. First-time visitors should try the Port Vila version for convenience and dietary options, while those looking for a traditional taste should head to the villages.

LapLap holds a special place in Vanuatu's food culture, often served at ceremonial occasions and communal gatherings. The dish is typically accompanied by coconut cream, which adds a creamy texture, and sometimes a side of fresh seafood or pork. Visitors will encounter a hearty, earthy flavor profile, with the root vegetables providing a starchy base and the banana leaves infusing a subtle smokiness. Variations exist, with some versions incorporating meat or fish directly into the mix. When trying LapLap for the first time, expect a warm, comforting dish that speaks to the heart of Vanuatu's communal dining traditions.

## The Story Behind the Dish

LapLap is more than just a meal; it is a reflection of Vanuatu's communal lifestyle and deep-rooted traditions. Historically, LapLap has been a staple dish for the Ni-Vanuatu people, made using locally available ingredients and traditional cooking methods. The preparation of LapLap is often a community affair, with family members and neighbors coming together to grate the root vegetables and prepare the banana leaves.

The dish is traditionally cooked in an underground oven, known as an "umu," where hot stones are used to create a consistent, even heat. This method of cooking not only preserves the nutritional value of the ingredients but also imparts a flavor that is difficult to replicate with modern cooking techniques. LapLap's origins can be traced back to the island's earliest inhabitants, who relied on the abundant root vegetables and coconuts as their primary food sources. Over time, the dish has evolved, incorporating new ingredients and techniques, but its core essence remains unchanged.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

When it comes to cost, LapLap is generally affordable, with prices in Port Vila ranging from 500 to 1,000 Vatu depending on the portion size and additional ingredients. In the villages, LapLap is often prepared for communal gatherings, so visitors may find themselves invited to share a meal at little to no cost, though it is customary to offer a small contribution or gift in return.

Portion sizes can vary, with larger servings typically found in the villages where the dish is meant to be shared among many. In Port Vila, smaller individual portions are more common, catering to tourists seeking a quick and convenient meal. When ordering LapLap, it is advisable to inquire about the ingredients, as some versions may include meat or fish, which can affect the taste and texture.

For those with dietary restrictions, it is important to communicate any allergies or preferences to the vendor or host. While LapLap is naturally gluten-free, the inclusion of certain meats or seafood may not suit all diets. When in doubt, ask for a vegetarian version, which typically consists of root vegetables and coconut cream.
