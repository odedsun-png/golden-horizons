# LGBTQ - Retiring as an LGBTQ American in Europe: Safest Countries — A Local's Perspective

In the 2021 Global Peace Index, Iceland ranked as the most peaceful country in the world, and it also consistently ranks high for LGBTQ+ rights and acceptance. For LGBTQ Americans considering retirement abroad, choosing a country that offers both safety and a welcoming community is crucial. This article will help you evaluate some of the safest European countries for LGBTQ retirees, considering factors such as legal protections, healthcare access, and cultural acceptance.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want a safe and accepting environment for LGBTQ individuals.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to spend $2,500–$4,000, depending on lifestyle and location.
- **Biggest advantage:** Strong legal protections and cultural acceptance in many European countries.
- **Biggest warning:** Language barriers and varying healthcare access can be challenges.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Iceland: A Haven of Peace and Acceptance

With a population of just over 366,000, Iceland offers a small but welcoming community for LGBTQ retirees. Reykjavik, the capital city, is known for its active LGBTQ scene, including annual events like Reykjavik Pride. The cost of living in Iceland can be high, with average monthly expenses for a retiree ranging from $3,000 to $4,000, including rent, food, and healthcare.

Iceland's healthcare system is robust and accessible, with universal coverage for residents. However, private insurance may be necessary for certain services not covered by the public system. The language barrier can be a concern, as Icelandic is the official language, although English is widely spoken.

Despite its high cost of living, Iceland's strong legal protections for LGBTQ individuals and its peaceful, inclusive society make it an attractive option for retirees seeking a safe and accepting environment.

## Spain: Sun, Culture, and LGBTQ Rights

Spain, with its interesting history and diverse culture, is another top choice for LGBTQ retirees. Cities like Madrid and Barcelona are known for their active LGBTQ communities and annual events such as Madrid Pride, one of the largest in Europe. The cost of living in Spain varies significantly between cities, with monthly expenses for a retiree typically ranging from $2,500 to $3,500.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1645286636903-890ce32aa287?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8U3dlZGVufGVufDB8MHx8fDE3ODQyNzc2NTd8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Healthcare in Spain is of high quality, with both public and private options. The public health system provides excellent care, but private insurance may be beneficial for quicker access to specialists. Spanish is the primary language, and while English is spoken in tourist areas, language classes might be helpful.

Spain's legal framework is supportive of LGBTQ rights, with same-sex marriage legalized since 2005. However, retirees should be aware of regional differences in acceptance, particularly in more rural areas.

## The Netherlands: Progressive and Inclusive

Known for its progressive policies and inclusive society, the Netherlands is another excellent option for LGBTQ retirees. Amsterdam, in particular, is renowned for its LGBTQ-friendly atmosphere and events like Amsterdam Pride. The cost of living in the Netherlands is moderate, with retirees spending around $2,800 to $3,800 monthly.

The Dutch healthcare system is highly regarded, with mandatory private health insurance ensuring access to a wide range of services. While Dutch is the official language, English is widely spoken, making daily life easier for expatriates.

Despite its many advantages, the Netherlands' climate can be a downside for some, with frequent rain and cool temperatures. Retirees should also be prepared for the high cost of housing, particularly in Amsterdam.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

When retiring in Europe, healthcare is a primary concern. Countries like Iceland, Spain, and the Netherlands offer robust healthcare systems, but the specifics can vary. Retirees should verify whether they need private health insurance in addition to public coverage.

Visa requirements are another critical consideration. Each country has its own rules for residency and retirement visas, and confirming these before making any commitments is crucial. Consulting with immigration professionals can help manage these complexities.

Tax obligations can be tricky, especially when dealing with U.S. tax requirements alongside local taxes. Retirees should consult with tax professionals to understand their liabilities in both countries.

Safety and walkability are significant factors, particularly for LGBTQ retirees. While the countries mentioned are generally safe and accepting, researching specific neighborhoods and communities is wise to ensure a comfortable living environment.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Overlooking the language barrier: Consider language classes or learning apps.
- Ignoring healthcare requirements: Research both public and private healthcare options.
- Underestimating living costs: Ensure your budget accounts for local expenses.
- Failing to check visa requirements: Verify residency rules before moving.
- Neglecting tax obligations: Consult with a tax professional for dual taxation issues.
- Assuming universal acceptance: Research local attitudes and communities.
- Forgetting to verify housing options: Investigate rental markets and lease terms.
