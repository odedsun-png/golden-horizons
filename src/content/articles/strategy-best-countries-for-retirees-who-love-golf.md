# Strategy - The Best Countries for Retirees Who Love Golf — A Local's Perspective

Spain boasts over 400 golf courses, making it one of the top golf destinations globally. With its mild Mediterranean climate, retirees can enjoy the sport year-round. This article will help you decide if Spain, along with other countries, is the right place for your retirement golf dreams.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want year-round golfing opportunities.
- **Monthly reality:** $2,000–$3,500 for living expenses, including golf memberships.
- **Biggest advantage:** Access to numerous high-quality golf courses.
- **Biggest warning:** Language barriers and cultural adjustments.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Spain: A Golfer's Paradise

Spain is home to the renowned Costa del Sol, which alone offers over 70 golf courses. The Real Club Valderrama, located in Sotogrande, is often ranked among the top courses in Europe. Monthly living costs for retirees in Spain can range from $2,000 to $3,500, depending on the region and lifestyle. Housing in popular golfing areas like Marbella can be pricey, but more affordable options exist in less tourist-heavy zones.

Healthcare in Spain is robust, with public and private options. The public healthcare system is accessible to residents, including retirees, but private insurance is recommended for expedited services. Language can be a hurdle in smaller towns, so some Spanish language skills are beneficial. While Spain offers excellent golfing, the cost of living can be higher in prime locations, and adjusting to the local lifestyle may take time.

## Portugal: Affordable and Golf-Friendly

Portugal's Algarve region is famous for its golf courses, with over 40 options available. Courses like Monte Rei Golf & Country Club are renowned for their quality and scenery. The cost of living in Portugal is generally lower than in Spain, with monthly expenses for retirees averaging $1,800 to $2,800. Housing is more affordable, especially in areas outside Lisbon and Porto.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1497005367839-6e852de72767?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8U3RyYXRlZ3l8ZW58MHwwfHx8MTc4NDUzNjgxNnww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Portugal's healthcare system is considered one of the best in Europe, with both public and private options. Retirees often opt for private insurance to avoid long wait times. English is widely spoken in the Algarve, easing the transition for American retirees. However, the popularity of the Algarve means it can be crowded during peak seasons, which might detract from the tranquility some retirees seek.

## Thailand: Exotic Golfing on a Budget

Thailand offers a distinctive golfing experience, with over 250 courses spread across the country. Chiang Mai and Hua Hin are popular among retirees for their affordable living costs and quality golf courses. Monthly expenses in Thailand can range from $1,200 to $2,000, making it an attractive option for budget-conscious retirees.

Healthcare in Thailand is of high quality, with many hospitals offering international standards. Private insurance is necessary for retirees, as the public system is not accessible to non-citizens. The language barrier can be significant, especially in rural areas, but English is commonly spoken in tourist regions. The tropical climate is ideal for golf but can be challenging for those unaccustomed to high humidity and heat.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Retirees should verify healthcare access and private insurance needs in each country. Spain and Portugal offer public healthcare to residents, but private insurance is advisable. Thailand requires private insurance for non-citizens.

Visa or residency rules vary: Spain and Portugal offer retirement visas, while Thailand has a "retirement visa" for those over 50. Verify U.S. and local tax obligations, as double taxation treaties may affect your finances. Safety is generally good in these countries, but be aware of petty crime, especially in tourist areas.

## Common Mistakes Retirees Should Avoid

- Underestimating the cost of living in prime golf areas.
- Failing to learn basic language skills.
- Ignoring the need for private health insurance.
- Overlooking visa and residency requirements.
- Assuming all areas are equally safe.
- Not considering climate impacts on health and lifestyle.
- Forgetting to account for cultural differences.

## Before You Move Checklist

- Verify visa or residency rules.
- Research healthcare access and private insurance.
- Understand tax obligations in both the U.S. and abroad.
- Confirm rent and lease terms.
- Assess walkability and transportation options.
- Evaluate safety and local crime rates.
- Consider language barriers and cultural adjustments.
- Plan for emergency access and healthcare needs.
- Budget for golf memberships and related expenses.
- Investigate local community and social opportunities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the cost of living in Spain for a retiree who loves golf?

Living in Spain can cost between $2,000 and $3,500 monthly, depending on lifestyle and location. Golf memberships and housing in prime areas can increase expenses.

### Is healthcare accessible in Portugal for retirees?

Yes, Portugal offers a robust healthcare system. Many retirees opt for private insurance to avoid long wait times and ensure comprehensive coverage.

### How does Thailand's climate affect golfing?

Thailand's tropical climate allows for year-round golfing but can be challenging due to heat and humidity. Retirees should consider their health and comfort levels.
