## What a Tamal Is

Tamale comes from the Nahuatl word "tamalli," which means "wrapped up." The dough is spread on a corn husk or a banana leaf, filled and cooked in steam. Fillings include salsas, meats, fruit and chocolate, so you can find savory and sweet versions.

You do not eat the husk or the leaf. Unwrap it first.

## Why Families Make Them Together

Making tamales takes many hands, and Wikipedia describes the work as labor-intensive and communal. Tamales also have a long place in Mexican festivals. The Florentine Codex records tamales served to honor the birth of a child, and Fray Bernardino de Sahagún wrote that the Mexica ate them on special occasions.

The Candelaria link is a rule of the table. Families eat rosca on January 6, and the person who finds the baby Jesus figurine is expected to provide the tamales on February 2. We found no verified count for how many a family makes in a day, so we do not quote one.

## How Locals Eat Them

Tamales are a comfort food that people eat at breakfast or dinner, often with hot atole or champurrado, according to Wikipedia. In Mexico City, a tamal tucked inside a bolillo roll is called a guajolota. El Universal says it is often paired with atole and is described as the most complete breakfast.

## Regional Wrappers

Wikipedia says tamales are usually wrapped in corn husks or plantain leaves, depending on the region. In tropical areas such as Oaxaca, Chiapas, Veracruz and the Yucatán, banana or plantain leaves replace corn husks. The Yucatán has large, square banana-leaf tamales called pibs, often about 15 inches long.

**What the evidence says:** Wikipedia says the wrapper depends on the region, with banana or plantain leaves in tropical areas such as Oaxaca and the Yucatán. El Universal describes the corn dough being spread on a corn or banana leaf.

**What to check next:** Order one tamal in each wrapper and note which dough and filling you prefer.

### Buying versus joining a family batch

**What the evidence says:** Tamale-making is labor-intensive and communal, usually done by several people. A shop or street vendor saves you the work, but we could not verify what any vendor charges.

**What to check next:** Ask a neighbor or host whether you can help with one batch before February 2, and offer to pay for the masa.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When do Mexican families eat tamales?

Families gather to eat tamales on Candelaria, February 2, and whoever finds the baby Jesus figurine in the Rosca de Reyes on January 6 provides them ([mexiconewsdaily.com](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/culture/tamales-baby-jesus-mexico-candelaria-traditions/))

### How much does a tamal cost in Mexico?

We could not verify a per-tamal price, but Mexico City's inexpensive restaurant meal averages $10.44 and a cappuccino $3.57, based on Numbeo's November 24, 2026 listing ([numbeo.com](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Mexico-City?displayCurrency=USD))

### What are tamales wrapped in?

Tamales are made from corn dough spread on 1 of 2 wrappers, a corn leaf or a banana leaf, and cooked with steam ([eluniversal.com.mx](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/english/tamales-icon-mexican-cuisine/))

### How old is the tamale?

Wikipedia gives a range of as early as 8000 to 5000 BC for tamales in Mesoamerica, and notes that other researchers suggest around 100 AD, so the date is debated ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale))

### Is Mexico safe for older visitors?

The U.S. State Department rates Mexico Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution, due to terrorism, crime and kidnapping, in an advisory dated May 28, 2026, and notes that conditions vary from state to state ([cadataapi.state.gov](https://cadataapi.state.gov/api/TravelAdvisories))
