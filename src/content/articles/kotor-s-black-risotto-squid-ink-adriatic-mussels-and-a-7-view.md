## Quick Taste

As you settle into a quaint seaside eatery in Kotor, Montenegro, the waiter places a steaming plate of black risotto before you, its dark, glossy grains glistening under the Adriatic sun. This dish, known locally as "crni rižot," is a blend of squid ink and tender Adriatic mussels, offering a briny, umami punch that is both comforting and intriguing. Accompanied by a crisp glass of local white wine, the risotto is a feast for the senses, with its unique color and deep, oceanic flavor. Expect to pay around EUR 6-8 (about $7-9), making it an affordable enjoynce with a view that's priceless.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Black risotto has its roots along the Dalmatian coast, where seafood is a staple of the local diet. The dish is traditionally made with cuttlefish or squid ink, which imparts its signature color and a subtle, earthy taste. This culinary tradition dates back centuries, reflecting the region's deep connection to the sea. In Kotor, the dish is often prepared using fresh mussels harvested from the nearby Adriatic waters, adding a local twist to this time-honored recipe. The use of ink in cooking is believed to have originated as a way to utilize every part of the catch, a testament to the resourcefulness of coastal communities.

## How Locals Eat It

In Kotor, locals enjoy black risotto as a leisurely lunch or dinner, often shared among friends and family. It's typically served with a side of crusty bread to soak up the savory sauce, and a simple salad of fresh greens to balance the richness of the dish. Dining al fresco is a popular choice, allowing you to enjoy the meal while taking in the striking views of Kotor Bay. For many residents, this dish is a reminder of childhood, evoking memories of family gatherings and seaside picnics. Unlike tourists who might seek out the dish for its novelty, locals appreciate it for its comforting familiarity.

## Where to Find It

While Kotor's waterfront restaurants are popular spots to enjoy black risotto, venturing into the town's narrow, cobbled streets can lead you to lesser-known spots favored by locals. Small family-run konobas, or taverns, often serve up more traditional versions at slightly lower prices than the lively tourist spots along the marina. For those looking to recreate the dish at home, the local market is a treasure trove of fresh ingredients. Here, you can purchase freshly caught mussels and squid ink, as well as locally grown herbs and vegetables to complete your meal.

## Regional Variations

While the basic components of black risotto remain consistent, variations can be found throughout the Adriatic region. In some areas, chefs might add a splash of local wine or a hint of garlic to the mix, enhancing the dish's depth of flavor. In neighboring Croatia, for example, the dish might include cuttlefish instead of squid, offering a slightly different texture. Meanwhile, in Italy, a similar dish known as "risotto al nero di seppia" often features a creamier consistency, achieved by stirring in butter or cheese. Each variation offers a different take on this coastal dish.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

When ordering black risotto in Kotor, expect a generous portion that can easily satisfy a hearty appetite. Prices typically range from EUR 6-8 (about $7-9), depending on the restaurant and its location. For the best value, consider dining during lunch hours when many eateries offer set menus that include a starter, main course, and a drink. Don't hesitate to ask the waiter for recommendations on wine pairings; a crisp Montenegrin white wine complements the dish beautifully. If you're dining with a group, consider ordering a few different seafood dishes to share, allowing everyone to sample a variety of local flavors.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before diving into a plate of black risotto, be prepared for its distinctive appearance, which might be off-putting to some at first glance. However, the taste is far from intimidating, offering a savory, slightly briny flavor that's both satisfying and complex. It's also worth noting that the squid ink can temporarily stain your teeth, so you might want to keep a glass of water handy. For those with dietary restrictions, be aware that the dish is typically made with seafood stock, which may not be suitable for vegetarians or those with shellfish allergies.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is assuming that all black risottos are created equal. While many restaurants in Kotor offer this dish, quality can vary significantly. To ensure an traditional experience, seek out eateries known for their seafood and avoid those with overly touristy menus. Another misstep is skipping the local wine pairing, which can enhance the meal. Lastly, some tourists might shy away from the dish due to its color, missing out on a truly local dining experience. Embrace the unexpected and you'll likely be rewarded with a memorable meal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main ingredient in black risotto?
The main ingredient in black risotto is squid ink, which gives the dish its distinctive black color and unique flavor. It is typically combined with seafood such as mussels or cuttlefish.

### Is black risotto spicy?
Black risotto is not typically spicy. It has a savory, slightly briny flavor profile, with the richness of the squid ink balanced by the freshness of the seafood and herbs.

### Can I find vegetarian black risotto in Kotor?
While traditional black risotto is made with seafood, some restaurants may offer vegetarian versions. It's best to inquire with the staff if you have dietary restrictions.

### How should I eat black risotto?
Black risotto is best enjoyed with a side of crusty bread and a glass of white wine. Use the bread to soak up the flavorful sauce, and savor each bite slowly to appreciate the complex flavors.
