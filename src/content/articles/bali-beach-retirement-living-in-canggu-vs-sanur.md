# Bali - Beach Retirement Living in Canggu vs Sanur — A Local's Perspective

Bali's population of over 4.2 million people includes a growing number of retirees seeking a tropical lifestyle. Among the many locales on the island, Canggu and Sanur stand out for different reasons. Canggu is a center for digital nomads and surfers, while Sanur offers a more traditional and tranquil setting. This article will help you decide which of these two contrasting areas might suit your retirement needs.

## Canggu: What $2,000/Month Actually Buys

In Canggu, a budget of $2,000 per month can go a long way. You can rent a modern, two-bedroom villa with a pool in Batu Bolong, a popular area with expats, for around $1,200. This neighborhood is known for its active cafe and restaurant scene, with places like Crate Café and Deus Ex Machina serving as local favorites. Your remaining budget can comfortably cover utilities, groceries, and occasional dining out, thanks to the relatively low cost of living.

However, Canggu's appeal comes with its own set of challenges. The influx of tourists and expats can make the area feel crowded, especially during the peak season. Traffic congestion is a common issue, with Jalan Pantai Batu Bolong often resembling a parking lot. If you're used to a more relaxed pace, this might be a hurdle.

On the brighter side, Canggu offers plenty of opportunities to stay active. Yoga and fitness studios are abundant, and the nearby beaches are perfect for morning walks or learning to surf. For healthcare, the Siloam Hospitals Bali in nearby Kuta is highly recommended by expats. It's a modern facility with English-speaking staff, which can be reassuring in times of need.

## Sanur: The Quieter Alternative to Canggu

Sanur presents a different vibe altogether. With $2,000 per month, you can rent a spacious, traditional Balinese-style home in the heart of Sanur, near Jalan Danau Tamblingan, for about $900. This leaves plenty left over for dining at local warungs or enjoying an occasional meal at upscale restaurants like Mezzanine Bar & Restaurant. Sanur's cost of living is generally lower than Canggu's, which means your dollar can stretch further.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1449480881392-716d0ea24a47?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8SW5kb25lc2lhfGVufDB8MHx8fDE3NzgxNjE1NjR8MA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Sanur is known for its 4-mile-long beachfront promenade, ideal for cycling or leisurely walks. It’s a quieter spot compared to the busy beaches of Canggu, and the ocean here is calmer, making it suitable for swimming. The local community is tight-knit and friendly, offering a more traditional Bali experience.

Healthcare in Sanur is also accessible, with the Bali Mandara Hospital providing good quality care. While it may not be as modern as facilities in more tourist-heavy areas, it covers most needs sufficiently.

One limitation in Sanur is the slower pace of life, which might not be ideal for everyone. If you're looking for a more dynamic environment with plenty of activities and nightlife, Canggu might be more your speed.

## Transport and Connectivity: Getting Around Canggu and Sanur

When it comes to getting around, Canggu and Sanur offer distinct experiences. In Canggu, motorbikes are the most practical and popular mode of transport. Renting one can cost as little as $60 per month. The roads can be chaotic, and getting through the busy streets requires a bit of courage and adaptability. For those less inclined to ride, apps like Grab and Gojek make it easy to hail a ride.

Sanur, by contrast, offers a much more relaxed driving environment. Traffic is lighter, and the roads are less congested, which makes cycling a viable and enjoyable option. Public transport is limited in both areas, so most expats rely on scooters or cars for their daily commute.

For internet connectivity, both Canggu and Sanur have decent options. Canggu, being a hotspot for digital nomads, has a range of co-working spaces and cafes with fast Wi-Fi. Sanur, while not as tech-centric, still offers reliable internet access in most residential areas and cafes.

For those with family visiting or planning trips back to the U.S., Ngurah Rai International Airport is approximately 45 minutes from Canggu and 30 minutes from Sanur, depending on traffic.

While Canggu's high-energy environment might be enticing, Sanur's peaceful setting offers a stark contrast. Both have their distinct appeal, depending on whether you value a busy scene or a laid-back atmosphere.
