# Bali - Beach Retirement Living in Canggu vs Sanur — A Local's Perspective

In Bali, the cost of living for a comfortable lifestyle in Canggu can be around $1,500 per month, while in Sanur, it's closer to $1,200. This is not just hearsay; these numbers reflect actual expenses from groceries to leisure activities. Canggu and Sanur offer distinct living experiences for retirees, each with its own perks and quirks. This article will help you weigh the differences and decide which might suit your retirement dreams.

## What $1,500/Month Actually Buys in Canggu

With a budget of $1,500 per month, you can cover most of your living expenses comfortably in Canggu. This area, known for its surf-friendly beaches and trendy cafes, has become a hotspot for digital nomads and retirees alike. A one-bedroom villa complete with a private pool in the Berawa area typically rents for about $700 to $900 per month. If you're more budget-conscious, smaller apartments or shared living spaces can be found for as low as $400 per month.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/33628177/pexels-photo-33628177.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Groceries in Canggu are a mix of local and imported goods. A weekly market visit to Pasar Canggu for fresh fruits and vegetables might cost you around $15. Imported goods, however, can be pricey. A block of imported cheese can set you back $10, so it's wise to adapt to local tastes when possible. Dining out is affordable, with a meal at a local warung costing around $3, while a dinner at a more upscale place like Milk & Madu can range from $10 to $20.

Transportation costs are relatively low. Renting a scooter is the most common way to get around, costing about $60 per month. However, traffic congestion is a real issue, especially during peak tourist seasons, and the roads can be challenging for those not accustomed to two-wheeled travel.

One limitation you might face is the increasing number of tourists and expats, which can make Canggu feel crowded at times. The influx has also led to a rise in the cost of living over the years. Yet, the lively atmosphere, coupled with a strong sense of community, makes it an attractive option for those seeking a vibrant retirement.

## Sanur: A Quieter, More Affordable Option

In Sanur, a monthly budget of $1,200 can go a long way. Known for its laid-back vibe and long stretches of beach, Sanur attracts retirees looking for a slower pace of life. A two-bedroom house in the Sindhu Beach area can be rented for around $500 to $700 per month. If you prefer something even more modest, local-style accommodations can be found for about $300.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/18396400/pexels-photo-18396400.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Grocery shopping in Sanur is generally cheaper than in Canggu, especially if you stick to local markets like Pasar Sindu. A basket of fresh produce might cost $10 weekly, and local fish is a bargain at $2 per pound. Dining out is also affordable, with local eateries offering meals for around $2 to $5.

Sanur is more pedestrian-friendly compared to Canggu. You can easily walk or cycle along the beachfront promenade, which stretches for about 5 kilometers. Public transport options are limited, but taxis and ride-hailing services like Grab are readily available and affordable.

One notable advantage of Sanur is its quieter atmosphere. Unlike Canggu, which is bustling with cafes and nightlife, Sanur offers a more peaceful environment, ideal for those who prefer tranquility. However, this might also be seen as a downside if you crave a more dynamic social scene.

Healthcare facilities are adequate, with several clinics and the Bali Mandara Hospital nearby. It's essential to have health insurance that covers medical evacuation, as more complex medical needs might require travel to Singapore or Australia.

## Lifestyle and Community: Finding Your Fit

Both Canggu and Sanur offer vibrant expat communities, but they cater to different lifestyles. Canggu's community is younger, with many digital nomads and creative professionals. Events like yoga retreats and surf competitions are common, and you'll find plenty of opportunities to engage in activities that promote a healthy lifestyle.

In contrast, Sanur's expat community is generally older, with many retirees choosing to spend their days enjoying the beach or participating in cultural activities. The local community is welcoming, and there are plenty of opportunities to get involved in volunteer work or local cultural events.

A surprising aspect of living in Bali is the strong sense of spirituality and tradition that permeates everyday life. You'll often encounter ceremonies and offerings, and participating in local customs can enrich your experience. While language barriers can exist, many locals speak English, particularly in areas with a high expat presence.

One challenge you might face is the tropical climate. Bali is hot and humid year-round, with a rainy season from November to March. This weather can be a significant adjustment if you're used to a more temperate climate.

In conclusion, whether you choose Canggu or Sanur, Bali offers a unique blend of affordability, community, and lifestyle. Canggu might appeal to those seeking a more active, social retirement, while Sanur is ideal for those who prefer peace and quiet. Whatever your preference, both options provide an opportunity to enjoy a fulfilling retirement in a beautiful setting.
