# What $1,300 a Month Really Buys You in Bali: Realistic cost anchors for a retiree in Denpasar (public data, Sep 2026)

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** A $1,300 monthly budget can cover a modest Denpasar routine only if housing stays toward the lower end and daily extras remain measured. A one-bedroom in Denpasar’s city center averages $385, utilities and internet run about $49, and a monthly public-transport pass is about $23. But crowd-sourced one-person estimates land around $1,420-$1,477, leaving a likely shortfall of $120-$177 before personal surprises.

A morning coffee for about $2. Fresh tomatoes, eggs, and milk for a few dollars. An 8-kilometer taxi ride for roughly $4. Those small numbers are what make Bali so tempting when you are looking at retirement income and wondering how far it could stretch.

But the coffee is not the whole story. A Denpasar one-bedroom averages about $385 in the city center, and published one-person estimates run above the $1,300 headline budget. Bali can still offer a pleasant, modest rhythm—but only if the apartment, the restaurant habits, and the practical details all cooperate.

## What $1,300 Has to Cover in Denpasar

The apartment decides whether this budget has any breathing room. A one-bedroom in Denpasar’s city center averages $385 per month. Outside the center, published listings range from roughly $256 to $443—a useful range, but not a guarantee that the next available apartment will be clean, furnished, convenient, and priced at the low end.

The larger-apartment figures tell a different story. An 85-square-meter furnished apartment is listed around $851 in a normal area and about $1,327 in an expensive area. Those are not like-for-like comparisons with a compact one-bedroom, but they make the point: Bali is not one price. Space, furnishings, and location can consume the budget quickly.

Utilities and internet together run about $49 per month, while a monthly public-transport pass is about $23. Those are encouraging anchors. The complication is groceries: the available public data offers individual food prices and a broader one-person estimate, but not a clean, reliable grocery-only total for a retiree. It would be false precision to manufacture one.

## A Tuesday of Coffee, Market Food, and Dinner Out

**A Typical Day in Denpasar**

Picture a Tuesday like this: start with a cappuccino at an expat-area café for about $2, then pick up a kilogram of tomatoes for about $1 at the market. A basic business-district lunch is about $9. Later, an 8-kilometer taxi trip costs roughly $4.

For dinner, a modest neighborhood-pub meal for two is about $14. Choose an Italian-style dinner in an expat area instead, and the bill can rise to about $36. That one decision captures the monthly challenge neatly: Bali can feel wonderfully affordable, but expat-area habits can quietly turn a careful budget into a tight one.

At home, a liter of milk is about $1, a dozen eggs about $2, and a two-liter bottle of cola about $1. These are not a full grocery budget, but they are useful signs of what a rented kitchen may make possible.

## Where the Budget Can Slip Away

Housing is the obvious lever. Air conditioning, taxis, and restaurant habits are the quieter ones. The $49 utilities-and-internet figure is an average, not a ceiling; a home using air conditioning heavily can cost more.

Then there is the question of space. The $851 normal-area reference and $1,327 expensive-area reference apply to furnished 85-square-meter apartments, not compact one-bedrooms. If you want a larger furnished home, frequent restaurant meals, and taxis in place of public transport, the published $1,420-$1,477 estimate is a more realistic starting point than $1,300.

Healthcare belongs in a separate line of your planning notebook. A short private-clinic visit is listed around $17, but the available evidence does not price insurance coverage, hospital care, prescriptions, or serious medical treatment. Those details matter more than the price of a routine visit.

The same caution applies to residency. Indonesia has long-stay pathways, but current rules should come from Imigrasi or an Indonesian consulate—not an old retirement-visa summary found online.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can one American retiree really live in Bali on $1,300 a month?

The available public data suggests it is possible only with restraint, not certainty. A city-center one-bedroom averages $385, while utilities and internet average about $49 and public transport about $23. The broader crowd-sourced one-person estimate is $1,420-$1,477, so the $1,300 target is likely short by $120-$177 before major surprises. (Expatistan)

### Where is the least expensive published one-bedroom option in Denpasar?

The lowest published one-bedroom range is outside Denpasar’s center, at about $256-$443 per month. The city-center average is about $385, meaning the ranges overlap rather than creating a clear bargain district. A larger furnished 85-square-meter apartment is listed at about $851 in a normal area, so apartment size matters as much as location. (Numbeo)

### What do ordinary groceries cost in Denpasar?

Current crowd-sourced examples place a liter of milk at about $1, a dozen eggs at about $2, and a kilogram of tomatoes at about $1. These are useful shopping anchors, but they are not a complete monthly grocery budget for a retiree. Prices can also shift by vendor, season, and exchange-rate movement. (Expatistan)

### Is healthcare included in the $1,300 Bali budget?

No reliable all-in healthcare allowance is available in the current evidence. A short private-clinic visit is listed at about $17, but insurance premiums, hospital treatment, prescriptions, and coverage limits are not safely captured by that number. Foreign residents commonly arrange local private or international coverage and should request current terms directly from providers. (Expatistan)

### Can Americans use a retirement visa to stay in Bali long term?

Indonesia has long-stay routes for foreign residents, including retirement-related concepts and other residence options. Exact KITAS, Second Home Visa, income, insurance, and document rules can change, so this article does not state a fixed threshold or approval timeline. Before spending more than 30 days planning, contact Imigrasi or an Indonesian consulate for the current route available to an American retiree.
