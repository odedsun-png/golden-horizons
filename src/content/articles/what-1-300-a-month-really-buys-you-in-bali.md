# What $1,300 a Month Buys You in Bali: A Practical Retiree Budget by Neighborhood

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** A solo retiree can potentially make $1,300 work in lower-cost parts of Bali, where a one-bedroom outside the city centre averages $657, groceries run about $280, utilities about $73, and internet about $19. That basic housing-and-home total is $1,029 before transport, meals out, healthcare, and personal spending. In the city centre, a one-bedroom averages $1,036, leaving little room for the rest of life on the same budget. Bali can work, but the neighborhood does most of the work.

A $379 difference in rent can decide whether Bali feels like a fresh start or a monthly squeeze. That is the gap between the available one-bedroom averages outside Bali’s city centre and in it—and on a $1,300 budget, it matters more than almost any other choice you make.

Picture a morning in Denpasar: coffee at a warung, motorbikes moving through the heat, fruit for the kitchen, and an afternoon errand reached with a short local ride. The small purchases can be remarkably gentle. The question is whether they still feel gentle after rent, groceries, utilities, and the practical realities of healthcare and long-stay residency enter the picture.

## The Neighborhood Choice That Changes the Entire Budget

The rent is the hinge point. You may find coffee for a few dollars and short rides for less than $1, but neither matters much if housing has already consumed the room your budget needs for ordinary life.

### City-centre convenience comes at a price

A one-bedroom in Bali’s city centre averages $1,036 per month. Add the $280 grocery basket, $73 in utilities, and $19 for internet, and the basic total reaches $1,408—before a single café stop, ride, lunch out, medical expense, or household purchase.

That does not mean city-centre life is impossible. It means $1,300 is not a comfortable planning number there.

### Outside the centre, the budget can breathe

Outside the city centre, a one-bedroom averages $657. Pair that with the same $280 grocery basket, $73 utilities, and $19 internet, and your core home budget comes to $1,029.

That leaves $271. It is not an extravagant cushion, but it can cover some local rides, occasional meals out, phone service, household basics, and the small expenses that turn up in any new home.

Denpasar is the practical reference point for the available utility data: an 85-square-meter apartment for two people showed utilities of about $73. A smaller one-bedroom may use less, though daily air-conditioning can move the number quickly.

For your housing search, use three price checks: Bali’s city-centre anchor is $1,036, while the outside-centre anchor is $657 for Denpasar outskirts and for a Sanur comparison. The research does not set a fixed rent for either Denpasar outskirts or Sanur, so treat $657 as a starting point rather than a promise.

When you look at listings, ask what is included. Electricity, water, internet, cleaning, and rubbish collection can make two similar rents very different deals.

### The purchases that shape an ordinary Tuesday

Local living can keep the daily rhythm affordable. Coffee at a Bali café commonly falls around $1-$3. A local-warung lunch may run $3-$6, while casual restaurant meals often land around $5-$12 as you move toward expat-oriented menus.

Short local transport or ride-share trips can cost under $1, though Bali does not offer one standardized monthly transit pass for every area. A kilo of fruit from a market may run $1-$3, and a modest dinner can land around $6-$15 depending on the district and menu.

The pattern is simple: local food and short rides can be easy on the wallet. Rent, imported grocery habits, and frequent Western-style dining are where a modest Bali budget starts to lose its shape.

Picture a Tuesday like this: start with a $1-$3 coffee at a Denpasar café, then pick up a kilo of fruit at a neighborhood market for $1-$3. A local-warung lunch might be $3-$6, and a short ride for an errand can stay under $1.

By evening, a modest local-friendly dinner may cost $6-$15. It can be a satisfying, unhurried day—but if the higher end of each range becomes your daily routine, it will quickly compete with the $271 left after the outside-centre core budget.

## Residency, Healthcare, and the Details That Cannot Be an Afterthought

The monthly math is only one part of a retirement decision. Bali may offer retirement-oriented long-stay routes, but the available research does not provide a verified current income threshold, processing timeline, or document list. Before you plan around a $1,300 life, confirm the active Indonesian immigration category, health-insurance expectation, and renewal schedule through an official immigration channel.

For routine care, many residents look to private providers such as BIMC Hospital Nusa Dua, Siloam Hospitals Denpasar, and BIMC Medical Clinic. Allianz Indonesia is one insurer to ask about local or international coverage, but request written quotes because the research does not include a verified premium or consultation figure.

Medicare generally does not pay for routine medical care outside the United States. A Bali plan therefore needs room for private coverage, private-pay care, and the possibility of returning to the United States if treatment requires it.

### Before you commit, make the plan personal

Spend time with the details that a listing cannot answer. Ask landlords for recent utility bills. Test internet from the actual apartment, not the lobby. Find out how long it takes to reach a clinic, market, and grocery store on an ordinary day.

Most of all, separate the appealing holiday version of Bali from the version that must work when you need a prescription, renew a visa, or simply want a quiet dinner at home.
