# Croatia - Expat Life in Split’s Waterfront Old Town — A Local's Perspective

Split's Old Town, a UNESCO World Heritage site, is a labyrinth of stone streets and ancient Roman architecture, with Diocletian's Palace at its heart. The cost of living here can be surprisingly affordable, with monthly expenses for a couple averaging around $2,000. This article will help you decide if the combination of history, culture, and affordability in Split's Old Town is the right retirement destination for you.

## Quick Take

- **Best for:** American retirees who want to immerse themselves in history while enjoying a Mediterranean lifestyle.
- **Monthly reality:** Expect to budget around $2,000 for a comfortable lifestyle, including rent, utilities, and dining out.
- **Biggest advantage:** The cultural heritage and active local life.
- **Biggest warning:** The cobblestone streets can be challenging for those with mobility issues.
- **Before moving:** Verify visa, healthcare, tax, housing, and insurance rules with official sources or a qualified professional.

## Housing in Split’s Old Town

Renting an apartment in Split’s Old Town can be an affordable option, with one-bedroom apartments averaging around $600 to $800 monthly. The area is known for its historical buildings, many of which have been converted into modern apartments while retaining their original character. Streets like Marmontova and the Riva promenade offer a central location with easy access to shops, cafes, and the waterfront.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1555990950-207bbce13fea?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwxMXx8Q3JvYXRpYXxlbnwwfDB8fHwxNzgyMTE3NTgwfDA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

However, living in a historic area comes with its challenges. The buildings often lack elevators, and the narrow, cobblestone streets can be difficult to get around, particularly for those with mobility concerns. Inspect potential properties thoroughly and consider how the layout and location will impact your daily life.

## Healthcare Access in Split

Split offers several healthcare options, with the main hospital being KBC Split, located about 15 minutes from the Old Town. The hospital provides a range of services, but having private health insurance is recommended for more comprehensive coverage and quicker access to specialists. There are also numerous private clinics in the area, such as the Poliklinika Medikol, which cater to expats and offer services in English.

![photo](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1589312225511-b16301a1d309?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3w5NDQ1NTl8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyMXx8Q3JvYXRpYXxlbnwwfDB8fHwxNzgyMTE3NTgwfDA&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

Public healthcare in Croatia is generally of good quality, but wait times can be long, especially for specialist appointments. Private healthcare can mitigate this issue, but it comes at an additional cost. Research and understand the healthcare system and insurance options before making the move.

## Lifestyle and Daily Life in Split

Living in Split’s Old Town means immersing yourself in a busy local culture. The daily rhythm is relaxed, with locals enjoying coffee at cafes along the Riva or shopping at the Pazar market for fresh produce. The climate is Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters, making outdoor activities enjoyable year-round.

A common assumption is that English is widely spoken, but while many younger Croatians and those in the tourism industry do speak English, learning basic Croatian phrases can significantly enhance your experience. While Split is a relatively safe city, petty crime such as pickpocketing can occur, especially in crowded tourist areas.

## Healthcare, Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

Before moving to Split, verify healthcare access, including the need for private insurance. U.S. citizens should check the latest visa or residency requirements, as Croatia is part of the EU, which may affect long-term stay options. Additionally, understanding both U.S. and Croatian tax obligations is crucial to avoid any surprises.

Safety in Split is generally good, but be aware of your surroundings, especially in busy areas. The city's walkability is a significant advantage, but the cobblestones can pose challenges, so consider your mobility needs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to live in Split’s Old Town?

A couple can live comfortably on about $2,000 per month, including rent, utilities, and dining out. However, costs can vary based on lifestyle choices and healthcare needs.

### Is healthcare in Split reliable?

Yes, Split offers reliable healthcare services, but having private insurance is recommended for quicker access to specialists and better coverage.

### What language is spoken in Split?

Croatian is the official language, but many people in the tourism industry speak English. Learning basic Croatian can enhance your experience.

### Are there any mobility concerns in Split’s Old Town?

Yes, the cobblestone streets and lack of elevators in historical buildings can pose challenges for those with mobility issues.

### What should I know about safety in Split?

Split is generally safe, but be cautious of petty crime, such as pickpocketing, especially in crowded areas.
