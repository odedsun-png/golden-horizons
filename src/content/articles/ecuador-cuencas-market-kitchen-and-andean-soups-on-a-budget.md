## Quick Taste

In Cuenca, Ecuador, the market kitchens are a haven for budget travelers seeking hearty Andean soups that offer a comforting blend of flavors. These soups, often featuring ingredients like potatoes, corn, and local herbs, are satisfying, with the city’s markets providing a more traditional and economical experience compared to upscale restaurants. For first-time visitors, the markets are better for value and traditional flavors, while restaurants offer convenience and a wider range of dietary options.

![Food image](https://images.pexels.com/photos/37758872/pexels-photo-37758872.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fit=crop&h=627&w=1200)

A visit to Cuenca’s lively markets will introduce you to a variety of soups such as locro de papas, a creamy potato soup, and caldo de gallina, a flavorful chicken broth. These dishes are typically served hot, often accompanied by aji, a spicy sauce that adds a kick to the meal. A key variation to watch for is the addition of avocado or cheese, which can enhance the soup's flavor. Visitors should be prepared for a popular atmosphere, with vendors calling out their offerings and the aroma of fresh ingredients filling the air.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Andean soups have a deep-rooted history in Ecuadorian culture, reflecting the agricultural abundance of the region. The use of local ingredients like potatoes, corn, and quinoa dates back to pre-Columbian times, when these staples were integral to the diet of indigenous communities. Over the years, these ingredients have been combined with Spanish influences to create the hearty soups enjoyed today.

In Cuenca, the tradition of market kitchens serving these soups has been passed down through generations. These kitchens are often family-run, with recipes that have been refined over decades. The emphasis is on fresh, locally sourced ingredients, which not only supports local farmers but also ensures that each bowl is full of flavor. The communal aspect of eating at a market kitchen, surrounded by locals and travelers alike, adds to the cultural richness of the experience.

## How Locals Eat It

Locals in Cuenca enjoy their soups at all times of the day, but they are especially popular during lunch, which is considered the main meal. A typical serving includes a generous portion of soup, often accompanied by a side of rice or a small salad. The meal is usually rounded off with a piece of fresh fruit or a sweet dessert, making it both filling and nutritious.

![Local dining](https://images.pexels.com/photos/37758872/pexels-photo-37758872.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fit=crop&h=627&w=1200)

When dining at a market kitchen, it’s common to see locals adding aji to their soup for extra spice. This sauce, made from hot peppers, garlic, and herbs, is a staple on every table. Another popular addition is chifles, thinly sliced and fried plantains, which add a crunchy texture to the meal. For those looking to eat like a local, it’s worth trying the soup with all the traditional accompaniments to fully appreciate the flavors.

## Where to Find It

Cuenca’s markets are the best places to find Andean soups. The 10 de Agosto Market is a favorite among locals and tourists alike, known for its popular stalls and wide variety of food options. Here, you can find numerous vendors offering steaming bowls of soup, each with their own twist on traditional recipes.

![Street food](https://images.pexels.com/photos/38786290/pexels-photo-38786290.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fit=crop&h=627&w=1200)

Another popular spot is the Feria Libre, the largest market in Cuenca. This market offers a more extensive selection of soups and other local dishes, providing a great opportunity to sample different flavors. For those who prefer a quieter setting, the smaller El Arenal Market offers a more relaxed atmosphere while still delivering on taste and traditionality.

## City vs. Countryside Flavor

The flavors of Andean soups can vary significantly between Cuenca and the surrounding countryside. In the city, soups tend to be more refined, with chefs experimenting with different ingredients and presentation styles to cater to a diverse clientele. This results in a more polished dining experience, although it may come at a slightly higher price.

In contrast, the countryside offers a more rustic version of these soups. Here, the focus is on simplicity and tradition, with recipes that have remained unchanged for generations. The ingredients are often sourced directly from local farms, ensuring freshness and traditionality. For those seeking a more traditional taste, the countryside is the place to be, offering larger portions and heartier flavors.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

Eating at a market kitchen in Cuenca is not only a cultural experience but also a budget-friendly one. A typical bowl of soup costs between $1.50 and $3.00, making it an affordable option for travelers. Portions are usually generous, often large enough to serve as a complete meal.

When ordering, it’s helpful to know a few key phrases in Spanish, such as “una sopa, por favor” (one soup, please) and “sin aji” (without spice) if you prefer a milder flavor. It’s also common to ask for additional toppings like avocado or cheese, which may incur a small extra charge. Being polite and friendly with the vendors can enhance the experience, as they are often eager to share their food traditions with visitors.

## What to Know Before You Try It

Before diving into a bowl of Andean soup, there are a few things to keep in mind. First, be prepared for the soup to be quite filling, as it often includes hearty ingredients like potatoes and corn. This makes it an ideal choice for those with a big appetite or looking to fuel up for a day of exploring.

It’s also important to be aware of potential dietary restrictions. While most soups are made with chicken or beef broth, vegetarian options are available but may require a special request. Additionally, those with gluten sensitivities should inquire about the use of wheat-based thickeners in the soup.

## Common Mistakes Visitors Make

One common mistake visitors make is not fully embracing the local dining customs. Eating at a market kitchen is a social experience, and engaging with the vendors and other diners can enhance your understanding of the culture. Don’t be afraid to ask questions about the ingredients or preparation methods, as locals are usually happy to share their knowledge.

Another mistake is underestimating the spice level of aji. While it adds a delicious kick to the soup, it can be quite potent for those not accustomed to spicy foods. Start with a small amount and adjust to taste. Lastly, visitors often overlook the importance of trying different market kitchens to experience the full range of flavors and styles available in Cuenca.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the most popular Andean soups in Cuenca?

The most popular Andean soups in Cuenca include locro de papas, a creamy potato soup often topped with cheese and avocado, and caldo de gallina, a savory chicken broth with vegetables. Other favorites are quinoa soup, which is nutritious and filling, and sopa de mote, made with hominy corn and various meats.

### Are there vegetarian options available at market kitchens?

Yes, many market kitchens offer vegetarian options, although they may not always be prominently displayed. It’s advisable to ask the vendor for “sopa vegetariana” or inquire about soups made with vegetable broth. Some soups, like quinoa soup, are naturally vegetarian but always confirm with the vendor.

### How spicy are the soups in Cuenca?

The soups themselves are generally not spicy, but they are often served with aji, a hot sauce that diners can add to taste. The level of spice can vary, so it’s best to start with a small amount and add more if desired. If you prefer no spice, you can request your soup “sin aji.”

### What is the best time to visit Cuenca’s markets for soup?

The best time to visit Cuenca’s markets for soup is during lunchtime, typically between 12:00 PM and 2:00 PM, when the selection is freshest and most vendors are open. However, some markets offer soups throughout the day, so you can enjoy them at your convenience.
