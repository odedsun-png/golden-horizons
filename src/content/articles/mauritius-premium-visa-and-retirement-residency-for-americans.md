# Mauritius - Premium Visa and Retirement Residency Program for Americans

Mauritius offers two main pathways for Americans considering a longer stay: the Premium Visa and the Residence Permit for Retired Non-Citizens. Each option has its own conditions and benefits. Understanding these can help retirees decide which path aligns best with their lifestyle and financial situation.

## Retirement Snapshot

- **Premium Visa Validity**: Up to 1 year, renewable
- **Residence Permit Validity**: Initially 3 years, renewable
- **Financial Requirement for Residence Permit**: USD 40,000 annually
- **Path to Permanent Residence**: Available after 3 years with Residence Permit
- **Local Employment**: Not permitted under Premium Visa
- **Healthcare & Safety**: Unverified, check local resources
- **Best Fit**: Retirees with financial means and desire for long-term residence
- **Biggest Warning**: Conflicting information on financial requirements; verify with official sources

## Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

The Premium Visa allows for a stay of up to one year but does not provide residency rights or the ability to work locally. The Residence Permit for Retired Non-Citizens, however, offers a path to permanent residence after three years. There is no verified information on tax advantages or safety advisories, so retirees should consult with local experts and official sources.

## The Trade-Off

**What you gain:**
- Flexibility with the Premium Visa for short-term stays.
- Long-term residency and potential permanent residence with the Residence Permit.

**What you give up:**
- No local employment opportunities with the Premium Visa.
- Significant financial commitment required for the Residence Permit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I work locally with the Premium Visa?**  
No, the Premium Visa does not permit local employment.

**What is the financial requirement for the Residence Permit?**  
The official requirement is an annual transfer of USD 40,000 into a Mauritius bank account.

**Does the Premium Visa lead to permanent residency?**  
No, the Premium Visa does not lead to permanent residency.
