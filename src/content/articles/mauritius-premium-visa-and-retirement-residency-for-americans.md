# Mauritius - Premium Visa and Retirement Residency Program for Americans

Mauritius, a scenic island nation in the Indian Ocean, offers two main options for Americans considering a long-term stay: the Premium Visa and the Retired Non-Citizen Residence Permit. Each option has unique features and requirements. This article provides insights for those considering Mauritius as a retirement destination.

## Retirement Snapshot

- **Premium Visa**: A 1-year renewable visa for tourists, retirees, and remote workers; free of charge; no local work rights; no residency path.
- **Retired Non-Citizen Residence Permit**: For retirees aged 50+; requires foreign income transfer; no local work rights; potential path to permanent residency; duration and income requirements vary.
- **Visa-Free Entry**: U.S. tourists can stay up to 60 days without a visa.
- **Healthcare**: No verified information on healthcare quality or access.
- **Housing/Cost**: Financial requirements for the retirement permit range from USD 1,500 to 2,000 monthly.
- **Safety**: Standard entry requirements; U.S. travel advisory available.
- **Best Fit**: Retirees with foreign income seeking long-term residence.
- **Biggest Warning**: Conflicting information on permit duration and financial thresholds.

## What the Monthly Budget Actually Looks Like

For those considering the Retired Non-Citizen Residence Permit, the financial requirement involves transferring between USD 1,500 and 2,000 monthly into Mauritius. This income must come from outside the country, ensuring retirees have enough funds to support their lifestyle without local employment.

## Visa, Tax, and Safety Notes

The Premium Visa offers a flexible, renewable 1-year stay for retirees and remote workers but does not provide residency rights or allow local employment. The Retired Non-Citizen Residence Permit is designed for retirees aged 50 and above, allowing for long-term residence and potential permanent residency. However, there is conflicting information regarding the permit's duration, with official sources stating a 3-year validity, while others suggest up to 20 years.

U.S. citizens can enter Mauritius for up to 60 days without a visa, provided they meet standard entry requirements, including a valid passport and proof of onward travel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I work in Mauritius with the Premium Visa?**  
No, the Premium Visa does not allow for local employment.

**What is the duration of the Retired Non-Citizen Residence Permit?**  
Official sources state a 3-year duration, but some secondary sources suggest it may be up to 20 years. Verify with the Mauritius Immigration Office.

**Is there a path to permanent residency in Mauritius?**  
Yes, the Retired Non-Citizen Residence Permit can lead to permanent residency after a qualifying period.
