# Bali - Safest Places to Live for Expats — A Local’s Perspective

In Bali, a place where the cost of living can range from $500 to $5,000 a month, the island offers a surprising array of lifestyle options that suit different budgets and preferences. For those considering retirement in Bali, it's crucial to know that while the island is generally safe, the experience can vary significantly depending on where you choose to live. This article aims to help you decide if Bali fits your retirement plans by exploring some of the safest areas to live.

## What $2,000–$5,000/Month Buys You in Sanur

In Sanur, a popular choice for retirees, a monthly budget of $2,000 to $5,000 can cover a comfortable lifestyle. Housing is a significant part of this budget, and you can rent a two-bedroom villa with a pool for around $1,200 to $2,500 per month. Sanur is known for its laid-back atmosphere and is less hectic than other parts of Bali, making it a favorite among expats seeking a quieter life.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/33780374/pexels-photo-33780374.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Groceries and dining out are reasonably priced. A meal at a local warung (small restaurant) can cost as little as $3, while a meal at a mid-range restaurant might set you back around $10–$20. The downside, however, is that while Sanur is relatively quiet, it lacks the vibrant nightlife found in areas like Seminyak or Canggu. If you're someone who enjoys night outings, this might be a limitation.

Sanur also boasts a long beachfront promenade perfect for morning walks or cycling. The area is well-equipped with amenities, including supermarkets, international schools, and clinics. However, it's essential to remember that while Sanur is generally safe, petty theft can occur, so staying vigilant and taking standard precautions is advisable.

## Healthcare and Daily Life in Ubud

Ubud, often considered the cultural heart of Bali, is another popular area among retirees. Here, with a budget of $2,000 to $5,000, you can enjoy a more artistic and spiritual lifestyle. Renting a traditional Balinese house or a modern villa can range from $800 to $2,000 a month, depending on the location and amenities.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/35729598/pexels-photo-35729598.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

Healthcare in Ubud is improving, with clinics like UbudCare and Bumi Sehat offering decent services for minor health issues. For more serious medical needs, you may need to travel to Denpasar or even Singapore. This can be a significant consideration if you have ongoing health concerns that require specialized care.

Ubud's community is vibrant, with yoga retreats, art classes, and organic markets contributing to a healthy and engaging lifestyle. However, Ubud is not without its drawbacks. The traffic can be congested, especially during the tourist season, and the area is hilly, which could be challenging for those with mobility issues.

The cost of daily life in Ubud is relatively affordable. Local markets provide fresh produce at low prices, and dining options range from budget-friendly local eateries to upscale restaurants. Yet, the influx of tourists can sometimes make the area feel crowded, affecting the tranquility that many retirees seek.

## Canggu: Balancing Modernity and Tradition

Canggu is a fast-growing area that offers a mix of modern conveniences and traditional Balinese culture. With a budget of $2,000 to $5,000, you can rent a stylish villa for about $1,500 to $3,000 per month. Canggu is known for its trendy cafes, co-working spaces, and surf-friendly beaches, making it attractive to a younger crowd and retirees who enjoy a more active lifestyle.

However, Canggu's popularity comes with its own set of challenges. The area is developing rapidly, leading to increased traffic and noise levels. While this might be a downside for some, others appreciate the energy and opportunities for socializing and engaging in community events.

One of the appealing aspects of Canggu is its proximity to both local and international amenities. The area is home to several supermarkets, boutiques, and healthcare facilities like Siloam Hospital, which provides a range of medical services. Despite these conveniences, the rapid development can sometimes feel overwhelming, and the area is not as serene as Sanur or Ubud.

The cost of living in Canggu can vary widely depending on your lifestyle. While you can find affordable local dining options, the prices at international restaurants and cafes can add up. Additionally, while Canggu is generally safe, the vibrant nightlife scene means it's advisable to be cautious, especially at night.
