# A Personal Trainer Three Times a Week for $60? Bali’s Costs for a U.S. Retiree

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** Bali can look remarkably affordable beside many U.S. cities, especially where a one-bedroom rents for about $250-$450 a month outside the busiest expat areas. Furnished homes in pricier parts of Canggu, Seminyak, and central hubs can reach $500-$800 or more. The $60 personal-trainer idea is not supported by the current research memo, so treat it as a question to price in person, not a promise. For a retiree, the real test is rent, food, air-conditioning, private healthcare, and the income rules for a KITAS or KITAP.

A warm morning near Badung Market can make Bali’s promise easy to understand: rice and vegetables in a bag, coffee lingering on the table, and nowhere urgent to be before the tropical heat gathers. Then comes the part that matters if you are considering retirement here—the lease, the air-conditioning bill, the clinic you would actually use, and the legal path that lets you stay.

Bali can be surprisingly gentle on a housing budget. It is not, however, one-price-fits-all. Denpasar, Sanur, Ubud, Canggu, and Seminyak can feel like entirely different versions of the same island, particularly when you begin looking at 2026 costs.

## What Bali’s Everyday Costs Really Ask of You

### Rent is where the island becomes five different budgets

A one-bedroom in a less-central Bali area can run about $250-$450 monthly. In furnished, expat-focused areas, the same basic category may rise to $500-$800 or more.

**Cheapest starting point in the evidence:** Less-central Denpasar-area housing at about $250-$450 a month.  
**Most expensive starting point in the evidence:** Furnished units in pricier expat zones at about $500-$800 or more.  
**Neighborhoods to compare:** Denpasar, Sanur, Ubud, Canggu, and Seminyak.

That spread is not a small detail. It shapes whether Bali feels like an ordinary home or a long vacation with a rent bill attached. Numbeo’s June 2026 pages distinguish Denpasar city-center rents from those outside the center, while Expatistan’s September 2026 snapshot underscores why the exact street and lease terms matter.

The quieter address may leave more room for groceries, rides, and medical care. Move closer to Canggu’s café strip or Seminyak’s polished conveniences, and housing begins consuming that room quickly.

### The grocery cart tells a different story than the café menu

Badung Market offers a useful first lesson in Bali pricing. Local staples such as rice and vegetables may cost far less than the imported goods and polished café menus often found in Canggu and Seminyak.

### About that $60 personal-trainer question

The evidence in this memo does not verify a personal trainer three times a week for $60. It may be possible through a particular gym, trainer, package, or neighborhood arrangement, but it is not a 2026 price you should count on before you arrive.

### Residency belongs in the budget conversation

### Healthcare is the part to test in person

Bali’s better-known private-care options include BIMC Hospital and Siloam Hospitals Denpasar, with clinics and hospitals serving the Denpasar, Sanur, Ubud, and Seminyak areas. Major international insurers such as Allianz can be part of a private-coverage conversation, but the memo does not provide verified Bali policy premiums, co-pays, consultation fees, imaging prices, or emergency costs.

For a retiree in their 60s or 70s, this is not a footnote. Ask for written cash estimates for a general consultation at two facilities. Ask what services are available locally and how direct billing works with your insurer.

Medicare generally does not pay for routine healthcare received outside the United States. Keep that reality separate from Bali’s local prices, and keep a plan for care when you return to the United States.
