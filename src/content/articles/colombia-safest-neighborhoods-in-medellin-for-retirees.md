# Colombia - Safest Neighborhoods in Medellín for Retirees — A Local's Perspective

Medellín, once infamous for its turbulent past, now boasts one of the most impressive urban transformations globally, with its homicide rate dropping by over 95% since the 1990s. Today, the city is a vibrant hub for expatriates and retirees, drawn by its welcoming communities and cost-effective living. This article will explore some of the safest neighborhoods in Medellín, helping you decide where you might want to settle.

## What $2,000/Month Actually Buys in El Poblado

El Poblado is often the first stop for many retirees considering Medellín due to its reputation for safety and amenities. Here, $2,000 a month can provide quite a comfortable lifestyle. For instance, a two-bedroom apartment in a high-rise with stunning views of the Aburrá Valley can run you around $800 to $1,200. This includes access to building amenities like a gym, pool, and 24-hour security. 

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/34493381/pexels-photo-34493381.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

El Poblado's Parque Lleras area is known for its bustling nightlife, but a short walk away, you'll find quieter streets like Calle 10B, where the noise fades and the air feels a bit fresher. The neighborhood is home to a variety of supermarkets, such as Carulla and Exito, where you can find both local and international products. Dining out ranges from affordable local eateries to high-end restaurants, with a typical meal costing between $5 to $15.

However, El Poblado isn't without its limitations. The cost of living here is higher than in other parts of Medellín, and traffic can be a headache, especially during rush hours. The hills, while offering beautiful views, can also be a challenge for those with mobility issues. But, if you're looking for a place where you can get by comfortably on your retirement income while enjoying a certain level of luxury, El Poblado is hard to beat.

## Laureles: A Blend of Local Charm and Modern Convenience

Laureles offers a different vibe compared to El Poblado, often described as more laid-back and authentically Colombian. Here, $2,000 a month can stretch even further. You might rent a spacious three-bedroom apartment for around $600 to $800, leaving plenty for other expenses like dining, entertainment, and healthcare.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/32441071/pexels-photo-32441071.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&dpr=2&h=650&w=940)

The tree-lined streets around Avenida Jardín are perfect for morning walks, and you'll find a mix of cafes and bakeries offering fresh coffee and pastries. Local markets such as the Mercado de La América provide an opportunity to shop for fresh produce at great prices, often much lower than in supermarkets. Dining out in Laureles is a pleasure, with meals ranging from $3 at a local eatery to $20 at a more upscale restaurant.

Healthcare facilities are easily accessible, with Clínica Conquistadores offering comprehensive services. Public transport is efficient, with several Metro stations nearby, and taxis or Uber rides are affordable for getting around the city.

One thing that might surprise you about Laureles is its vibrant art scene. The neighborhood features numerous murals and public art installations, adding a splash of color and creativity to your daily walks. Laureles might not offer the same level of luxury as El Poblado, but its charm and affordability make it a compelling choice for retirees seeking a more local experience.

## Envigado: Small-Town Feel with Big-City Access

Envigado, technically a separate municipality, feels like a small town but benefits from its proximity to Medellín. Here, $2,000 a month affords a very comfortable lifestyle, often with more space than you’d find in the city center. A three-bedroom apartment with modern amenities might cost between $500 and $700, allowing for generous spending on leisure and healthcare.

The area around Parque Envigado is particularly popular, known for its community feel and safety. The streets are lined with shops, cafes, and a variety of restaurants offering everything from traditional Colombian dishes to international cuisine. One standout is the Mercado del Parque, a local market where you can find fresh produce and homemade goods.

Envigado is home to several reputable healthcare facilities, including Hospital Manuel Uribe Ángel, providing a range of services at costs lower than in the United States. Public transportation is accessible, with buses and the Metro providing easy connections to Medellín.

A surprising aspect of Envigado is its green spaces. Places like Parque Ecologico El Salado offer a retreat into nature without leaving the area. Here, you can enjoy hiking trails and picnic spots, which are perfect for spending a leisurely afternoon.

Envigado strikes a balance between the conveniences of city living and the tranquility of a smaller community. It's an ideal option for those who want to enjoy Medellín's amenities while living in a peaceful environment.
