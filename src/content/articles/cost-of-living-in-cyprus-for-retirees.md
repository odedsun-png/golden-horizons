# The Real Cost of Living in Cyprus for Retirees

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** A one-bedroom in Nicosia runs about $715 outside the center or $828 in the city center. A solo grocery basket is roughly $288-$403 monthly, basic utilities and internet can reach $268, and a monthly transit pass is about $46. A practical core monthly range is about $1,279-$1,614 before healthcare and residency costs. Limassol is usually pricier than Nicosia, with reported two-bedroom rents in Agios Athanasios and Limassol City Centre around $1,380-$2,300.

A $3.75 cappuccino, a $17 lunch, and a one-bedroom that may cost $715 outside central Nicosia: those are the numbers that make Cyprus worth a closer look. They suggest a daily life that could feel manageable for an American retiree—until the questions get bigger. Which neighborhood are you renting in? What will utilities really look like? Can you meet residency requirements? And what happens when healthcare enters the picture?

The appealing part is easy to see. The responsible part is looking past the coffee and the rent listing, toward the ordinary bills that shape a life abroad.

## What Everyday Life in Nicosia Costs Before Healthcare

Nicosia offers the clearest current price picture in the research. A one-bedroom averages about $715 outside the city center and $828 in Nicosia City Centre, based on September 2026 Numbeo entries converted using the European Central Bank reference rate. That is a meaningful difference before you have paid a utility bill, bought groceries, or chosen how often dinner out belongs in your week.

Food is not bargain-basement cheap, but the basics are easy to picture. A liter of milk is about $1.89, a loaf of bread about $1.83, and a dozen eggs about $4.90. Apples run about $1.59 per pound, bananas about $0.98 per pound, tomatoes about $1.62 per pound, and potatoes about $0.87 per pound. A sensible solo grocery allowance falls around $288-$403 monthly.

Then come the bills that do not appear in a cheerful apartment listing. For a 915-square-foot Nicosia apartment, electricity, heating, cooling, water, and garbage average about $213 monthly. Broadband adds about $34, and a mobile plan about $21; with all three, the monthly total reaches roughly $268.

Getting around without a car has a straightforward price tag: about $3 for a one-way local ticket or $46 for a monthly public-transport pass. A casual lunch is around $17. A mid-range dinner for two comes to about $69. A domestic beer is about $1.74 at the store, while a bottle of wine is about $8.05. The rent may be the headline, but these are the choices that give a month its actual shape.

## Why Limassol Changes the Housing Math

Nicosia City Centre is the steadier housing benchmark in this research at about $828 for a one-bedroom. Outside the center, the equivalent average is about $715—a difference of roughly $113 each month before food, transport, and utilities enter the picture.

Limassol is the more expensive comparison, particularly in Agios Athanasios and Limassol City Centre. Cyprus-focused guides report two-bedroom homes around $1,380-$2,300 depending on amenities and building quality. Mouttagiaka is also cited in Limassol neighborhood guidance as an area where housing demand can be high, although this research does not provide a separate rent range for it.

That distinction matters because the Nicosia grocery, transit, and restaurant figures may still help you plan, while a Limassol lease can push the entire monthly picture upward quickly. The coastal address may be the dream. The listing price is what decides whether it remains comfortable after the first month.
