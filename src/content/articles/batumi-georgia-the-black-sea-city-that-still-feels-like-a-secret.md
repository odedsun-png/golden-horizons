## Cost of Living and Budget Benchmarking

Picture a Tuesday morning in Batumi: you start your day with a $1.20 espresso at a café tucked beside the Batumi Piazza, the air salted by the Black Sea. A stroll through the local market yields fresh bread, cheese, and fruit for about $6, enough for breakfast and lunch.

Rent is the biggest variable. For $300 a month, you’ll find a furnished one-bedroom in quieter neighborhoods like Makhinjauri, while a spot in the lively, walkable Old Boulevard district might run $500–$600. Most apartments are close to the water, and many come furnished—though it’s always worth confirming what’s included.

Groceries for one person typically run $120–$180 per month, depending on how often you cook at home versus enjoying khachapuri at a local diner ($4–$7 per meal). Utilities—electricity, water, heating, and internet—add another $60–$90. A city bus ride costs just $0.25, and a monthly pass is rarely more than $15. Dining out is refreshingly affordable: a two-course dinner at a neighborhood restaurant is often $10–$12, with a local beer for $1.50.

Other everyday costs: a mobile plan with data is about $7/month, taxis across town are $2–$4, and a gym membership runs $20–$30. These numbers are drawn from Numbeo’s June 2026 update and expat guides. It's smart to check for seasonal or currency shifts, but the overall picture is clear—Batumi’s daily expenses are gentle on a fixed income.

## Residency Pathways and Healthcare Access

Georgia’s visa policy is famously open: Americans can enter visa-free for up to one year. For stays beyond that, general residence permits are available, but as of 2026, there is no official “retiree visa” with a set income requirement. Most expats pursue temporary residence permits, which may require proof of address, local bank statements, and sometimes a background check. The process can be straightforward or bureaucratic, depending on timing and documentation. Before making any commitments, consult a Batumi-based immigration attorney or check the Georgia state portal for the latest rules.

Healthcare in Batumi is accessible and generally affordable, especially through private clinics and hospitals. Two well-known options are Iashvili Batumi Central Hospital and Batumi Republican Hospital. A GP visit typically costs $15–$30, and basic lab work is $10–$20. Private health insurance is available, but coverage and English-language support vary; some expats rely on international insurers, while others pay out of pocket. Emergency care is available, but for complex procedures, many expats travel to Tbilisi or even abroad.

Language can be a hurdle in clinics and pharmacies. Younger staff may speak some English, but it’s wise to clarify costs and coverage before treatment, and to bring a local friend or translator if possible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a dedicated retirement visa for U.S. citizens in Georgia in 2026?
No, there is no single, clearly named “retiree visa” for Americans in Georgia as of 2026. U.S. citizens can stay visa-free for up to one year, and longer stays may be possible through general residence permits. Always verify the latest requirements with official sources or a local attorney. (Source: xmetr.com)

### What is the rough monthly cost of living for a solo retiree in Batumi?
A solo retiree can expect to spend $300–$600 on rent, $120–$180 on groceries, $60–$90 on utilities and internet, and under $20 on local transport. Dining out is $4–$12 per meal. These numbers are based on Numbeo’s June 2026 update and serve as planning anchors. (Source: numbeo.com)

### How accessible is healthcare for foreigners in Batumi?
Healthcare is available through private clinics and hospitals like Iashvili Batumi Central Hospital. GP visits cost $15–$30, and basic lab work is $10–$20. Some clinics accept international insurance, but language support and coverage vary—always check before treatment. (Source: nvi.ge)

### What neighborhoods are popular among expats in Batumi?
Old Boulevard is favored for its seaside location and walkability, while greener areas like Makhinjauri offer quieter living at lower rents. Expat guides highlight both for their amenities and community feel. (Source: residence-estate.ge)

### Are there everyday costs (coffee, meals, transport) documented for Batumi?
