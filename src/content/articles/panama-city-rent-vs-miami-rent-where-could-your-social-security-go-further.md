## Cost of Living and Rent Dispersion
Imagine sipping a rich, aromatic coffee at a café in Panama City's El Cangrejo neighborhood, where a cup costs just $1.50. Here, the cost of living can be significantly lower than in Miami, especially when it comes to rent. In Panama City, a 1-bedroom apartment in expat-friendly areas like Costa del Este or Punta Pacifica can range from $1,500 to $3,200 per month. In contrast, Miami's average 1-bedroom rent hovers around $2,205, with prices climbing higher in premium districts.

Groceries in Panama City can also be more affordable. A typical basket including eggs, bread, milk, chicken, and vegetables might cost around $50, while similar groceries in Miami could set you back $75. Utilities and internet in Panama City vary, but generally, they are lower than in Miami, where monthly bills can easily exceed $150.

Dining out in Panama City offers great value, with a casual meal costing about $8 compared to Miami's $15. Public transport in Panama City is also budget-friendly, with a monthly pass costing around $30, whereas Miami's transit expenses can reach $70.

**Estimated core monthly spend:** $1,800-$2,500
**Remaining buffer vs the $2,000 budget:** +$200 to -$500

**How your money compares to the US:** That $8 dinner in Panama City? A similar meal in Miami costs $15. A $50 grocery basket in Panama City might cost $75 in Miami.

**Income requirement:** $1,000/month

**A Typical Day in Panama City**
Start your day with a $1.50 coffee in El Cangrejo, then head to the local market for $50 worth of groceries. Enjoy a $8 lunch at a neighborhood café, and wind down with a $3 beer at a local bar. Your daily transport costs might be just $1 per ride.

## Practical Residency and Healthcare Guidance
Panama's Pensionado visa is a popular choice for retirees, requiring a minimum income of $1,000 per month, plus $250 for a spouse. This visa offers benefits like discounts on entertainment and healthcare, making it an attractive option for those on a fixed income.

Healthcare in Panama City is a mix of public and private services. Many expats opt for private clinics, where a routine checkup might cost $50, significantly less than in the U.S. However, it's essential to verify the quality and availability of services in your chosen neighborhood.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is the Pensionado visa income threshold, and is Social Security eligible?
Many sources cite $1,000/month lifetime income for the applicant (plus $250 for a spouse) as a threshold; verify with official Migración Panamá and current Kiplinger references. (migracion.gob.pa)

### What is typical Panama City 1BR rent in expat corridors?
Ranges commonly cited include approximately $1,500–$3,200 for 2BRs in top neighborhoods; 1BR could be lower depending on location. (insidepanama.com)

### How does Panama City healthcare cost compare to the U.S.?
Private clinics and insurance can lower out-of-pocket costs vs U.S. private care, but costs vary; verify with local clinics and insurance quotes. (kiplinger.com)

### Are there currency or exchange-rate considerations for Social Security in Panama?
Panama uses the Panamanian balboa (par with USD) and the U.S. dollar; rental and local prices are often quoted in USD; consider currency fluctuations for non-USD-based income. (expatistan.com)

### What is Miami’s current 1BR rent baseline?
1BR in Miami metro around $2,205/month (per Zillow 1BR market data); ranges exist by district. (zillow.com)
