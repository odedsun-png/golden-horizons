## Quick Taste

Papas arrugadas are small potatoes cooked with their skins on in very salty water, ideally seawater, until the skins wrinkle and carry a salt crust. They come with mojo verde or mojo rojo. Spain's official tourism site describes them this way ([spain.info](https://www.spain.info/es/gastronomia/cocina-islas-canarias/)). We did not verify a current menu price, so this guide gives none. Ask the price before you order.

![papas arrugadas, Spain](https://images.pexels.com/photos/32489640/pexels-photo-32489640.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fit=crop&h=627&w=1200)

## What Goes in the Dish

The potatoes are boiled with the skin on in very salty water, seawater if possible. The same page lists no other ingredients for the potatoes. Mojo verde is made with parsley and cilantro and tastes milder. Mojo rojo is closest to the mojo picón, made with peppers, vinegar, olive oil, garlic, cumin, salt and paprika ([spain.info](https://www.spain.info/es/gastronomia/cocina-islas-canarias/)). Recipe sites add that they are eaten as a side for grilled fish, stews or roasted meats, or on their own with mojo ([eightyflavors.com](https://eightyflavors.com/papas-arrugadas-canarian-wrinkled-potatoes/)).

## What We Could Not Verify

Origin stories vary. One recipe site says potatoes reached the islands in the 16th century and that cooks later developed the seawater method, but it gives no date or source for the technique ([eightyflavors.com](https://eightyflavors.com/papas-arrugadas-canarian-wrinkled-potatoes/)). We have not confirmed island-by-island differences, guachinche practices or market prices, so we leave them out.

## Safety and Practical Notes

The U.S. State Department rates Spain Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution, due to terrorism and unrest, with an advisory dated July 31, 2026. The advisory text we read names Ceuta for Reconsider Travel and does not name the Canary Islands ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/content/tsg_aem/us/en/home/international-travel/travel-advisories/destination.esp.html)). The dish is very salty by design. If you limit sodium, ask your doctor before making it a regular habit.

Original Medicare generally does not pay for care outside the U.S. ([aarp.org](https://www.aarp.org/medicare/does-medicare-cover-me-outside-the-us/)). If you plan to stay in the Canary Islands, ask about travel or private insurance first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are papas arrugadas made of?
Papas arrugadas are potatoes cooked with their skins on in very salty water, ideally seawater ([spain.info](https://www.spain.info/es/gastronomia/cocina-islas-canarias/)).

### What sauce comes with papas arrugadas?
They are served with mojo verde, made with parsley and cilantro, or mojo rojo ([spain.info](https://www.spain.info/es/gastronomia/cocina-islas-canarias/)).

### Are papas arrugadas safe for people watching sodium?
They are cooked in very salty water, so they are not a low-sodium food ([spain.info](https://www.spain.info/es/gastronomia/cocina-islas-canarias/)). Ask your doctor about your limit.
