## Quick Taste

Before you, a plate of papas arrugadas arrives—small, wrinkled potatoes boiled in seawater until their skins are crusted with a fine layer of salt. Each bite is a burst of earthy flavor, enhanced by a dollop of mojo sauce, either the popular green of cilantro or the fiery red of paprika. These potatoes are more than a side dish; they are a staple of Canary Island life, often costing around EUR 4-6 (about $5-7) for a generous serving. For anyone considering a longer stay in the Canary Islands, these papas offer a taste of everyday life—simple, satisfying, and deeply connected to the local landscape.

## The Story Behind the Dish

Papas arrugadas, or "wrinkled potatoes," trace their origins to the Canary Islands, where the volcanic soil and mild climate create ideal conditions for growing unique potato varieties. These islands, located off the northwest coast of Africa, have long been a crossroads of cultures, and their cuisine reflects a blend of Spanish, African, and Latin American influences. The dish emerged as a practical solution for preserving potatoes in a region where fresh water was scarce. By boiling the potatoes in seawater, locals finded that the natural salt preserved the potatoes and enhanced their flavor. Over time, papas arrugadas became a cherished part of the Canary culinary tradition, served with mojo sauce—a tangy, garlicky condiment that varies from island to island.

## How Locals Eat It

For locals, papas arrugadas are a versatile dish enjoyed at any time of day. They might be served as a tapa with a cold beer or as a side dish accompanying grilled fish or meat. The key to enjoying these potatoes like a local is to embrace the simplicity: the potatoes are best when their natural flavor shines through. Locals often dip them in mojo verde, made with cilantro, garlic, and olive oil, or mojo rojo, a spicier version with red peppers and paprika. In family gatherings, papas arrugadas are a communal dish, with everyone reaching in for a potato and a spoonful of sauce. It's not just about the food; it's about sharing a moment and enjoying the island's flavors together.

## Where to Find It

To truly experience papas arrugadas, venture beyond the tourist hotspots and explore the local markets and eateries. In Santa Cruz de Tenerife, you might find them at a lively market stall, where vendors sell them by the kilo, ready to be taken home and paired with your favorite mojo. Alternatively, head to a guachinche, a type of informal restaurant unique to the Canary Islands, often run by families and offering a taste of island cuisine. Here, you can enjoy papas arrugadas alongside other local favorites like grilled sardines or rabbit stew. While tourist areas may offer the dish, the prices can be higher and the traditionality less assured. For the best experience, follow the locals to their favorite spots.

## Regional Variations

While the basic concept of papas arrugadas remains consistent across the Canary Islands, subtle variations reflect the unique characteristics of each island. On Gran Canaria, you might encounter papas negras, a dark-skinned variety with a nutty flavor, while on Lanzarote, the potatoes might be smaller and served with a particularly spicy mojo rojo. Some cooks add a touch of lemon or orange peel to the boiling water, imparting a hint of citrus to the potatoes. The choice of mojo also varies, with some regions favoring a thicker, more garlicky sauce, while others prefer a lighter, herbaceous version. These variations offer an exploration of the islands' diverse food landscape, each bite revealing a new facet of the Canarian identity.

## Cost, Portions, and Ordering Tips

When ordering papas arrugadas, expect to pay around EUR 4-6 (about $5-7) for a portion that comfortably serves two as a side dish. In more touristy areas, prices can rise, but in local markets or guachinches, the cost remains reasonable. Portions are typically generous, reflecting the dish's communal nature. When ordering, don't hesitate to ask for both types of mojo sauce—verde and rojo—as they complement the potatoes in different ways. If you're buying potatoes to cook at home, look for the small, firm varieties sold in local markets. They are often priced by weight, and a kilo should cost around EUR 2-3 (about $2.50-3.50), making it an affordable and delicious addition to your home-cooked meals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are papas arrugadas made of?
Papas arrugadas are made from small potatoes, typically boiled in seawater until their skins wrinkle and form a salty crust. They are traditionally served with mojo sauce, which can be either green (mojo verde) or red (mojo rojo).

### Can I make papas arrugadas at home?
Yes, you can make papas arrugadas at home. While seawater is ideal, you can substitute it with heavily salted water. Boil the potatoes until tender and their skins wrinkle. Serve them with homemade mojo sauce for an traditional taste.

### Are papas arrugadas gluten-free?
Papas arrugadas themselves are gluten-free, as they consist of only potatoes and salt. However, if you're sensitive to gluten, check the ingredients of the mojo sauce, as some recipes may include breadcrumbs or other gluten-containing ingredients.

### What is the best time to eat papas arrugadas?
Papas arrugadas can be enjoyed at any time of day, but they are especially popular as a tapa or side dish during lunch or dinner. They are also a great accompaniment to a cold drink on a warm afternoon.
