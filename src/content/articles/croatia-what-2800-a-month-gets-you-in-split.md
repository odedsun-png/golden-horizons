# Croatia — What $2,800 a Month Gets You in Split

Nestled along the stunning Dalmatian Coast, Split offers a charming blend of ancient history, vibrant culture, and natural beauty, making it an increasingly popular destination for American retirees. With a budget of $2,800 a month, you can enjoy a comfortable lifestyle in this Mediterranean gem, surrounded by crystal-clear waters and historic architecture.

## Why Retire Here

Split, the second-largest city in Croatia, captivates with its blend of Roman history and modern-day allure. The centerpiece of the city is the UNESCO-listed Diocletian's Palace, a sprawling complex where ancient ruins intertwine with bustling cafes and shops. The city’s coastal location ensures that azure waters and scenic beaches are never far away, providing endless opportunities for swimming, sailing, and sunbathing. The Riva, Split's lively waterfront promenade, is perfect for leisurely strolls and people-watching while sipping a cappuccino.

The lifestyle in Split is relaxed and sociable, with a strong emphasis on community and family. Locals enjoy a slower pace of life, often gathering at open-air markets or indulging in long lunches. The city is teeming with cultural events, from music festivals to art exhibitions, offering retirees ample opportunities to engage with the local arts scene. Additionally, the Mediterranean climate ensures mild winters and warm summers, making outdoor activities enjoyable year-round.

Daily life in Split offers a delightful mix of old-world charm and modern conveniences. The city’s compact size makes it highly walkable, with most amenities and attractions within easy reach. Neighborhoods like Varoš and Bačvice provide a blend of traditional stone houses and contemporary apartments, offering diverse living options. Whether exploring the narrow streets of the Old Town or relaxing on a terrace with a view of the Adriatic, Split promises a fulfilling and picturesque retirement.

## Practical Tips

For American retirees considering a move to Split, understanding the visa and residency requirements is crucial. Croatia offers a temporary residence permit for non-EU citizens, which can be extended annually. Retirees often apply for this permit by demonstrating sufficient financial means, typically requiring proof of income or savings. It is advisable to consult with a Croatian consulate or an immigration attorney for the latest details and application procedures.

Safety in Split is generally high, with low crime rates and a welcoming atmosphere for foreigners. However, as with any city, it's wise to remain vigilant in tourist-heavy areas and keep personal belongings secure. Neighborhoods like Meje and Žnjan are particularly popular with expats, offering a quieter environment with stunning sea views and easy access to amenities. The local community is friendly, and many expats have found it easy to integrate, often participating in language exchanges and cultural clubs.

While Croatian is the official language, English is widely spoken in Split, especially in tourist areas and among the younger population. However, learning a few basic Croatian phrases can enhance your experience and help you connect more deeply with locals. Expat communities in Split are active and welcoming, providing a support network for newcomers. Joining local forums or attending meet-up events can be a great way to make friends and settle into your new home.

## Cost & Healthcare

Living on a budget of $2,800 a month in Split is quite feasible, allowing for a comfortable lifestyle without compromising on quality. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment in the city center typically ranges from $600 to $1,000 per month (verify before deciding), while properties further from the center, like in the Spinut neighborhood, offer more affordable options. Utilities, including electricity, heating, cooling, and water, generally cost between $100 and $150 monthly.

Groceries and dining out are reasonably priced, with fresh produce and local specialties readily available at markets like Pazar. A meal at an inexpensive restaurant might cost around $10, while a three-course meal for two at a mid-range restaurant can range from $40 to $60. Public transportation is efficient and affordable, with a monthly pass costing approximately $50, making it easy to navigate the city without a car.

![photo](https://images.pexels.com/photos/27651186/pexels-photo-27651186.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&fit=crop&h=627&w=1200)

Healthcare in Croatia is of high quality and accessible to foreigners. The country operates a public healthcare system, but many expats opt for private health insurance to avoid long wait times and ensure English-speaking medical staff. Private health insurance typically costs between $100 and $300 per month, depending on the coverage level. Hospitals in Split are well-equipped, and there are numerous private clinics offering specialized care. It is advisable to research and select a healthcare plan that best suits your needs before relocating.

Retiring in Split offers the perfect blend of cultural richness, scenic beauty, and modern convenience. With a budget of $2,800 a month, you can enjoy a fulfilling and comfortable lifestyle in this vibrant city, surrounded by history and the stunning Adriatic Sea.
