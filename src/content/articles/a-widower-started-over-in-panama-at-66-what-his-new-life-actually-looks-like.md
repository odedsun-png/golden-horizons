# A Widower at 66 Could Retire in Panama: Residency Options, Current Living Costs in Panama City, and Healthcare Access

> You can rent a one-bedroom in Panama City for $400-$1,400, groceries run $150-$250, utilities about $100-$350, and private insurance starts around $50/month.

## Retirement Snapshot
**Primary decision:** Can a 66-year-old retiree start anew in Panama?  
**Best fit:** Retirees with stable pension income meeting the Pensionado visa threshold.  
**Biggest warning:** Real estate and healthcare costs vary; verify current expenses.

## Residency Viability and Practical Steps for Pensionado Visa
Panama's Pensionado visa is a retiree-friendly option, requiring a verifiable lifetime income of at least $1,000 per month for a single applicant. Couples need $1,250 per month. This visa is designed to fast-track residency for retirees, with processing times varying based on documentation and application completeness.

To apply, you'll need proof of income, a valid passport, a birth certificate, and a police clearance certificate. Consulting with a visa expert can ensure all documents are in order and help you understand the timeline for processing.

## Cost of Living in Panama City for a Solo Retiree
Panama City offers a range of living costs that can suit different budgets. Rent for a one-bedroom apartment varies significantly by neighborhood, from $400 in more affordable areas to $1,400 in upscale districts like Punta Pacifica.

Groceries and basic food items are reasonably priced. Eggs, milk, and bread are staples, with local produce like bananas and onions costing around $0.40–$0.80 per piece. Utilities, including internet, range from $100 to $350 monthly, while local transportation costs between $30 and $120.

Dining out is affordable, with a casual lunch costing $5–$10 and a modest dinner $8–$15. Overall, estimated monthly expenses range from $1,000 to $2,400, depending on lifestyle choices.

## Healthcare and Safety in Panama City
Panama City is known for its accessible private healthcare, with Hospital Punta Pacífica affiliated with Johns Hopkins Medicine International. This hospital is a top choice for expats seeking quality care. Private healthcare costs are generally lower than in the U.S., though they vary by procedure and insurer.

Safety is a key consideration, and many expats find Panama City manageable with proper planning. It's important to research neighborhood safety and connect with local expat communities for insights.
