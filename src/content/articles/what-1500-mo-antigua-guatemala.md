# What $1,500 a Month Gets You in Antigua Guatemala
**Golden Horizons Status:** 🟢 SOLID OPTION

> **THE SHORT ANSWER:** A single retiree can build a modest Antigua Guatemala life on about $1,200-$1,500 a month. One-bedroom rent runs roughly $400-$900, solo groceries about $235-$341, utilities and internet about $46-$132, and casual meals about $6-$12. A central one-bedroom can bring a careful monthly plan to about $1,315-$1,520 before private healthcare and larger surprises.

A coffee in Antigua can cost $2-$3. The larger question is whether the rest of the day—your apartment, market bag, tuk-tuk ride, and dinner—can fit around it without making retirement feel like a constant exercise in restraint.

It can, but the address matters. Antigua’s cobbled Centro Histórico is the version most visitors remember. A few miles beyond it, in places such as San Pedro Las Huertas, Jocotenango, and nearby Ciudad Vieja, the monthly equation can look very different. For a retiree working with $1,500, that difference is not merely about scenery. It is the difference between a handsome rental and a little room to breathe.

**Biggest warning:**  
Housing can consume the budget quickly. Private healthcare, insurance, imported groceries, and frequent restaurant meals need their own planning cushion.

**Planning-budget lens:**

**Housing / daily life:**  
Centro Histórico generally costs more than San Pedro Las Huertas, Jocotenango, and nearby Ciudad Vieja. The trade-off is simple: central charm versus more flexibility in the monthly budget.

Private clinics are commonly used by foreign residents. Before committing, get insurance quotes for your age and ask exactly how pre-existing conditions and annual caps are handled.

**Safety:**  
Check the U.S. Department of State’s current Guatemala advisory before travel. Once there, build your routine around daylight errands, trusted transport, and the neighborhood that feels right for your everyday life.

## Where the Budget Is Won or Lost: Your Address

Antigua is easy to romanticize from a café table. The more useful question is where you will buy milk, carry groceries home, and get to an appointment on an ordinary Tuesday.

Centro Histórico offers the walkable old-town experience many people want, with low walls, café doors, and volcanic views at the end of a street. It also comes with a higher housing bill: central one-bedroom rents are commonly planned around $880-$900 a month.

In San Pedro Las Huertas, Jocotenango, and Ciudad Vieja, smaller or more peripheral rentals may fall closer to $400-$600. That is where a $1,500 plan begins to feel less precarious. The rent savings can cover a grocery cart, utility bills, local rides, and the occasional meal out without forcing every decision through a calculator.

Food is one of Antigua’s friendlier categories if you favor local staples. Milk runs roughly $1.05-$1.31 per liter, bread about $0.66-$1.05 a loaf, and a dozen eggs around $2.88-$3.93. A solo grocery plan of $235-$341 a month is realistic when produce and home cooking carry most of the week.

Utilities and internet add about $46-$132, depending on the home, provider, and season. For someone living near the center, transport can remain modest: a short tuk-tuk ride is generally $0.50-$1.50, while a light monthly allowance may be about $15-$30.

U.S. groceries often run about $300-$500 a month for one person, and a casual U.S. lunch often costs $12-$20. Private U.S. coverage for adults aged 60-64 can also run about $200-$500 monthly.

**A Typical Day in Antigua Guatemala**

Picture a Tuesday like this: coffee at a mid-priced Antigua café for $2-$3, a casual lunch for $6-$12, and a short tuk-tuk ride for $0.50-$1.50. Pick up tomatoes or peppers at a market stall for about $0.50-$1.50 per item, then make dinner at home. Save a modest dinner for two at a local restaurant—typically $20-$40—for an evening when you want to linger.

That is the rhythm a modest budget supports best: daily pleasures that stay small, with restaurant meals chosen rather than automatic. Housing may fit the plan. Healthcare is where the math deserves more attention.

## Healthcare, Residency, and Safety: The Details That Need Their Own Budget

Antigua has public and private healthcare channels, and many foreign residents look first to private clinics for appointments and testing. Ask at least two Guatemalan insurers for quotes at your actual age, especially if you are between 60 and 70. Ask directly about pre-existing conditions, annual caps, and how care in Guatemala City would be handled.

The core monthly plan leaves little room for guessing. If you choose a higher-rent apartment, healthcare coverage should not be squeezed into whatever remains after groceries and dining. Give it its own line in your budget from the start.

For residency, review current Guatemalan consular guidance for your circumstances before signing a 12-month lease. Gather current requirements, confirm which income records you will need, and keep copies of each document.

Safety planning belongs in the same practical category. During a scouting stay, walk the routes you would actually use—between your rental, grocery stops, cafés, and transport points—before deciding which area suits your daily routine.

## The Lease Questions That Matter More Than the Courtyard

A lower-priced listing in a peripheral area can turn a tight month into a workable one. But ask what comes with it. Water, internet, furnishings, and garbage collection can matter as much as the advertised rent.

Compare at least three homes: one in Centro Histórico, one in San Pedro Las Huertas or Jocotenango, and one near Ciudad Vieja. Confirm whether the lease is 12 months or month-to-month, and whether the price changes for a furnished stay.

The best apartment is not necessarily the prettiest courtyard listing. It is the one that leaves room for food, transport, medical care, and the occasional dinner shared with someone you like.

## The Trade-Off

**What you may gain:**

- A one-bedroom beyond the center may cost $400-$600 a month, leaving more room than a $880-$900 central rental.
- A solo grocery budget of $235-$341 can be lower than the typical U.S. range of $300-$500.
- Coffee for $2-$3 and casual lunches for $6-$12 make small outings easier to fit into the week.
- Short tuk-tuk rides of $0.50-$1.50 can keep daily errands simple when you do not want to walk.

**What you may give up:**

- A central apartment can bring the core plan to $1,315-$1,520 before health coverage, leaving little or no cushion.
- Imported groceries and regular meals out can quickly raise a food budget built around local produce and home cooking.
- More specialized care may mean travel to Guatemala City, along with separate insurance and transport costs.
- Living in San Pedro Las Huertas, Jocotenango, or Ciudad Vieja can mean giving up some of Centro Histórico’s immediate walkability.

The question is not whether Antigua can be affordable. It is whether your preferred version of Antigua—the apartment, the medical plan, the dining habits, the mobility—fits comfortably within your income.

## The Golden Horizons 30-Day Test

1. Spend your first week in Centro Histórico and note the walk time from your accommodation to a grocery store, café, pharmacy, and tuk-tuk stand.

2. Visit San Pedro Las Huertas, Jocotenango, and Ciudad Vieja. Collect three rental quotes that state clearly whether utilities and furnishings are included.

3. Build a seven-day food basket using milk, bread, eggs, tomatoes, peppers, and your usual breakfast staples. Multiply it by four and compare it with the $235-$341 grocery target.

4. Take five short tuk-tuk rides and compare the actual fares with the $0.50-$1.50 planning range.

5. Try two casual lunches in the $6-$12 range and one dinner for two near $20-$40. Then decide how often those meals belong in your normal month rather than your vacation month.

6. Request health-insurance quotes from two Guatemalan insurers. Before choosing a long-term neighborhood, check the current U.S. Department of State Guatemala advisory and city-area notes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a retiree live in Antigua Guatemala on $1,500 a month?

Yes, a careful single retiree can build a modest plan around $1,500 a month. The central planning budget runs about $1,315-$1,520, so the result depends heavily on rent, dining, and medical costs. Choosing a smaller home in San Pedro Las Huertas or Jocotenango may create more breathing room than a central apartment. (Living in Guatemala)

### What does a one-bedroom apartment cost in Antigua Guatemala?

A one-bedroom in central Antigua commonly runs about $880-$902 a month. More peripheral options in areas such as San Pedro Las Huertas, Jocotenango, or Ciudad Vieja may fall around $400-$600. Lease length, furnishings, and included utilities can change the final figure. (Numbeo)

### How much should one person budget for groceries?

A practical solo grocery budget is about $235-$341 monthly, depending on brands and shopping habits. Milk is roughly $1.05-$1.31 per liter, bread about $0.66-$1.05 a loaf, and eggs about $2.88-$3.93 per dozen. Local produce can help keep the total lower than an import-heavy shopping cart. (Expatistan)

### Is Antigua Guatemala safe for an American retiree?

During a 30-day visit, test your ordinary routes in Centro Histórico, San Pedro Las Huertas, and Jocotenango at different times of day.
